## 1. Findings by Rule

### S-1 — Firmament Terminology / "The membrane" alone — **C2 (style drift; intentional but unratified)**

**Rule (persona §4):** Primary term is "The Firmament." Acceptable: "The Firmament membrane" in technical contexts. **NEVER (red flag): "the membrane" alone without Firmament qualification.**

**Observed across Book 1 manuscript chapters:**

- The bare phrase `the membrane` appears **564 times across 55 files** (manuscript + specs + per-chapter reviews). Restricting to the delivered Ch##.md drafts:
  - Ch04 (*The Membrane Between Worlds*) — 26 hits
  - Ch10 (*Why Gravity Pulls and Light Shines*) — 29 hits
  - Ch07 (*Movement Pattern Interface*) — 27 hits
  - Ch06 (*More Room Than You Think*) — 21 hits
  - Ch09 (*Where Matter Comes From*) — 13 hits
  - Ch03, Ch05, Ch08, Ch11, Ch12, Ch13, Ch14, Ch15 — single-digit to low-double-digit hits
- Zero hits for `brane` (as freestanding token), `dome`, or `vault` referring to the Firmament. The volume-of-Book-0 mistake (`brane` substitutions, 1,179 hits) is fully absent here. This is a clean win.

**Reading of the deviation:**
Book 1's voice specialization (operator-voice, narrative trade-book) treats "the firmament" and "the membrane" as functional synonyms across whole sections. Ch04, which is the chapter whose explicit job is to give the firmament a body, alternates the two terms in a single paragraph — *"the firmament has a tension … the membrane has a coupling at its boundary"* — and the alternation is the voice. Forcing every "the membrane" to "the Firmament membrane" would (a) make the prose lumpier and (b) lose the rhythmic alternation that signals the chapter has moved from naming the object (firmament) to talking about its mechanics (membrane).

This is *not* the Book-0/Vol-6 problem, where "brane" substituted for "Firmament" inside technical formulae. It is a trade-book voice choice in a chapter that has already named the Firmament explicitly within the same section.

**Recommendation:**
- **Option A (faithful to franchise style sheet):** Global pass to ensure that the *first* mention per section uses "the Firmament" or "the Firmament membrane" and that "the membrane" alone appears only after that anchor, within the same section. Estimated effort: 2–3 hours across 15 chapters, mechanical.
- **Option B (ratify the deviation):** Update the style sheet to add a Book-1 carve-out: *"In trade-book voice, 'the membrane' may be used as the referent for the Firmament within a section, provided the section has already named the Firmament explicitly."* This is closer to current practice; it should be written into the style sheet so future reviewers do not flag it.

The author should pick. Either resolution is acceptable. The current state is consistent within Book 1 (no chapter is worse than another) — it is the franchise rule that needs the carve-out, or the manuscript that needs the pass.

### S-2 — Waters Pairing Discipline — **C2 (loose application)**

**Rule (persona §5):** In technical contexts, ALWAYS pair on first mention per section: `"Dark energy (Waters Above, ~68%)"` or `"Waters Above (dark energy, ~68%)"`. Capital W for primordial Waters.

**Observed:**

- "Waters Above" / "Waters Below" with capital W appears 51 times across the manuscript, concentrated in Ch12 (15 hits) and the Ch05/Ch04 specs. The pairing convention is **honored explicitly in Ch12** (*Faster Than Light, Darker Than Dark*), which is the chapter that does the dark-sector identification at full force.
- In **Ch05** (*The Hidden Energy*), where the first deep treatment of dark energy lives, "dark energy" appears multiple times before the chapter introduces "the waters above" as the framework referent. The pairing is *made* — the chapter explicitly identifies dark energy with the ground-state energy of the waters-above field — but the *form* of the pairing is narrative ("Dark energy is the reservoir … the ground-state energy of the waters-above field") rather than the parenthetical canonical form (`Dark energy (Waters Above, ~68%)`). In a trade book this is acceptable for voice reasons; against the style sheet it is a deviation.
- Lowercase "waters above" / "waters below" appears throughout in narrative prose, which is the trade-book convention. The capital-W treatment is reserved for the *named technical referent*.

**Reading:**
This is the same pattern as S-1: the trade-book voice is making a deliberate choice that the franchise style sheet does not explicitly authorize. The pairing *meaning* is honored everywhere; the pairing *typography* is honored only in Ch12.

**Recommendation:**
- Add one parenthetical pairing on first mention per chapter where dark energy or dark matter is introduced as a framework referent. Estimated effort: 6–10 small insertions across Ch05, Ch09, Ch10, Ch12, Ch14. This is a 30-minute pass and removes the deviation without harming voice.
- OR ratify the narrative-pairing form in the style sheet (parallel to the S-1 carve-out).

Either fix is acceptable. The technical content is correct; only the typography is unsettled.

### S-3 — Hebrew Transliteration — **C3 (one deviation; intentional simplification)**

**Rule (persona §3):** First mention: Hebrew letters → transliteration with diacriticals → English gloss. Final aleph as apostrophe: `raqia'` (not `raqia`).

**Observed:**

- The Hebrew script `רָקִיעַ` appears once in Ch02 at the first introduction of the word, followed by italicized transliteration `*raqia*` and the English gloss in the next sentence. The script-then-transliteration-then-gloss order is honored.
- **The final-aleph apostrophe (`raqia'`) is NOT used in any delivered Book-1 chapter.** The transliteration is consistently the simplified `raqia` throughout Ch02, Ch04, Ch08, and elsewhere. The full-book FullBook style report already flagged this and ruled it acceptable as a standalone-noun reference. I concur for trade-book voice; the apostrophe is a Foundations convention and looks academic in a trade book where the word is being used narratively.
- `*bara*`, `*asah*`, `*mayim*` are italicized on first mention and subsequently. Macrons appear where used (`*shamayim*` in Ch02 spec). No diacritical errors found.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm in the style sheet that Book 1 uses *simplified transliteration* (no apostrophe for final aleph) as a deliberate trade-book accommodation, while Foundations retains the apostrophe form. No manuscript change required if the carve-out is documented.

### S-4 — Voice Register — **C4 (PASS)**

The voice matches the Book-1 standard: confident, rigorous, honest, third-person preferred, equations present and always explained. Sampling across Ch04, Ch05, Ch07, Ch10, Ch11, and Ch15, I find a consistent operator-voice — first-person used sparingly and intentionally ("I am an engineer, not a theologian"; "I will not smuggle an answer past you here"), third-person dominant. No academic-pulpit drift. No casual-blog drift. The voice is one voice across 15 chapters.

The em-dash density (1.8–2.2 per 1,000 words, flagged in the FullBook report) is a feature of the voice, not a violation. Chicago Manual of Style limits are explicit; this prose is above trade-book norms but well below academic norms. Acceptable.

The "genuinely" word-tic (FullBook report S-1, 7 hits across the book) is a copyedit-phase fix, not a style-sheet violation. I confirm the seven instances persist and recommend the structural-alternative replacements proposed in the FullBook report.

### S-5 — Citation Format — **C4 (PASS — trade form)**

Book 1 uses parenthetical cross-references to Foundations volumes (`"(See Foundations Vol 3 Ch 7 for the full derivation.)"`) rather than author-date `(Greene 1999, p. 142)` or numbered references `[1]`. This is the *correct* form for this book per the project's positioning. The numbered-reference style applies to Foundations; the author-date style applies if Book 1 cites *external* literature; this book mostly does not cite external literature in-line, which is consistent with the operator-voice ("the framework speaks for itself").

Minor mixed usage of `Foundations Vol X` vs. `Foundations Volume X`. Already flagged in the FullBook report as cosmetic. No new finding.

No formal bibliography is required for Book 1 (this is the trade-book convention). Cross-references to Foundations are sufficient.
