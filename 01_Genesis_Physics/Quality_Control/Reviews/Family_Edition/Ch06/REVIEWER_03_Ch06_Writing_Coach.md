**No voice shifts detected.** The register remains consistently warm-teacher-who-is-also-an-operator from §1 through §10.

## OPENING HOOK — PASS

**Requirement:** Chapter opens with something that makes the reader want to continue. Even textbook chapters can have compelling openings.

**What happens in §1:**

## WEAKEST PASSAGES

These sections are solid but have minor opportunities for polish (non-blocking).

1. **§6, sentence about dimensional reasoning.** The sentence *"Engineers call this *dimensional reasoning.*"* is a label that might not land for every reader. The concept is clear (and the next sentences explain it), but the jargon could be optional. Suggestion: consider whether "dimensional reasoning" adds value or is a minor aside. **Disposition:** Optional polish. Not a blocker.

2. **§7, second objection, opening movement.** The phrase *"This is the question the skeptical uncle will ask at Thanksgiving."* is specific and relatable, but it sets up the possibility that the answer that follows will only convince some readers. The answer itself is excellent, but the setup primes skepticism. **Disposition:** This is intentional (acknowledging that not all readers will find the answer convincing), so it is appropriate. No change needed. Not a weakness; intentional honesty.

3. **§2, transition between dark matter and dark energy paragraphs.** The chapter moves from dark energy (§4) to dark matter (§5) without an explicit transition sentence like "Now the other invisible layer." The reader follows the logic, but a one-sentence bridge (e.g., "The other invisible layer — the one beneath — was found first") would smooth the move. **Disposition:** §5 opening begins *"The other invisible layer — the one beneath — was found first and has been accumulating evidence for ninety years."* This IS a transition sentence. No change needed.

**No passages require revision.** All weak points are either intentional framing or already addressed in the text.
