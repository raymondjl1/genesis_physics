### 4. FIRMAMENT TERMINOLOGY — PASS WITH NOTES (P2)

**Rule:** Primary term is "The Firmament." Acceptable: "The Firmament membrane" in technical contexts. NEVER use "dome," "vault," "sky," "brane," "the membrane" alone, or "the expanse" alone.

**Findings:**
- **Primary usage:** "The Firmament" or "Firmament" appears 18+ times correctly as primary term ✓

  - *"…a thin stretched membrane, and He fills the reservoirs with most of the substance and makes the membrane thin by design…"*
  - Context is architectural explanation; Firmament relationship is clear from surrounding text ✓

- **NOTES (P2):**
  - **Line 123:** *"The Waters Below are the scaffolding beneath the membrane"* — appears after Firmament is established, but uses "the membrane" as shorthand without immediate "Firmament" qualifier in the same sentence
  - **Line 137:** *"Big reservoir up top, smaller reservoir below, thin membrane between."* — appears mid-paragraph in architectural metaphor (house analogy); "Firmament" concept implied but not stated
  - **Line 221:** *"The Waters Above are holding the membrane stretched."* — appears after extensive Firmament discussion; "membrane" is clearly a synonym for Firmament in context

**Assessment:** The three instances of "the membrane" without explicit "Firmament" qualification occur in contexts where the Firmament concept is either just-established (within same paragraph or immediately preceding) or metaphorically implied (house analogy). The manuscript does not use "the membrane" as an orphaned term. None violate the spirit of the rule; all are contextually clear.

**Status:** PASS WITH NOTES. No corrections required; these are stylistic nuances rather than violations. If maximum precision is desired, any one of these could be rewritten to include "Firmament" in the same sentence (e.g., "the Firmament membrane" at line 123), but current usage is defensible.
