### P2 Findings (All from REVIEWER_02: "But Why?" Reader)

#### P2-C1: "Membrane" Concept Introduction Needed (§5, line 1)

**Location:** §5, opening line: *"Take the three adjectives the prophets gave us — stretched, transparent, crystalline-ordered — and lay the modern physics alongside them one at a time."*

**Issue:** The reader encounters the word "membrane" in §5 without a direct explanation of what physicists mean by this term.

**Suggested Fix:** Add one sentence in §5 before the three properties:
> "When physicists say the cosmos is a **membrane**, they mean a thin layer that can vibrate, carry waves, and be deformed by mass — like the surface of a drumhead, which is why we call it that."

**Severity:** P2. The chapter recovers through the "drumhead analogy" and sheet demo (§9), but a pausing reader at §5, line 1, would feel the gap.

**Flagged by:** REVIEWER_02

---

#### P2-C2: Why Crystalline = Lattice Mechanism Missing (§5, "Crystalline-ordered" subsection)

**Location:** §5, paragraph starting with *"Crystalline here means what the jeweler means..."*

**Issue:** The chapter defines crystalline (salt, diamond examples) but does not explain the *mechanism* by which the Firmament organizes matter into crystalline patterns.

**Suggested Fix:** Add sentence after salt-vs-dust comparison:
> "The Firmament itself — that stretched, tensioned membrane — naturally organizes matter into patterns along its surface, the way standing waves on a guitar string are organized into regular modes. Galaxies are no exception."

**Severity:** P2. The chapter compensates with visual evidence (Fig 2.5.3) and Ezekiel's witness, so the intuition lands. But the mechanism is missing for a "but why?" reader.

**Flagged by:** REVIEWER_02

---

#### P2-C3: Why One Firmament (Not Multiple) Mechanism Incomplete (§7, "One Firmament or Many")

**Location:** §7, "Third. Are there many firmaments or only one?"

**Issue:** The text asserts "no reason at all for their tensions to match to ten decimal places" but does not explain *why* independent systems should have different tensions or what mechanism would cause divergence.

**Suggested Fix:** Extend the guitar-string analogy in the existing explanation:
> "Two independent guitar strings can be tightened by two separate hands — and most of the time, if the hands have no communication, one string will be tighter than the other. The universe would need to fine-tune two independent tensions to ten decimal places for them to match. But astronomers have measured the speed of light from sources across the observable universe, and the answer comes back the same everywhere. That level of agreement is not a coincidence; it is evidence of *one* membrane, not two."

**Severity:** P2. The argument is correct and leads to the right conclusion. But the "why" is incomplete.

**Flagged by:** REVIEWER_02
