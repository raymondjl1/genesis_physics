## What I Loved

**The Barn Opening.** Jeff writes: *"It was the middle of March… and the cows were already standing at the feed bunk. All three of them. In the exact same order…"* I literally said "oh" out loud. This is not a physics lecture pretending to be a story. This is a real human moment I could see my husband and me in. The wife leaning on the rail. The wife's one line — *"That's what Malachi 3:6 is"* — that's when it clicked for me that this book understands families. I could teach from that opening without notes. I could tell my husband about it at dinner.

**Malachi 3:6 as the Load-Bearing Verse.** I have read Malachi 3:6 a hundred times in a hundred devotions, but I have never — *never* — connected it to physics. This chapter does not force that connection. It *earns* it. By the time I got to the explanation of *shanah* (the Hebrew verb meaning "to change"), I understood that God's *unchangingness* is not just theology, it is the foundation for every law written in a physics textbook. That is the kind of moment my 16-year-old needs. She asks hard questions, and usually I can only say "the Bible says," but with this chapter I can say, "the Bible says, and here is why that matters in the real world."

**Noether's Theorem Without Equations.** I braced myself for this section. I got an A- in physics in high school, and then I never thought about it again until my kids started homeschool. But this chapter explains — in pure English, with the metronome and the melody analogy — why *time staying the same* produces *energy staying the same.* Then it connects it to Hebrews 13:8: *"Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever."* My 14-year-old will eat that up. I can actually explain it to him.

**The Covenant Reading.** Jeremiah 33:25–26 as the answer to "why does God hold to the laws?" — not because He *must* (which makes Him seem constrained) and not because He's *uninvolved* (which makes Him seem distant), but because He *committed* to them by covenant. I am using this the next time a neighbor asks me how I can believe in both the Bible and science. That is the kind of answer people want.

**The Miracles Paragraph.** The cadenza analogy. A composer writing a cadenza into his own symphony is not breaking the rules; he is expressing his authorial authority. My 16-year-old has asked me point-blank whether miracles mean the Bible contradicts science, and I never had a frame good enough. Now I do. This chapter gave me an answer I can actually defend.

**"But What About?" Both Answers Land.** The two questions kids *actually ask* — are miracles law-breaking? (no, they're cadenzas) and what about heat death? (only a problem if the cosmos is closed, which Scripture says it isn't) — are both answered without hand-waving. The heat death answer actually *refers back* to Chapters 6 and 7 instead of re-deriving. That shows respect for the reader's time and the earlier chapters. I felt that respect.

**Honest About Limits.** The section on what is RIGOROUS vs. WORKING vs. OPEN is exactly what I need to say to my kids when they push back. *"Some tests pass with margins. Some tests are in progress. Some items are on the schedule. The vehicle flies."* That is the language of a parent who builds things and knows what it means to have confidence without pretending everything is perfect. It is the language my kids trust.

**The Activity is Real.** Five Laws, Five Verses. Materials: paper, pen, Bible. Time: 30 minutes. I can do this on a Tuesday afternoon. The starter matches Jeff provides (Hebrews 13:8 for energy, Psalm 139:8 for momentum, etc.) are solid, but he also says *"yours may differ, and that is fine — this is your table, not mine."* That permission matters. I can imagine my 14-year-old arguing that Romans 8:21 *should* go with something else, and Jeff's chapter gives me the right to say yes, make the case. The paper goes on the refrigerator. Three weeks later, when my youngest's science class talks about conservation of energy, she'll see the verse again and say, "oh, that's the one about Jesus being the same." The wiring sets.

**The Closing.** *"The Lord does not change. Carry Malachi 3:6 with you this week… The laws God cannot break He cannot break because He has bound Himself to them by covenant. And the covenant holds because He holds."* My hands got chills. That is not flowery; that is precise. That is a pastor's language. That is a physicist's language. They met and said hello and it is good.

### P1 (High Priority)

**FINDING 04.P1 — Noether's Theorem Explanation** ✓ PASS  
§3 breaks Noether's theorem into three steps: (1) time-translation symmetry (the laws don't change with time); (2) Hebrews 13:8 (Christ is the same across time); (3) the translation (constancy of the Sustainer produces constancy of the laws, producing energy conservation). Each step is digestible. A 14-year-old can grasp this. A parent can teach it. No equations. **TAG: C6, C2**

**FINDING 05.P1 — Miracles as Cadenzas, Not Rule-Breaking** ✓ PASS  
§7a answers the hard question: *"If the laws are so reliable, what about miracles?"* The answer is not "miracles are exceptions" (which sounds like lawlessness) but "miracles are cadenzas" (the Author writing differently in His own score). The analogy holds: a cadenza is not a mistake, not a rebellion, it is *written into the score*. Water's ordinary pattern is sustained by the Word; when the Word speaks differently, water does differently. This is theologically honest and physically coherent. **TAG: C3, C1**

**FINDING 06.P1 — Heat Death Addressed Directly** ✓ PASS  
§7b answers: "Doesn't the Second Law mean the universe dies?" The chapter does not hand-wave. It explains: (1) heat-death is a prediction *for closed systems*; (2) the cosmos is not closed (Hebrews 1:3, Colossians 1:17); (3) therefore the prediction doesn't bind; (4) Scripture's witness is that decay is not the end (Romans 8:21, Revelation 21:5). All of this is tied *back* to Chapters 6 and 7 (open-system cosmos) instead of re-derived. Respectful and complete. **TAG: C1, C3**

**FINDING 07.P1 — Activity is Doable and Meaningful** ✓ PASS  
The Five Laws, Five Verses activity requires: paper, pen, Bible, 30 minutes, and a family table. The starter matches are offered but not mandatory ("yours may differ, and that is fine — this is your table, not mine"). The paper goes on the fridge. When the kids encounter a conservation law in school, they'll see the verse again. The wiring sets. This is real pedagogy, not busywork. **TAG: C6, C5**

**FINDING 08.P1 — Discussion Questions Spark Real Conversation** ✓ PASS  
The five discussion questions (§ before the Key Terms section) layer from youngest to oldest:
- Youngest: "What is something you count on your mom or dad doing the same way every day?" (Concrete, personal, safe.)
- Elementary: "If God changed His mind about a law tomorrow, what would be different?" (Hypothetical, helps imagine the importance.)
- Middle school: "Think of two more things where something staying the same causes something else to stay the same." (Patterns, meta-thinking.)
- High school: "Why is 'higher-order sustaining act' better than 'law-breaking' for understanding miracles?" (Theology + physics integration.)
- Parents: "How does Malachi 3:6 change when you read it as both theological promise *and* physical guarantee?" (Spiritual depth.)

None of these have obvious "right answers" that make a child feel wrong for questioning. All of them spark conversation. **TAG: C6, C5**

**FINDING 09.P1 — Covenant Reading Reframes Constraint** ✓ PASS  
Jeremiah 33:25–26 (*"my covenant with day and night, the fixed order of heaven and earth"*) is the answer to a question that haunts both skeptics and careless believers: *"Why does God hold to the laws?"* Not because He must (constraint model), not because He's absent (deist model), but because He *committed* to them by covenant. The laws are a promise He is keeping, not a cage He is locked in. This is theologically sound and emotionally powerful. **TAG: C3, C4**

**FINDING 10.P1 — Honest About Open Questions** ✓ PASS  
§8 is brutally honest: Standard Model conservation laws from Zone-2 topology are "in progress," quantum gravity is "not settled in any framework in physics, not the Standard Model, not General Relativity, and not Genesis Physics." A parent reading this knows: *"I can say to my kids 'this is solid' or 'we have a working picture' or 'this is still open,' and I am telling them the truth."* **TAG: C1, C3**
