### 1. VOICE REGISTER: PASS WITH NOTES

The chapter opens with earned scene and builder's rigor, maintaining the operator-engineer voice throughout. Four minor refinements recommended.

**Strengths:**

- Opens with earned trout-tank scene (lines 10–22): specific farm detail (200-gallon tote, well water, recirculating pump), wife present but not centered, builder's focus on constraint change. Excellent.
- Engineer's posture consistent: "what water does" (lines 20, 321, 323), "constraint," "setpoint," "boundary condition" (lines 16, 20), "coherent physical mechanism" (line 261).
- Farm household detail earned throughout: barn bay, dogs investigating, gravel, shop towels, milk crates. No invented kids. ✓
- First-person sparing and natural: "I am there," "I kill the breaker," "I stand for a second and watch" (lines 14, 18–19).
- Short sentences dominant: "The tank is overflowing." "I kill the breaker." "The water is not doing anything strange." Excellent pacing.
- Confidence levels proportionate to rigor: HIGH, MODERATE, LOW labels on sections 3, 6, 7, 8 signal honesty. ✓
- Scripture density appropriate: nine direct quotations, well-integrated, not preached.

**Notes requiring attention:**

1. **Line 26 — "Sermon-like" opening sentence**
   - Current: "This chapter is for the parent whose kid came home from a state-school geology class and was told the Flood is a myth."
   - Issue: The phrase "state-school geology class" carries a faint us-vs-them tone that risks sounding defensive. The engineer's voice avoids tribal framing.
   - Proposed fix: "This chapter is for the parent whose child came home from a geology class and was told the Flood is a myth." (Drop "state-school.")
   - Rationale: Wider, more neutral audience. The geology objection is the point, not the institutional source.

2. **Line 53 — "Walk both phrases" — awkward verb**
   - Current: "Let me walk both phrases."
   - Issue: "Walk" is conversational but slightly informal for the technical sections that follow. Lines 55–62 are dense Hebrew analysis, and the lead-in should signal rigor.
   - Proposed fix: "Let me unpack both phrases." OR "Let me examine both phrases."
   - Rationale: "Unpack" or "examine" maintains the direct tone while signaling technical work ahead.

3. **Lines 69–70 — Repetition of "This matters"**
   - Current: "This matters because of the verb on the fountains. *Baqa.* ... This matters because in Chapter 8 we said the Lawgiver does not change."
   - Issue: Two "This matters" openings in quick succession (lines 69 and 69). The repetition signals importance but risks becoming a tic.
   - Proposed fix:
   ```
   This matters because of the verb on the fountains. *Baqa.* ...
   The significance deepens when we recall that in Chapter 8, we established the Lawgiver does not change.
   ```
   - Rationale: Varies the transition; "deepens" moves toward the next point without repeating "matters."

4. **Line 27–31 — "Guardrails" section — tone shift**
   - Current: "I am going to give that answer in this chapter. Two guardrails before we start, the same way I gave them last chapter. I am not going to tell you the Flood was a small local event... And I am not going to do creationism-versus-secular-geology."
   - Issue: Not a violation, but the defensive framing ("I am not going to...") appears twice in quick succession. The engineer's voice prefers to state what *is* being done, not what *isn't*.
   - Proposed enhancement (optional): Reframe as: "Two guardrails for this chapter, the same way we set them last chapter. First: [positive position]. Second: [positive position]." This inverts the negative framing to positive claim-making.
   - Status: Optional refinement. Current phrasing is acceptable but slightly defensive for the engineer's register.

**Overall:** Voice register holds strong throughout. Engineer-builder posture is consistent. Four minor refinements are tightening opportunities, not failures. The chapter reads like Jeff Raymond writing to a concerned parent, not like a defensive apologist.
