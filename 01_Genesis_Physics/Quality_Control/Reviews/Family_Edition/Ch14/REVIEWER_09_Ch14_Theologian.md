### 2. CONTEXTUAL FIDELITY — PASS

All passages are used *in context*, not proof-texted. The chapter demonstrates careful exegetical restraint:

**Deuteronomy 6:6–7** — The chapter uses the full verse including *sitting in thy house, walking by the way, lying down, rising up* to emphasize that teaching is not a *curriculum or institution* but a *lifestyle rhythm.* This is the verse's actual intention in context. The Hebrew word *shanan* is correctly extracted as central to the meaning (teaching by repetition, sharpening). The verse stands as the *instituting command* to parents — not borrowed or forced.

**Proverbs 22:6** — Used to address parental imposter syndrome. The chapter correctly reads the verse as a *long-arc promise* (*when he is old, he will not depart*), not a transactional guarantee. This honors the verse's actual contextual meaning — wisdom literature's typical form (true in general, not a contract for every child). The Hebrew *chanak* (initiate, dedicate) is correctly glossed and carries the weight the text intends.

**Psalm 78:3–7** — Used as the generational-transmission frame. The passage is quoted in full context (the full chain from *we have heard...our fathers have told us...we will not hide them from our children...that they might set their hope in God*). This is exactly the passage's structural and spiritual meaning: transmitting the *praises and strength of the LORD* across generations so that *they might set their hope in God.* No forcing.

**Romans 13:7** — Used to ground honor toward public-school teachers even when the parent disagrees with curriculum claims. The chapter correctly interprets Paul's *render therefore...honour to whom honour* as commanding respect toward those in legitimate authority (the teacher is a legitimate authority in her classroom). The context (Paul writing to Christians under hostile imperial government) makes the application even stronger: if you honor hostile magistrates, certainly honor the patient public-school teacher.

**1 Peter 3:15** — Used to frame the high-schooler's posture in a hostile secular classroom. The verse's full context — *sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer...with meekness and fear* — is honored. The chapter correctly identifies the posture as *defensiveness without arrogance* (the Greek *prautes* and *phobos* are identified as gentleness and reverent awe). The verb *ready always* (continuous state of preparedness) is correctly distinguished from *aggressive.*

**Hebrews 1:3 callback** — The closing invokes *upholding all things by the word of his power* to anchor the pastoral synthesis (God is sustaining the household conversation the same way He sustains creation). This is a callback to Chapter 13's technical treatment, and the chapter does not re-derive the physics. The application is pastoral but biblically sound: the same sustaining power that holds matter in being holds the kitchen-table conversation in being.

**Contextual integrity: PASS.** No passage is torn from its moorings.

### 3. HEBREW ACCURACY — PASS

The chapter introduces two Hebrew terms with exegetical precision:

**שָׁנַן (*shanan*)** — Deuteronomy 6:7
- **Glossed as:** "to sharpen, to whet, to teach diligently by repetition"
- **Literal sense:** whetting a blade on a stone
- **Exegetical claim:** Names the *kind* of teaching — slow, repetitive, patient
- **Verification:** Standard lexicons (BDB, HALOT, TWOT) confirm *shanan* as "to sharpen, whet, teach diligently." The literal sense (whetting) is attested. The application to teaching (sharpening the student) is established in Hebrew pedagogy. The chapter's gloss is **ACCURATE and contextually sound.**

**חָנַךְ (*chanak*)** — Proverbs 22:6 
- **Glossed as:** "to initiate, to dedicate, to start a child on a path"
- **Translation issue:** English "train up" is *chanak*, which primarily means "to dedicate" or "to initiate" (as in dedicating a building or starting a path)
- **Exegetical claim:** Names the *long-arc dedication* of the child to a way of life, not a transactional guarantee
- **Verification:** BDB and HALOT confirm *chanak* as "to dedicate, to initiate, to start (on a path)." The use in Proverbs 22:6 is indeed about setting a child on a *path* — a lifelong orientation, not a contractual outcome. The chapter's reading is **ACCURATE.**

**Consistency with REVIEWER-08 (Linguist) standards:** The transliterations use the standard vowel-pointing convention (macrons for long vowels when needed, standard Latin transliteration). No conflicts with franchise Hebrew standards.

**New technical vocabulary:** The chapter restrains itself — no new Greek terms are introduced. The Greek *prautes* and *phobos* are introduced only where needed (§7, explaining 1 Peter 3:15), and they are correctly glossed (gentleness, reverent awe). No linguistic overreach.

**Hebrew accuracy: PASS.**

### 4. THEOLOGICAL CLAIMS — PASS

The chapter makes several theological claims. All are examined for orthodox Christian defensibility:

**Claim 1:** "The parent, not the curriculum or the institution, is the primary teacher of the child" (§2).  
- **Biblical anchor:** Deuteronomy 6:6–7 (explicitly, §2); Ephesians 6:4 (referenced); Psalm 78 (generational frame).
- **Orthodoxy check:** This is *standard evangelical theology* on parental authority and responsibility. It is uncontroversial within the broader Christian tradition (Augustine, Luther, Calvin, and modern evangelical theology all affirm parental primacy in spiritual formation). The chapter does not deny the role of church, co-op, or teacher — it correctly identifies the *hierarchy.* **PASS.**

**Claim 2:** "Scripture does not require credentialing [of the teacher]; it requires faithfulness" (§3).  
- **Biblical anchor:** Proverbs 22:6 (long-arc promise, not credential-based guarantee); Deuteronomy 6 (no credentialing language).
- **Orthodoxy check:** This is biblically sound. Parental authority flows from position, not expertise. The chapter is not claiming that expertise is unimportant; it is claiming that faithfulness is the *prerequisite,* not credentials. **PASS.**

**Claim 3:** "The framework is a working hypothesis with HIGH, MODERATE, and OPEN parts, and should be taught that way" (§11).  
- **Biblical anchor:** Implicitly grounded in intellectual humility and the command to avoid false certainty (Proverbs 18:15, *the ear of the wise seeketh knowledge*; 2 Timothy 2:15, *rightly dividing the word of truth*).
- **Orthodoxy check:** This is *sound evangelical epistemology.* The franchise's "cleared-community discretion" voice pillar — the willingness to say *I don't know* — is biblically grounded in Proverbs 12:15 (the wise listen to counsel), 1 Corinthians 13:12 (we know in part), and James 3:17 (wisdom is open to persuasion). The claim is **ORTHODOX.**

**Claim 4:** "If a child later abandons the framework's specific physics but keeps the Bible-first reading, that is a victory, not a failure" (§11).  
- **Biblical anchor:** The claim prioritizes biblical authority over *our reading* of the Bible. This is grounded in sola scriptura (Scripture is the final authority) and the distinction between the *text* and the *interpretation.* No specific verse is needed; the claim is downstream from the franchise's founding posture (Bible first).
- **Orthodoxy check:** This is **SOUND AND HUMBLE.** It avoids the danger of turning the framework into a secondary authority. **PASS.**

**Claim 5:** "The least credible Christian students are honest Christian students; materialists fear honest, not confident, Christian students" (§7, paraphrased).  
- **Biblical anchor:** The claim is operationally grounded in 1 Peter 3:15 (*answer with meekness and fear,* i.e., with intellectual humility) and implicitly in Proverbs 27:12 (the prudent [wise] person thinks before speaking).
- **Orthodoxy check:** The claim is pragmatically sound and aligns with apologetic best practices across evangelical tradition (see Craig, Carson, McGrew). It is not making a *theological* claim but an *operational* claim about persuasion. The chapter correctly ties this to **cleared-community discretion,** a voice pillar. **PASS.**

**Overall theological integrity: PASS.** No heretical implications. No unorthodox claims. All theological statements are defensible within evangelical Christianity and consistent with Scripture.

### 5. CHRISTOLOGICAL THREAD — PASS

This is the most pastoral chapter in the series. The Christological thread is **structural, not decorative** — exactly what the franchise voice requires.

**Primary Christological passage: Hebrews 1:3** — "Upholding all things by the word of his power."

In §10, the chapter invokes this verse to anchor the pastoral synthesis. The kitchen-table conversation, the child's question, the tired Saturday afternoon — all are being *upheld* by the same Christ (the *Word* of Hebrews 1:3 = the *Logos* of John 1:1) who is the subject of the conversation itself. The family is teaching *about* creation while being *sustained by* the Creator.

This is a profound integration: **the act of teaching about Christ's sustaining power is itself an act of being sustained by that power.** The chapter does not preach this; it *describes* it as a sustained reality (§10, §12).

**Secondary thread: Christ as the answer to the framework** — The chapter closes with a gesture forward (§10, closing sentence): *The framework is a way of seeing the whole creation that returns the family to worship...that the One making and upholding it is the same Person who took on flesh and walked through Galilee and went to the Cross.*

This is **not explicit Christology** in this chapter (it will be explicit in Ch15, the closing theological chapter). But the frame is correct: the framework itself is *an instrument of pointing to Christ.* The teaching of the framework is *formation in the knowledge and worship of Christ.*

**Evaluation:** The Christological thread is present at the right level for this chapter. It is not crowded out by pedagogy or psychology. It is structural. The chapter trusts the reader to understand that teaching is *inherently* a Christological act (Deuteronomy 6 is a command from the LORD; Christ is the LORD; therefore teaching His Word is teaching Christ). **PASS.**
