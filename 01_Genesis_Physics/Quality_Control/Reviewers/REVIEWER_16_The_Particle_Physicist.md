### Must Check

1. **Particle mass derivations from membrane resonances:** The claim that particle masses arise from organizing vibration frequencies requires a specific mechanism. For each particle species (electron, muon, tau, up/down/strange/charm/bottom/top quarks, W, Z, Higgs, photon, gluon, neutrinos): what is the membrane resonance frequency that produces it? What is the mathematical relationship between frequency and mass? Does the resulting mass agree with the measured value? The PDG values (in MeV/c²) are the reference: electron 0.511, muon 105.7, tau 1776.9, up ~2.2, down ~4.7, strange ~96, charm ~1270, bottom ~4180, top ~172760, W 80377, Z 91188, Higgs 125250. The derivation must hit these numbers, not just produce a plausible-looking spectrum.

2. **The three coupling constants:** The Standard Model has three fundamental coupling constants at low energy: α ≈ 1/137 (electromagnetic), α_s ≈ 0.118 at M_Z (strong), and the weak mixing angle sin²θ_W ≈ 0.231. Zone architecture must derive all three from zone architecture parameters, not fit them. The fine structure constant derivation (α⁻¹ ≈ 137.15) is the framework's strongest existing result — but the 1.44 coefficient in that formula must be derived, not fitted, before it fully counts. For α_s and θ_W: what zone architecture features produce these values?

3. **Running of coupling constants:** The three coupling constants run with energy scale — this is measured experimentally and computed in the Standard Model. At the GUT scale (~10¹⁶ GeV), the three couplings converge (approximately in the Standard Model, exactly in supersymmetric extensions). Does zone architecture predict any modification to the running? Does it predict gauge coupling unification, and at what scale? This is a specific, testable prediction.

4. **The CKM matrix:** The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix describes quark flavor mixing in weak interactions. Its four parameters (three angles and one CP-violating phase) are measured. Does zone architecture predict them? If the framework claims that weak interactions are zone boundary crossings, what zone property determines the mixing angles? The measured values (Wolfenstein parameterization: λ ≈ 0.225, A ≈ 0.826, ρ̄ ≈ 0.159, η̄ ≈ 0.348) are the target.

5. **The PMNS matrix:** The Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrix describes neutrino oscillations. Neutrino masses are non-zero (established by oscillation experiments) but their absolute values are not precisely known (only mass-squared differences are measured: Δm²₂₁ ≈ 7.53 × 10⁻⁵ eV², |Δm²₃₁| ≈ 2.46 × 10⁻³ eV²). Does zone architecture predict neutrino masses? What zone mechanism generates the mass-squared splittings? Is CP violation in the lepton sector predicted?

6. **Electroweak symmetry breaking:** The Standard Model breaks SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y → U(1)_em via the Higgs mechanism at the electroweak scale ~246 GeV. This gives mass to the W and Z bosons (but not the photon) and to fermions via Yukawa couplings. Does zone architecture have an equivalent mechanism? If the Higgs is a zone architecture object (a membrane mode, a Waters condensate, something else), what is its self-interaction potential? Does zone architecture predict the Higgs mass at 125 GeV, or does it post-dict it?

7. **Quark confinement and asymptotic freedom:** QCD has two remarkable properties: quarks cannot be isolated (confinement, dominant at low energy) and the strong coupling decreases at high energy (asymptotic freedom, Nobel Prize 2004). The zone architecture describes the strong force as "boundary layer contact." This must be specific enough to: (a) explain why isolated quarks are never observed, (b) compute the string tension of the QCD flux tube (~1 GeV/fm), and (c) reproduce the logarithmic running of α_s(μ).

8. **Precision electroweak observables:** The Standard Model makes precise predictions for the oblique corrections S, T, U, which parameterize new physics contributions to electroweak precision data. Any modification to the Standard Model (such as zone architecture provides) will shift these parameters. Does zone architecture predict values of S, T, U different from zero? If so, they must be consistent with the LEP/SLC measurements at the 1σ level.

9. **Exotic particles and beyond-Standard-Model predictions:** If zone architecture is a complete theory, it may predict new particles not present in the Standard Model — new membrane modes, zone boundary states, etc. What are these particles? What are their masses, spins, charges, and decay modes? Are any of them already excluded by collider searches (LHC, Tevatron, LEP)? Novel predictions are the most interesting contribution a new theory can make — but they must be specific enough to be testable.

10. **The hierarchy problem:** The claim in Vol 2, Ch 9 is that the hierarchy problem is solved. The hierarchy problem is: why is the Higgs mass 125 GeV when quantum corrections would naturally push it to the Planck scale (~10¹⁹ GeV)? The "solution" in zone architecture must specify: what zone architecture property prevents the quantum corrections from being large? This is not merely a geometric statement — it requires identifying a symmetry or dynamical mechanism that protects the Higgs mass. "Gravity is geometry not a quantum force" is an intuition, not a solution.

### Zone Architecture vs. The Standard Model: The Right Framing

Zone architecture is not competing with the Standard Model for the title of "most predictively powerful framework" — it is offering a derivation of why the Standard Model has the structure it has. The Standard Model's particle content, gauge groups, and coupling constants are inputs to the Standard Model; they should be outputs of zone architecture. Where zone architecture derives the same values, that is a confirmation. Where zone architecture derives different values, those values must be compared against measurement. Where zone architecture predicts new physics beyond the Standard Model, those predictions are the framework's most important scientific contribution.

This means: zone architecture is not required to reproduce the Standard Model's technical machinery (Feynman diagrams, MS-bar renormalization scheme, etc.) — it is required to reproduce the Standard Model's measured predictions or explicitly improve on them. The question is always "what does this predict?" and "does that match what we measure?"
