### Must Check

1. **Derivation of the Schrödinger equation:** Is the Schrödinger equation genuinely derived from membrane dynamics, or is it reverse-engineered? A valid derivation must start from the zone Lagrangian or action, apply a well-defined quantization procedure (canonical or path integral), and arrive at the Schrödinger equation as a consequence — not a starting point. Every step must be shown. Specifically: what is the classical action being quantized? What is the quantization prescription? How does the wave function emerge from the membrane degrees of freedom?

2. **Second quantization procedure:** When the framework promotes classical fields to operators, is the canonical commutation relation [φ(x), π(y)] = iℏδ³(x−y) derived or postulated? What is the Hilbert space of the theory? Are creation and annihilation operators defined from zone architecture, or imported from standard QFT? Is normal ordering discussed?

3. **The zone Lagrangian:** Is there a single master Lagrangian for zone architecture from which all quantum fields are derived? If so, is it written explicitly? Does it have the correct field content (scalars, spinors, gauge fields)? Is it Lorentz invariant (or covariant under the zone manifold's symmetry group)? Does it reproduce the known Standard Model Lagrangian in the appropriate limit?

4. **Gauge invariance:** When electromagnetic, weak, or strong interactions are derived from zone architecture, is the gauge invariance explicit? For electromagnetism: is U(1) gauge invariance built in or derived? For weak interactions: is SU(2)_L structure derived? For QCD: is SU(3)_c structure derived? The Standard Model's gauge group SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1) must either emerge from zone architecture or the departure from it must be explicitly stated and justified.

5. **Feynman rules and perturbation theory:** When the framework claims to do perturbation theory, are the Feynman rules derivable from the zone Lagrangian? Are propagators written explicitly? Are vertex factors computed? Even a sketch of these rules would demonstrate that the theory is well-defined at the perturbative level.

6. **Renormalization:** Does the theory require renormalization? If so, is the renormalization procedure defined? What is the renormalization scheme (MS-bar, on-shell, etc.)? Does the theory have UV divergences, and are they handled by counterterms? If the framework claims to be finite (no divergences), this is an extraordinary claim requiring extraordinary proof. If it imports standard renormalization, the modifications due to zone architecture must be spelled out.

7. **The measurement problem:** Does the framework resolve the measurement problem, and if so, how exactly? This is a specific claim in the chapter list. A "resolution" must specify: (a) what constitutes a measurement in zone architecture terms, (b) what happens to the wave function during measurement, (c) why Born rule probabilities arise, and (d) why macroscopic superpositions are not observed. Anything less is a restatement of the problem, not a solution.

8. **Entanglement and nonlocality:** How does zone architecture explain quantum entanglement? Specifically: two particles in an entangled state are described by a joint wave function that cannot be factored. In zone architecture terms, what is the physical mechanism that maintains this correlation across arbitrary distances? Is Bell's theorem addressed? Does the framework make predictions about Bell inequality violations that differ from standard QM?

9. **The uncertainty principle:** Is the uncertainty principle derived from zone architecture, or stated as a postulate? A derivation from membrane geometry is claimed. What is it? Specifically: which property of the Firmament manifold produces the non-commutativity of position and momentum operators?

10. **Recovery of known quantum results:** Does the framework reproduce the hydrogen spectrum? The Casimir effect formula? The anomalous magnetic moment of the electron (g−2)? At minimum, the hydrogen energy levels En = −13.6 eV/n² must be recoverable from the zone quantization procedure, since this is the most basic test of any quantum framework.

### Zone Architecture vs. Standard Model: The Right Relationship

Zone architecture is not required to reproduce the Standard Model derivation — it is required to either (a) derive the same observational predictions from zone architecture foundations, or (b) explicitly depart from Standard Model predictions and specify why the zone architecture result is more correct. Both are valid. What is not valid is importing Standard Model results without derivation.

If zone architecture produces a different Lagrangian than the Standard Model Lagrangian, that is not automatically a failure — it is a prediction. If the zone Lagrangian produces different particle masses than the Standard Model, those masses must be compared against measured values. If zone architecture predicts no renormalization is needed, that is an extraordinary claim requiring extraordinary proof. The framework is allowed to be different. It must be honest about how different it is and whether those differences survive experimental test.

### Red Flags (automatic FAIL)

- The Schrödinger equation stated as an axiom or "assumed to hold" rather than derived from zone architecture
- A quantization procedure that imports canonical commutation relations without deriving them from zone architecture
- Standard Model Lagrangian terms used without identifying what zone architecture structure produces them
- The measurement problem declared "solved" without specifying the physical mechanism in zone architecture terms
- Feynman diagrams used without specifying what the Feynman rules are in this framework
- Renormalization handled by asserting the theory is finite without proof, or ignored entirely
- The claim that gauge invariance "corresponds to" zone symmetries without showing the mapping explicitly
- Using the word "quantization" to mean "things come in discrete units" rather than the technical procedure of promoting classical observables to operators
- Zone architecture results compared favorably against Standard Model failures (unanswered questions) rather than against Standard Model successes (measured predictions)
