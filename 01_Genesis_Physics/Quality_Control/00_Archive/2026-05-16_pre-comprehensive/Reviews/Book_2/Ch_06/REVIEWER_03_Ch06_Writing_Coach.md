## FINDINGS

### P1 Finding: §7 Cosmological-Constant Paragraph Runs Long

**Severity:** P2 (publishable, but benefits from tightening)

**Location:** §7, third sub-answer ("Third. 'Isn't the cosmological constant just a fudge factor?'")

**Quote:**
> "It *was* one when Einstein first wrote it in 1917 to make his equations yield a static universe. He later called it his greatest blunder. For seventy years the term sat unwanted in the corner of physics. Then in 1998, two independent teams of astronomers — *looking for deceleration,* not for a cosmological constant — came back with numbers that could only be explained by a cosmological constant of a very specific value. Since then, four other kinds of measurements — the cosmic microwave background, galaxy surveys, baryon acoustic oscillations, and the bending of light around large-scale structure — have confirmed the same value to within a few percent."

**Craft Issue:** The paragraph is ~220 words and contains strong material, but the *payoff line* comes too late:

> "A fudge factor makes one observation come out right. Five independent observations, five different techniques, thirty years, converging on the same number — that is what a *feature of reality* looks like."

This sentence is quotable and should anchor the section earlier. Currently, readers have to wait through the Einstein history to arrive at the core claim.

**Recommended Revision:** Reorganize the paragraph so that the payoff line arrives by sentence 4–5, then support it with the detail about four independent measurement types. Current structure: history → detail → payoff. Better structure: history → payoff → detail that *proves* the payoff.

Consider: "It *was* one when Einstein first wrote it in 1917, and he later called it his greatest blunder. But here is what made it real: in 1998, two independent teams of astronomers — looking for deceleration, not a cosmological constant — came back with numbers that could only be explained by a specific cosmological constant value. Since then, four other kinds of measurements have confirmed the same value. A fudge factor makes one observation come out right. Five independent observations, five different techniques, thirty years, converging on the same number — that is what a feature of reality looks like."

**Result:** Payoff line comes at word ~110 instead of ~210. Paragraph clarity improves without losing content.
