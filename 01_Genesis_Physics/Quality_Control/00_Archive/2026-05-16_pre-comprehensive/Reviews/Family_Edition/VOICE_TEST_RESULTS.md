## Household Constraint Findings — CRITICAL

The project memory constraint is explicit: **Jeff and his wife have NO KIDS (wanted but unable)**. The household is: wife, 2 dogs, 3 barn cats, chickens, 2 horses, 1 mule, 3 angus cows.

### P1 VIOLATION: Chapter 12

**Finding:** Chapter 12 invents children for the author that do not exist. This violates the household memory and is a PRIMARY voice violation per project guidelines.

**Location 1 — Line 273, family-activity preamble:**  
> "What this chapter wants for *your* family is simpler. Not that you go use Romans 1:20 as a rhetorical bludgeon at the next dinner. The point is that *your* family — **your kids, your spouse**..."

**Assessment:** This passage addresses the *reader's* family, not the author's. **ACCEPTABLE.** The generic address to families reading the book is within scope (Family Edition is written *to parents*). The word "your" establishes reader perspective, not Jeff's household.

**Location 2 — Line 381 (Critical Issue):**  
> "What this chapter wants for *your* family is simpler. Not that you go use Romans 1:20 as a rhetorical bludgeon at the next dinner. The point is that *your* family — your kids, your spouse — when they look up tonight, would *hear* what the heavens are declaring."

**Same as Location 1 — no violation here.**

**Location 3 — Line 262, Family Discussion Questions, Item 7 (Parents column):**  
> "*(Parents.)* The chapter says *you do not have to be a physicist to teach your children honestly about creation.* What is the part of the framework you feel least confident teaching? Where is the *imposter syndrome* loudest? What would it look like to say *I don't know — let's find out together* in the next conversation about that topic?"

**Assessment:** This is addressing the reader (the parent teaching), not the author. This is ACCEPTABLE framing.

**Re-audit of Ch12 for invented-character instances:**

Searched entire Ch12 for "Sarah," "Daniel," "children," "kids," "my son," "my daughter," etc. in author's voice (not reader address).

**Result:** The review comments (Ch12_REVIEW_03_WritingCoach.md) explicitly confirm: "Zero mentioned. None invented. The activity does not assume children. Consistent with 'wanted but unable' memo. ✓"

**UPDATED ASSESSMENT: NO VIOLATION IN Ch12.** The memory flagged issue appears to have been from an earlier draft. The current manuscript passes the household constraint test.

## Cross-Chapter Patterns

### What Works Across the Book

1. **Opening scenes are universally strong.** Every chapter opens with a specific moment (kitchen table, porch, yard, shop, trampoline) before moving to explanation. This is the "open chapters with scenes" rule executed flawlessly.

2. **Wife as conversational partner.** The wife appears in 11 of 15 chapters (Ch01, 02, 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, and by reference in Ch04). Her questions drive the inquiry forward. This humanizes the work and ensures the voice stays relational.

3. **Household animals.** Cows (Ch08), dogs (Ch09), mule (Ch07, mentioned), barn cats—these are *earned* references, not signaling. They arise naturally from homestead life, not inserted for flavor.

4. **Two-column charts.** Every chapter that needs to compare Scripture to physics uses side-by-side visual language: "Scripture says… Physics discovered…" This is the MBSE principle made visual.

5. **"But What About" sections.** Every chapter anticipates objections. Not dismissively—with care. Ch01 takes on the "ancient mythology" objection. Ch02 on "solid dome." Ch10 on "13.8 billion years." This is the cleared-community discretion pillar in action.

6. **Family Activity escalation.** Activities graduate in sophistication: Ch01 (translation comparison, no equipment), Ch03 (prism, sunny afternoon), Ch05 (bedsheet stretched by 4 people), Ch07 (Chladni plate or speaker). The progression respects different household capabilities.

7. **Scripture memory verses.** Each chapter ends with a single verse, woven into the chapter's logic. Not pasted on. Grown from the argument.

### What Could Strengthen

1. **Variability in opening scene types.** By Ch15, the "sitting with the Bible on the porch" opening has been used many times. Minor variation (a drive to town, a conversation overheard, a moment with animals) could add texture without changing voice.

2. **Dad perspective dominance.** While the wife has strong roles, the narrator's perspective is almost entirely Jeff's. An occasional moment from the wife's direct voice (not questions to Jeff) could deepen the warmth—though this is a minor note. The current balance is appropriate for the author voice.

3. **Cross-chapter callbacks.** The manuscript does not refer back to earlier chapters much. Occasional lines like "Remember the Chladni plate from Ch07?" would build coherence.
