### 3. HEBREW ACCURACY: **PASS WITH NOTES**

**Term-by-term verification:**

| Hebrew Term | Transliteration | Meaning Claimed | ESV Usage | Assessment |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|------------|
| *qavah* | kah-VAH | "Gather a diffuse substance into an ordered configuration" | Genesis 1:9 *gathered* | Correct |
| *yabashah* | yah-bah-SHAH | "Dry land" | Genesis 1:9, 1:10 | Correct |
| *dabar* | dah-VAR | "Word, command, ordering utterance" | Psalm 33:6 *word* | Correct |
| *synistēmi* (Greek) | soo-NIS-tay-mee | "Stand together, hold coherence" | Colossians 1:17 *hold together* | Correct |

**Etymological claims tested:**

1. **Qavah** — The author claims it means "gather a diffuse substance into an ordered configuration" with the sense of *calling together* or *collecting*. This is accurate. The verb does NOT mean "create" or "make from nothing." It is used in the Hebrew Bible for:
   - Water gathering into pools (Exodus 15:8, *qavah mayim*)
   - Assembling troops or people to a rally point
   - The semantic core is **collection of dispersed elements into ordered form**
   - The author correctly preserves this distinction from *bara* (create from nothing)
   - **CORRECT**

2. **Yabashah** — Correctly glossed as "dry land." The noun is formed from the root *yabesh* (dry, desiccate). It describes the *emergent stable state* after the field gathers. Not a new creation. An organization of existing material. **CORRECT**

3. **Dabar** — Correctly identified as "word, spoken command, ordering utterance." The Hebrew noun *dabar* carries both the sense of a *thing that is spoken* and *the authority by which the thing is done*. It is not mere sound; it is performative utterance. When God says *dabar*, the thing is named *and done.* The author's rendering as "authoritative issuance" and connection to the concept of *fiat* is **THEOLOGICALLY SOUND** and **EXEGETICALLY ACCURATE**.

4. **Synistēmi (Greek)** — Correctly identified as *syn-* (together) + *histēmi* (to stand). The compound means "stand together, hold coherence, maintain configuration." The present-tense force is correctly emphasized: Colossians 1:17 uses the *articular present participle*, indicating continuous action in present time. **CORRECT**

**Transliteration quality:**
- All transliterations follow standard scholarly conventions (ESV Study Bible alignment)
- Vowel marks are present and correct
- One note: *qavah* in the text reads "kah-VAH" — the long *a* vowel mark could be more explicit in a teaching context (perhaps *qā-VAH* with the macron for clarity), but this is a minor teaching-register refinement, not an error
- **ACCEPTABLE**

**Grade: PASS WITH NOTES**

### NOTE 1: Transliteration Mark (Minor Teaching Clarity)

**Location:** §2, first mention of *qavah*

**Current text:**
> *Qavah is a specific verb. It is not the Hebrew word for make, not the word for create, not the word for build or form. It is a gathering verb...*

**Suggestion:** In the transliteration line, add a macron over the first *a* for clarity in a teaching context:

*qāvah* (with macron) rather than *qavah* (ambiguous to non-specialists)

This makes it clear the *a* is long, not short, which affects pronunciation and is part of standard scholarly convention. Not a theological issue; purely a teaching-register refinement.

### NOTE 2: Divine Attribute Mapping (Citation Recommendation)

**Location:** Implicit throughout, especially §6

**Current text:** The chapter maps sustaining language to standing-wave dynamics and claims this reflects God's nature as immutable and faithful.

**Observation:** While this is sound theology, it would strengthen the teaching to note that evangelical commentators (Waltke on Genesis, Piper on divine glory) treat God's sustaining as a central attribute. A single citation (e.g., ESV Study Bible note on Colossians 1:17) would anchor this as consensus within evangelical tradition, not just a project framework.

**Suggestion:** Add one sentence in §6 or §10 such as:

> Evangelical scholarship has long understood Colossians 1:17 as teaching that matter is not self-sustaining but requires continuous divine upholding — a theme central to reformed theology and consistent with modern physics' discovery that the universe is an open system.

This is optional refinement, not required. The chapter already passes exegetically.

### NOTE 3: Eschatological Grounding (Open Question)

**Location:** §5 and throughout

**Current text:** The chapter describes matter as standing waves in a sustained field. The question (not asked or answered here): Does this sustaining continue into the new creation? Is the standing-wave model eternal, or age-specific?

**Observation:** This is a sophisticated question that belongs to a *different* chapter (eschatology, renewal, Revelation 21-22). The Creator's Blueprint is well-scoped to avoid this. However, if a reader asks, "Will atoms still be standing waves in heaven?" the answer is currently not in the book.

**Suggestion:** This is not a revision item for Ch. 7. Rather, note it as a topic for the eschatology chapter(s) later in the series. Flag for the series-level theological review.
