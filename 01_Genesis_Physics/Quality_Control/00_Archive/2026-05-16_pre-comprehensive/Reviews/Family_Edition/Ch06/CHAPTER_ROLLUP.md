### P1 — Major (Substance)

**[P1] [C2: Orphan Claims & "But Why?" Coverage] Timing: When does the decimal-place explanation arrive? (Flagged by: REVIEWER_02.)**

**Location:** §2–§6 (decimal-place reasoning)

**Finding:** The chapter introduces precise percentages (68%, 27%, 5%) in §2 as raw numbers. Readers hold these specific decimals through §3, §4, §5 without understanding *why these numbers specifically* rather than some other split. The answer (the house metaphor: big attic, small basement, thin floor) arrives in §6, a full section later.

**The Issue:** A faster reader will ask "but why *those* decimals?" as early as §2, but the geometric justification is delayed four pages. While the chapter *does* answer it eventually, there is a moment of "why should I trust these numbers?" that could be closed earlier.

**Suggested Fix:** Either (a) introduce the house metaphor in §2 immediately after the 68/27/5 numbers are stated, or (b) add one anticipatory sentence in §2 like: *"Those percentages are not arbitrary; they fall out of the cosmos's basic architecture, which we'll see in a moment."* This closes the "but why these numbers?" question before it hardens into doubt.

**Note on Defense:** The honest-about-limits paragraph in §6 does acknowledge that the *exact* decimal derivation lives in Foundations and "some of those answers are still being refined," so readers are told the detailed math is elsewhere. The *geometric reason* (big/medium/small) is right here and could be introduced sooner without cost.

**Verdict:** Substance issue, not a fail. Addressable in a revision pass. **No change required for publication; recommend for revision.**

### P2 — Polish (Clarity and Flow)

**[P2.1] [C2: Orphan Claims] Potential circularity in §7 "Why Haven't Scientists Found This?" (Flagged by: REVIEWER_02.)**

**Location:** §7, first objection

**Finding:** The paragraph answers "why haven't scientists found dark matter/energy?" with "because they were hunting particles, not fields." This is good. But there is a moment of potential circularity: a reader might think: "Wait — how do *you* know it's a field and not a particle? You just said the Bible names it; you didn't prove it's a field."

**The Text:** "They have been hunting a particle… with nothing to show… The strategy has been to find the dark sector in the particle zoo of the Standard Model. It is not there. It is two fields, not two particles — an architecture, not a bestiary."

**The "But Why?" Moment:** "But how do you know those are fields and not particles?" The text asserts the distinction but doesn't explain why the evidence (no particle detections in 40 years despite intensive searching) points to fields.

**Note:** This is *not* a blocker because (a) the chapter is not trying to *prove* fields vs. particles—it's narrating measured facts and offering an explanation; (b) the reader who asks this deeper question is directed to Book 1 Ch 5 and Foundations Vol 5 Ch 11, which presumably answer it. The Family Edition is scripture-first, not physics-first.

**Suggested Clarification:** Add one sentence early in the objection: *"The observed evidence—that dark matter does not show up in any particle detector despite forty years of searching—tells us we're looking for a field, not a particle."*

**Verdict:** Optional polish. The current text is not wrong; a one-sentence clarification would strengthen it. **No change required; optional enhancement.**

**[P2.2] [C2: Orphan Claims] "Fudge Factor" section could clarify "fudge" definition (Flagged by: REVIEWER_02.)**

**Location:** §7, third objection ("Isn't the cosmological constant just a fudge factor?")

**Finding:** The section effectively argues "No, it's been measured five ways and they agree." But the word "fudge factor" itself deserves a one-sentence definition. A family reader might not know what "fudge factor" means—they might think it's literal or might not understand why five measurements disqualify something from being a fudge.

**Suggested Clarification:** Add early in the objection: *"A fudge factor is a number you add to an equation to make it fit your data when you don't understand why. It only makes one observation come out right. Once you check it against other measurements and it keeps showing up, it's no longer a fudge—it's a measured feature of reality."*

**Verdict:** Optional polish. Family readers without physics training would benefit from the term being unpacked. **No change required; optional enhancement.**

**[P2.3] [C1: Voice/Tone] "Why It Matters to the Family" (§8) could sharpen the rhetorical move (Flagged by: REVIEWER_02.)**

**Location:** §8

**Finding:** This section is warm and family-focused, which is right. But the core move—"your son will come home rattled because he heard the 95% number"—might feel constructed to some families. Not all ten-year-olds will encounter this exact rhetorical attack.

**Current Text:** "One afternoon your son will come home rattled. Somebody will have told him — on a video, in a classroom — that scientists don't even know what 95 percent of the universe is made of, and therefore all those old religious ideas are probably wrong too."

**Alternative Framing:** "At some point, your son or daughter will hear the 95 percent number used as if it's evidence against faith — as if the fact that scientists don't yet know what most of the universe is made of means we should doubt the Bible's reliability. Here's what to tell them."

**Note:** This is very minor. The current text is specific and will resonate with many families. This is optional polish.

**Verdict:** Very minor style issue. **No change required; current text is fine.**

### P3 — Minor (Style and Phrasing)

**[P3.1] [C1: Voice/Tone] Tone in §4: "Einstein…Later Called It" — casual phrasing (Flagged by: REVIEWER_02.)**

**Location:** §4, re: cosmological constant

**Current:** "Einstein had guessed at something like it in 1917, called it the cosmological constant, and later called it the biggest blunder of his career."

**Observation:** The phrasing "guessed at something like it" is slightly casual for the tone of the chapter. A reader might hear "Einstein just guessed" rather than "Einstein proposed an idea that turned out to be partly right."

**Suggested Revision:** "Einstein proposed…" instead of "guessed at" would tighten tone.

**Verdict:** Stylistic only. Overall tone of §4 is strong. **No change required; optional polish.**
