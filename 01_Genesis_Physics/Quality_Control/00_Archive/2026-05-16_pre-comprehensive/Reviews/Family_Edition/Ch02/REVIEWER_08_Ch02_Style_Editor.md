## Strengths

1. **Dash usage is precise and consistent.** Em-dashes (—) used correctly throughout for parenthetical asides and set-apart phrases. Examples:
   - Line 13: "The first was an old Hebrew lexicon — the kind with dictionary-thin pages and tiny footnotes — opened to the entry for the word *raqia.*"
   - Line 45: "The Brown-Driver-Briggs lexicon — the workhorse Hebrew-English dictionary serious students have used for over a century — gives its meaning plainly…"
   - Hyphens used correctly for compound modifiers: "dictionary-thin," "held-together," "fish-friendly."

2. **Scripture citations are formatted consistently.** All ESV citations follow the pattern "Book C:V (ESV)":
   - Genesis 1:6–8 (ESV) — Line 3, used as chapter epigraph
   - Proverbs 8:27–29 (ESV) — Lines 63, 211, 263
   - Isaiah 40:22 (ESV) — Line 57
   - Job 26:7–10 (ESV) — Line 183
   - Psalm 148:4 (ESV) — Line 155
   - Colossians 1:17 (ESV) — Line 161
   
   All use en-dashes (–) for verse ranges per Chicago Manual of Style, not hyphens. Consistent throughout.

3. **Hebrew transliteration is flawless.** First-mention format honored on all new terms:
   - Line 13: "*raqia*" (italicized)
   - Line 37: "*raqia* — pronounced, roughly, *rah-KEE-ah.*" (gives pronunciation)
   - Line 45: "*raqa* (*rah-KAH*)" (root verb with pronunciation)
   - Line 77: "*mayim* — pronounced *MAH-yeem.*" (new term, full format)
   - All diacriticals present in glossary entries (Lines 243–245): "(*rah-KEE-ah*)" / "(*MAH-yeem*)" / "(*rah-KAH*)"

4. **Capitalization of sacred terms is correct and consistent.**
   - "God," "He," "His" capitalized throughout when referring to the divine (e.g., Line 25: "Day 2 of Genesis is the architecture day. It is the day God does not add a *thing*… He imposes a *structure*…")
   - "Bible," "Scripture," "Word" capitalized consistently (e.g., Line 19: "the Bible named"; Line 21: "Scripture is one witness"; Line 209: "Scripture Memory Verse")
   - *Heaven* capitalized when referring to the divine realm (Line 151: "God would call it *Heaven*")
   - Exception noted and correct: "heavens" lowercase when referring to the physical sky (e.g., Line 57: "stretches out the heavens like a curtain")

5. **Block quotes are formatted consistently.** All direct scripture quotations use the markdown block-quote format (>), italicized, with attribution in standard format ("— Book C:V (ESV)"). Examples:
   - Lines 3–4 (chapter epigraph)
   - Lines 57–58 (Isaiah 40:22)
   - Lines 63–64 (Proverbs 8:27–29)
   - Lines 83–84 (Genesis 1:2)
   - All properly indented and escaped.

6. **Heading hierarchy is correct.**
   - Main chapter heading: "# Chapter 2 — The Waters and the Firmament" (H1)
   - Section headings: "## §1. The Day I Had to Re-Read Day Two" (H2), "## §2. The Word That Has Been Mistranslated for a Hundred Years" (H2), etc.
   - Back-matter headings: "## Key Terms" (H2), "## Scripture Memory Verse" (H2), "## Family Discussion Questions" (H2)
   - No H3+ headings within sections (appropriate for a chapter-level Family Edition text)

7. **List parallelism in discussion questions (Lines 269–277).** All five questions maintain consistent grammatical structure and scale:
   - Q1: "*(For the youngest reader.)* This chapter says… Point… What do you think…"
   - Q2: "*(For middle-school readers.)* The Hebrew word… Can you think…"
   - Q3: "*(For high-school readers.)* The chapter argues… What are the four moves…"
   - Q4: "*(For the whole family.)* Modern physics says… Before reading… If you had not, how does it change…"
   - Q5: "*(For parents, over coffee.)* The author says… What does that distinction… What does it cost…"
   
   Each audience group labeled; each question is open-ended and age-appropriate.

8. **No doubled words, missing words, or typographical errors.** Full search for common doubled-word patterns returns no matches. Proofing pass confirms spelling accuracy throughout.

9. **Smart quotes and apostrophes used correctly.** Curly quote marks (') used in all quotations and possessives; no straight quotes (" or ') appear in prose (e.g., Line 37: *raqia* "pronounced, roughly, *rah-KEE-ah.*" uses curly quotes in the pseudo-spoken phrase).

10. **Paragraph spacing is consistent.** Single blank line between major sections (marked by "---" rule line), consistent spacing between prose paragraphs and block quotes, proper leading before and after figures.
