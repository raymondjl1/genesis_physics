### P2 (Important — Should Resolve, Non-Blocking)

#### [P2] [C1] Wave speed claim — "Why" could be strengthened
**Quote:** *"The speed of light through our universe is the wave speed on the Firmament."*

**Finding:** This is a key claim, stated rather than derived. For Family Edition, acceptable because the reader was already shown that light is the oscillation mode of the Firmament (§3), and the chapter directs families to Foundations Vol 1 Ch 5 for formal derivation. However, the "why" could be one sentence stronger.

**Recommendation:** Optional polish. Consider adding before or after the current sentence: *"The speed of light, like any wave speed, is determined by the medium's tension and its effective mass — the membrane's properties working together."* This adds one line of causal reasoning.

**Impact:** Minor; does not affect verdict.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C1] "Why" delayed in §5 theological claim
**Quote:** *"Day 1 is not God creating something foreign to Himself. Day 1 is God extending a capacity that He Himself, in His own nature, already is."*

**Finding:** The heaviest theological claim is introduced *after* four supporting scripture quotations rather than *before*. This is actually strong pedagogy (let scripture speak, then explain), but a first-time reader might experience a brief moment of "why am I reading these verses without context?"

**Recommendation:** Optional. Current structure is defensible and may be intentional. If a parent finds opening of §5 disorienting, consider moving the topic sentence *"The Bible does not only say that God made light. In several of its most load-bearing moments, it says that God is light"* to immediately precede the quotations.

**Impact:** Stylistic choice, not logical error. Current structure is acceptable.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C2] Weak "why" in §2 poetic language
**Quote:** *"The Bible is telling the order in a way you would not have thought to tell it if you were making this up. If you were making this up, you would put the sun on Day 1."*

**Finding:** Strong intuitive claim but relies on reader agreement that "common sense" dictates sun-first. Some readers might ask: "Why would I put the sun on Day 1? Why not darkness, then light, then sources?"

**Recommendation:** Optional polish. Strengthen with one sentence: *"If you were making this up — if you were writing a creation account from human intuition — you would put the sun on Day 1, because that is the only source of light humans ever experienced before electric lights. Genesis does something different."* Makes the intuitive claim explicit rather than assumed.

**Impact:** Minor; does not affect verdict.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C3] Minor visual clarity in §3 CMB explanation
**Quote:** *"In 1964, two engineers at Bell Labs in New Jersey, Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson, were chasing a persistent hiss on a sensitive microwave antenna."*

**Finding:** Sentence is clear, but reader might wonder: "Why does a hiss matter? Why were they chasing it?" The paragraph explains in next sentence, but "why" could be signaled earlier.

**Recommendation:** Optional. Consider: *"In 1964, two engineers at Bell Labs were chasing a persistent hiss — an unexplained signal — on a sensitive microwave antenna."* Signals that an unexplained signal is worth chasing before explaining what they found.

**Impact:** Minor; does not affect verdict.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C4] Clarity in §6 Maxwell's equations
**Quote:** *"The mathematical backbone of electromagnetism — the equations that describe how electric and magnetic fields behave, how they propagate, how they interact — is written down in terms of the field itself, independent of any particular emitter."*

**Finding:** Accurate and well-phrased, but reader might ask: "Why would physicists write equations without a source? Doesn't that seem artificial?" Paragraph answers in next sentence, but "why" could be stronger.

**Recommendation:** Optional. Consider adding: *"…is written down in terms of the field itself, independent of any particular emitter — because you want to understand the field's fundamental laws before asking what happens when you put things into it."* Explains scientific reasoning behind the choice.

**Impact:** Minor; does not affect verdict.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C5] Transition language in §4
**Quote:** *"Ask a physicist what lives on that kind of membrane, and the first thing he will tell you is: it can carry waves."*

**Finding:** Conversational and vivid. However, cascade from "drumhead carries waves" to "light is EM field" is compressed. Family reader might find jump fast.

**Recommendation:** Optional. Consider clarifying sentence after "wave speed" discussion: *"These ripples moving across the Firmament at its own wave speed are what we call light — the oscillation mode of the stretched expanse itself."* Gives reader moment to absorb identification before moving to Fig 2.3.3.

**Impact:** Pacing preference, not logic error.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*

---

#### [P2] [C6] "God is Light" language precision
**Quote:** *"Day 1 is not God creating something foreign to Himself. Day 1 is God extending a capacity that He Himself, in His own nature, already is."*

**Finding:** Theologically careful. Some readers might ask: "How do we know God is light before creation exists? Isn't that circular?" Paragraph handles carefully, so not orphan. But "why" could be one sentence clearer.

**Recommendation:** Optional. Consider adding: *"The Bible does not say light is a property God created. It says light originates in His own being — and then creation reveals that light, making it knowable to us."* Clarifies direction of causation (God → light → knowability).

**Impact:** Minor; does not affect verdict.

*(Flagged by: REVIEWER-02.)*
