### Finding REVIEWER-10-Ch01-09

- **Severity:** P2 Important
- **Concern tags:** C4 (Self-consistency)
- **Location:** Sections 1.2 and 1.6, lines 209–213 (fine-tuning constancy bounds)
- **Quote:** "These experimental bounds on the constancy of fundamental constants over cosmic time require active maintenance. ... The constancy of α over 10¹⁰ years is consistent with Axiom 3's predictions."
- **What's wrong:** The chapter claims both (a) fine-tuning is a *symptom* of Axiom 1 (sustaining field), and (b) the *constancy* of fundamental constants is evidence for Axiom 1. These are not contradictory, but they are framed differently in different places. §1.2 emphasizes fine-tuning as evidence for design. §1.8 emphasizes constancy as evidence for maintenance. A reader could reasonably ask: "Are the constants fine-tuned (meaning they could be different) or are they constant (meaning they don't change)?" - **Why it matters:** Cascading chapters that use this logic will need to keep both claims in mind. If they slip into saying "the constants are exactly what they must be" (necessity) vs. "the constants are maintained at their observed values" (contingent maintenance), the logic will bifurcate.
- **Suggested fix:** Clarify in §1.2: "The fine constants are fine-tuned in the sense that small variations would prevent structure formation. They are also constant (not drifting) over cosmic time, suggesting active maintenance rather than pure accident. Axiom 1 explains both: the sustaining field couples to matter in a way that maintains the observed values, which happen to be life-permitting."
