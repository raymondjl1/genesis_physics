## Section 2.1: What "Differs" Means — A Taxonomy of Disagreements (~800 words)

**Topic sentence:** Not all disagreements between frameworks are created equal, and a reader must know which kind of difference they're looking at before evaluating the catalog.

**"Why" entry point:** Chapter 1 demonstrated zone architecture reproduces known physics. The natural question is: where does it make *different* predictions? But "different" can mean several things, and confusing them leads to bad science.

**Key content:**
- Type A differences: ZA and SM predict different numerical values for the same observable. A single measurement can resolve these. (Example: particle masses — ZA's membrane model gives values ~1000× off from observation.)
- Type B differences: ZA and SM make different *commitments* about quantities the SM doesn't predict or leaves open. No single measurement resolves these — they require experimental programs. (Example: neutrino mass hierarchy — SM allows both, ZA allows only one.)
- Type C differences: ZA predicts effects the SM says are exactly zero, but at magnitudes far below current detection. These are real predictions but may not be testable for decades. (Example: GW dispersion.)
- The role of experimental precision: a "difference" that falls within the error bars of current measurements isn't scientifically useful *yet*, but may become decisive as precision improves.
- How this chapter is organized: by domain, leading with the honest failure (particle masses), then progressing to the most testable near-term differences.

**Exit condition:** Reader can classify any prediction into Type A/B/C and knows the scientific implications of each type.

## Section 2.2: Particle Masses — The Honest Failure (~2,000 words)

**Topic sentence:** The particle mass spectrum is where zone architecture fails most dramatically — and this section explains exactly how it fails, why it fails, and what it means.

**"Why" entry point:** A framework that hides its worst results doesn't deserve trust. We present the mass spectrum failure first because it is the most important thing a skeptic should know.

**Key content:**
- The membrane resonance model (Vol 4, Ch 10): particles as quantized vibrational modes
- P-052: Electron mass — predicted ~500 MeV from hard-wall boundary, observed 0.511 MeV. Error: ~1000×.
- P-053: Muon/electron mass ratio — predicted 2–10, observed 206.77. Error: 20–100×.
- P-054: Quark mass pattern — quantum number assignment fails; masses (2, 5, 95, 1275, 4180, 173000 MeV) don't fit simple membrane modes.
- P-055: Neutrino mass scale — framework gives no mechanism for sub-eV masses; critical gap.
- Root cause analysis: geometric boundaries (ξ_A, η_B) don't directly set particle mass scales. Masses are set by Higgs-Waters coupling dynamics, not geometry alone.
- What works despite the failure: three generations predicted correctly; fine structure constant from same framework; gauge group structure intact.
- Parameter counting: ~5-10 adjustable parameters for ~20 masses = mediocre predictive power (vs. SM's ~30 parameters).
- Honest framing: this is incomplete physics, not contradicted physics. The framework isn't falsified by this — it's unfinished.

**Exit condition:** Reader understands the mass spectrum is a genuine, serious problem; knows its root cause; and can distinguish "incomplete" from "wrong."

## Section 2.3: Coupling Constants — Close But Not Exact (~1,200 words)

**Topic sentence:** Zone architecture derives coupling constants from geometry with small but nonzero offsets — and those offsets are themselves predictions about the framework's refinement needs.

**Key content:**
- P-056: Fine structure constant — α⁻¹ = 137.17 ± 0.15 vs. experiment 137.036. The 0.10% offset traced to b_eff coefficient uncertainty. Distinguishing test: refine b_hi coefficient.
- P-057: Weak mixing angle — sin²θ_W derived from zone geometry vs. fitted in SM. Current precision: measured to 0.0005. Status: zone value within 1% of experiment but not yet precision-derived.
- P-058: Strong coupling running — QCD from zone boundary effects predicts α_s(M_Z) = 0.118, matching PDG. But the running with energy scale may differ at extreme energies (near zone boundaries). Distinguishing test: precision α_s measurements at future colliders.
- The common pattern: zone architecture gets close (sub-percent) but carries theoretical uncertainty from higher-loop and reduction coefficients. These are Type A differences — refinable, not fundamental.

## Section 2.4: Dark Matter — A Categorical Commitment (~1,800 words)

**Topic sentence:** Zone architecture makes perhaps its boldest testable prediction: dark matter has exactly zero non-gravitational interactions — a commitment that every dark matter detector in the world is working to test.

**Key content:**
- P-061: Dark matter interaction cross-section — ZA predicts σ_DM-SM = 0 (exactly). SM candidates predict: WIMPs σ ~ 10⁻⁴⁶ cm² (spin-independent), axions g_aγγ ~ 10⁻¹² GeV⁻¹.
- Why zero: Waters Below confined to bulk (η dimension); Firmament at boundary (η₀); only gravitational coupling crosses bulk-brane gap. All gauge interactions are strictly 4D.
- P-062: DM decay lifetime = ∞. Waters Below is a geometric field, not a particle — no decay channel exists. SM extensions allow finite DM lifetime.
- Current experimental status: XENON1T/XENONnT (σ < 4.1 × 10⁻⁴⁷), LZ (approaching 10⁻⁴⁷), PandaX-4T. No detection. Consistent with ZA.
- Next-generation: LZ (2024-25), XLZD (10⁻⁴⁸), Darwin/ARGO (10⁻⁴⁹, >2030).
- Indirect detection: Fermi-LAT, IceCube — no annihilation signal. Consistent with ZA.
- The logic: ZA is "confirmed" by continued null results but can be falsified by a single confirmed detection. This is a Type B difference — different *kind* of dark matter, not different parameters.
- The neutrino floor: eventually detectors hit the "neutrino fog" at ~10⁻⁴⁹ cm², where coherent neutrino scattering becomes background. Below this, distinguishing DM detection from neutrino background becomes extremely difficult. ZA predicts the signal is below even this floor.

**Exit condition:** Reader understands the categorical nature of this prediction and the experimental program that could resolve it.

## Section 2.5: Dark Energy — Geometric Certainty vs. Dynamical Freedom (~1,400 words)

**Topic sentence:** Standard cosmology treats the dark energy equation of state as a measurable parameter that might evolve; zone architecture says it is geometrically fixed at w = -1, forever.

**Key content:**
- P-063: w = -1.000 ± 0.001 (ZA) vs. w₀ = -1.03 ± 0.03 (current measurement) vs. dynamical DE models (w₀ = -0.95 to -1.05, with evolution).
- Why geometric: Waters Above confined to ξ ∈ [ξ₀, ξ_A]. Confinement enforces w = -1. Field configuration time-independent (set at Genesis).
- P-064: No w(z) evolution — dw/dz = 0 exactly. ZA commitment: w(z) = -1 for all redshifts.
- Current tests: Planck 2018 + BAO: w = -1.028 ± 0.032. DES Y3: w = -0.98⁺⁰·⁰⁶₋₀.₀₅. eBOSS: w = -1.00 ± 0.04. All consistent with w = -1.
- Near-term decisive tests: DESI (±0.02 precision, 2026-27), Euclid + Roman (±0.01, 2028), CMB-S4 + LSST cross-correlations (±0.01, 2030s), GW standard sirens (±0.005, next decade).
- The critical distinction: ZA predicts w = -1 even if dark energy is "dynamical" in some sense — because the dynamics are geometric, not field-theoretic. ΛCDM also predicts w = -1, but allows w ≠ -1 as an alternative. ZA does not.
- Falsification: if w deviates from -1 by more than 0.05 at >3σ, ZA is falsified.

**Exit condition:** Reader knows when and how this prediction will be tested.

## Section 2.6: Fine Structure Constant — Fixed or Free? (~1,000 words)

**Topic sentence:** Some extensions of the Standard Model allow α to vary over cosmic time; zone architecture says it cannot — and the experimental precision to distinguish these claims is approaching.

**Key content:**
- P-065: Δα/α = 0 ± 10⁻⁹ per billion years (ZA) vs. possible Δα/α ~ 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁵ per Gyr (coupled-dilaton models).
- Why fixed: ξ_A and η_B are boundary conditions, not dynamical variables. The ratio ξ_A/η_B is structurally invariant.
- Current constraints: ESPRESSO + VLT: |Δα/α| < 1.6 × 10⁻⁶. Atomic clocks: Δα/α = (1.6 ± 2.3) × 10⁻¹⁷/yr.
- Future tests: ALMA (10⁻⁸), optical lattice clocks (10⁻¹⁸), ELT + JWST (10⁻⁷ at high z).
- Note: ΛCDM with w = -1 also predicts Δα/α = 0. ZA differs from *dynamical dark energy models*, not from ΛCDM itself. This distinction must be stated clearly.
- Type B difference: ZA says α *cannot* vary; SM says it *happens not to* (but could in extensions).

**Exit condition:** Reader understands the prediction, its current consistency with data, and what would violate it.

## Section 2.7: Neutrino Mass Hierarchy — A Binary Test (~1,000 words)

**Topic sentence:** Zone architecture predicts that neutrinos must follow normal mass ordering — m₁ < m₂ < m₃ — and that inverted hierarchy is topologically forbidden.

**Key content:**
- P-066: Normal hierarchy mandatory. Eigenvalue spectrum from boundary modes: λ₁ : λ₂ : λ₃ = 1 : 1.4 : 2.8.
- Mass splitting agreement: Δm²₂₁ = 7.5 × 10⁻⁵ eV² (predicted) vs. 7.53 ± 0.18 eV² (measured). Δm²₃₂ = 2.5 × 10⁻³ eV² vs. 2.51 ± 0.05 eV².
- SM status: global fit favors NH at ~1.5σ (NuFIT v5.2). Both hierarchies allowed.
- Decisive experiments: JUNO (~3σ by 2027), DUNE (>5σ by 2035), combined >5σ by ~2028-2030.
- This is the cleanest binary test in the catalog: if IH is confirmed at >3σ, ZA is falsified. Period.
- Type B difference: SM allows both; ZA forbids one.

**Exit condition:** Reader understands this is a clean, near-term test with binary outcome.

## Section 2.8: Gravitational Wave Dispersion — The Long Game (~1,200 words)

**Topic sentence:** The 6D framework predicts gravitational waves propagate with a tiny frequency-dependent speed correction that general relativity forbids — but the effect is so small it may take decades to test.

**Key content:**
- P-059: GW dispersion δ(f) ~ 10⁻³⁵ (ZA) vs. δ(f) = 0 exactly (GR).
- Mechanism: KK mode tower creates effective refractive index. Warp factor modulation. Zone boundary scattering.
- Functional form: v_GW(f) = c[1 + β ln(f/f₀) + γ(f/f₀)⁻² + ...] with β ~ 10⁻³⁵ to 10⁻³⁰.
- P-060: KK mode structure — higher-dimensional graviton excitations that could produce secondary GW signatures.
- Current constraints: GW170817: |v_GW - c|/c < 3 × 10⁻¹⁵. ZA prediction 20 orders of magnitude below this.
- Future: LIGO+/Voyager/ET (10⁻¹⁸), LISA + ground network (10⁻²⁸ to 10⁻³⁰), PTA + LISA (nHz to mHz coverage).
- Type C difference: real prediction, but beyond current and near-future technology.
- Honest assessment: this prediction may not be testable until LISA era (2030s-2040s), and even then only marginally.

**Exit condition:** Reader understands this is a genuine prediction, not hand-waving, but acknowledges the enormous experimental gap.

## Section 2.9: Proton Stability — Topology vs. Grand Unification (~1,000 words)

**Topic sentence:** Zone architecture says the proton cannot decay because baryons are topological defects — a claim that directly contradicts Grand Unified Theories predicting proton lifetimes of 10³⁴ to 10³⁶ years.

**Key content:**
- P-067: τ_p = ∞ (ZA, topological protection) vs. τ_p ~ 10³⁴⁻³⁶ years (various GUTs).
- Why absolutely stable: baryons = quantized winding numbers in ξ-η topology. Unwinding requires violating 6D topology, which has no mechanism.
- Note: SM without GUT also predicts proton stability. ZA aligns with SM here but for a different reason (topology vs. ad hoc conservation).
- Current limits: Super-K: τ_p > 2.4 × 10³⁴ years. Zero events in 27 years.
- Future: Hyper-K (2027+, reaching 10³⁵), DUNE, megaton detectors (2030s+, reaching 10³⁶⁻³⁷).
- Confidence: SPECULATIVE (40%). Topological protection hypothesis not yet proven rigorously in the framework.
- Type B/C hybrid: different theoretical commitment, and the experimental program is very long.
