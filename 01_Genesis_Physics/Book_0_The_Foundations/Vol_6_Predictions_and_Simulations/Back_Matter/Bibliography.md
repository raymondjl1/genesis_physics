# Comprehensive Bibliography — The Foundations Series

*Master reference work for **The Exodus Protocol / Genesis Physics — Book 0: The Foundations**, spanning all six volumes. This bibliography consolidates, deduplicates, and canonicalizes every citation across Volumes 1 through 6. It is the single source of truth for sources used in the Foundations Series.*

---

## How To Read This Bibliography

**Format.** Chicago author–date with sequential numbering within each section. Each entry is identified by a section code (Bib.N) and an entry number (Bib.N.M).

**Volume tags.** Each entry is suffixed with a bracketed list of volumes that cite it: `[V1, V4, V5]`, `[V6]`, etc. A single entry may carry up to six volume tags. Where a source originates inside the project itself, it is additionally tagged `[Internal]`.

**Symbol legend** (carried over from Volumes 3–5):

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 📜 | Foundational paper — historically pivotal primary literature |
| 📘 | Textbook or monograph — graduate-level reference |
| ⚛ | Experimental result or measurement campaign |
| ⚙ | Data compilation, table, or numerical reference |
| ☷ | Zone-architecture internal source — research file inside this project |

**Section index.**

| Section | Topic | Approx. entries |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| Bib.1 | Foundational physics texts (classical & modern) | ~40 |
| Bib.2 | Modern theoretical physics — QM, QFT, GR, cosmology | ~60 |
| Bib.3 | Experimental physics and precision measurement | ~60 |
| Bib.4 | Theoretical programs compared (string, LQG, causal sets, constructor, holography) | ~30 |
| Bib.5 | Scripture, biblical scholarship, and theological sources | ~25 |
| Bib.6 | Philosophy of science and research methodology | ~25 |
| Bib.7 | Technology, engineering, and applied physics | ~40 |
| Bib.8 | Zone-architecture internal sources | ~60 |
| Bib.9 | Online resources, databases, and simulation repositories | ~25 |
| Bib.10 | Reverse index — concept → entries | n/a |

**Citation completeness invariant.** Every citation used anywhere in Volumes 1–6 appears in exactly one entry here. Every entry here is cited by at least one volume. Where a source has been split across multiple historical entries in earlier volume bibliographies, those entries are merged.

**Editorial note on scope.** The Foundations Series is, by design, a bottom-up reconstruction: it begins with a specific textual claim (the first page of Genesis) and attempts to recover the standard inventory of twentieth- and twenty-first-century physics. A bibliography for a project of that scope must satisfy two audiences at once — the physicist who will scan for the canonical references on QED precision, the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon, the LIGO events, the Planck cosmological parameters, the BCS theory of superconductivity, the Bell-inequality experiments — and the reader who will want to see, in the same volume, the commentaries on Genesis 1, the Hebrew lexica, and the contemporary analytic-philosophical debate about falsifiability. The organization of this bibliography reflects that dual obligation. The first four sections (Bib.1 through Bib.4) catalogue the physics literature in conventional order, moving from classical foundations through quantum field theory and general relativity and then into the contemporary theoretical programs that zone-architecture is compared against in Chapter 11. Section Bib.5 collects the scriptural and theological sources, which in this series are primary rather than decorative: the structural conjecture driving the research program is textual, and the texts cited are the ones load-bearing on the conjecture. Section Bib.6 collects the philosophy of science and methodology — Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos, Dawid — that frames Volume 6's stance toward the falsifiability of the program. Section Bib.7 collects applied-physics and engineering references that underwrite the T-001 through T-018 technology roadmap, including the Alcubierre, Morris–Thorne, and Casimir literature on which the exotic-spacetime chapters are built. Section Bib.8 catalogues the internal research corpus — the zone-architecture mathematical derivations, simulation code, peer-review documents, and test-suite results — that live inside the project repository and are cited in the text as `[Internal]`. Section Bib.9 lists living online resources, including the project's GitHub repository and reproducibility container. The reverse index in Section Bib.10 gives a topic-first lookup for common concepts.

**What is not here.** Popular-science writing is not cited, even where it engages with the same questions the Foundations Series addresses; the intended reader of Volume 6 is assumed to have direct access to the primary literature. Blog posts and informal online discussions are not cited. News articles about physics results are not cited; the corresponding peer-reviewed papers are. Textbooks that duplicate the content of others listed here — for instance, many competing undergraduate mechanics textbooks — are not separately catalogued unless they were materially used by a specific volume. Where an idea belongs to a tradition rather than to a single canonical citation (for instance, "the idea that the laws of physics might emerge from thermodynamic principles"), a representative entry is given and the reader is directed to its own reference list for further branches of the tree.
