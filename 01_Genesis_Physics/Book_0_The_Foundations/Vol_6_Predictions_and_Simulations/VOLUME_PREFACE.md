# Volume 6: Predictions and Simulations — Preface

This volume is divided into three sections with distinct epistemic status:

**Part A — Core Validation (Chapters 1–8):** Predictions derived directly from zone architecture axioms, comparison to experimental data, numerical simulations, and the reproducibility package. These chapters represent the framework's primary scientific accountability. Claims in Part A rest on completed derivations from Vols 1–5.

**Part B — Conditional Engineering (Chapters 9–13):** Speculative applications that are physically self-consistent IF the unverified axioms of zone architecture are correct AND IF foundational open problems (OP-1: spin-1/2 derivation; OP-2: mass spectrum) are resolved. FTL travel, energy harvesting, advanced sensors, and the consciousness interface belong here. These chapters are not tested physics — they are engineering extrapolations from an unverified framework. Readers should treat them as theoretically motivated speculation, not engineering blueprints.

**Part C — Self-Assessment (Chapters 14–17):** Open problems, connections to other research programs, technology roadmap, and the research agenda. These chapters are the series' most honest and are recommended reading for anyone evaluating the framework's scientific status.

The distinction between Parts A, B, and C is essential. Part A's results are as strong as any in theoretical physics. Part B's results would be revolutionary if the foundational framework is correct — but that is a very large "if."

A word on posture. Part C's open-problems catalogue (Chapter 14) is written against four stances that have sunk more than one open-problems program. We reject **minimization** — listing real gaps as if they were minor or almost-solved (the framework's outstanding fermion-mass discrepancy is a structural puzzle, not a rounding error, and is labeled accordingly). We reject **maximization** — inflating every loose thread into an existential threat until no competent contributor wants to invest, which confuses a choice of scope with a gap. We reject **deflection** — neutralizing one's own problems by noting that competing programs share them; a shared problem is still open. And we reject **apology** — opening each problem with a paragraph of self-flagellation rather than the matter-of-fact register of a research plan. Each open problem is stated cleanly, with enough specificity to be thesis-buildable, and then the volume moves on.
