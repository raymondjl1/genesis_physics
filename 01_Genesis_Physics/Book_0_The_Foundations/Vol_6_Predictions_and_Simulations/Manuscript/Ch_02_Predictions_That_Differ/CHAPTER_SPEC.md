## "Why" Chain

1. **Why present differences at all — isn't matching observation enough?** — Because a framework that merely reproduces known physics is unfalsifiable. The differences are where zone architecture makes commitments that can be tested independently of standard physics.
2. **Why distinguish Type A (different values) from Type B (different commitments)?** — Because the scientific status differs: a Type A difference can be resolved by a single measurement, while a Type B difference requires an entire experimental program. The reader must know which kind of test they're looking at.
3. **Why lead with the honest failures (particle masses)?** — Because credibility demands putting the worst results up front. The 1000× particle mass error is the framework's biggest quantitative failure; hiding it would destroy trust.
4. **Why include predictions where current experiments can't distinguish?** — Because science plans ahead. A prediction that can't be tested today but can be tested in 2035 is still scientifically valuable — it tells experimentalists where to look.
5. **Why does this chapter matter for the research program?** — Because every differing prediction is a potential thesis topic. Each one defines a concrete experimental goal that could confirm or falsify the framework.

## Section Outline

### Section 2.1: What "Differs" Means — A Taxonomy of Disagreements
- **Topic sentence:** Not all disagreements are created equal — this section defines what kinds of differences zone architecture has with standard physics.
- **"Why" entry point:** Ch 1 showed matches; now we need to define precisely what a "difference" means scientifically.
- **Key content:** Type A vs. Type B; current vs. future testability; the role of experimental precision
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the two types of differences and can classify each prediction that follows.

### Section 2.2: Particle Masses — The Honest Failure
- **Topic sentence:** The particle mass spectrum is where zone architecture fails most dramatically — and we need to understand exactly how and why.
- **"Why" entry point:** The most important thing a framework can do is tell you where it breaks.
- **Key content:** P-052 through P-055; the 1000× error; root cause analysis; what it means for the framework
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the mass spectrum failure is real, its causes are identified, and it doesn't invalidate the framework's successes elsewhere.

### Section 2.3: Coupling Constants — Close But Not Exact
- **Topic sentence:** Zone architecture derives coupling constants from geometry, getting close but not perfectly matching — and the small residual differences are themselves predictions.
- **Key content:** P-056 through P-058; the 0.10% α offset; strong coupling running differences
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the coupling constant predictions and their refinement status.

### Section 2.4: Dark Matter — A Categorical Commitment
- **Topic sentence:** Zone architecture makes perhaps its boldest prediction: dark matter has exactly zero non-gravitational interactions.
- **Key content:** P-061, P-062; WIMP vs. Waters Below; detection landscape; why null results are evidence
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands that every null detection result supports ZA, and a single confirmed detection falsifies it.

### Section 2.5: Dark Energy — Geometric Certainty vs. Dynamical Freedom
- **Topic sentence:** Standard physics allows dark energy to evolve; zone architecture says it cannot.
- **Key content:** P-063, P-064; w = -1 exactly; DESI/Euclid/Roman sensitivity projections
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the DESI/Euclid tests and their timeline.

### Section 2.6: Fine Structure Constant — Fixed or Free?
- **Topic sentence:** Zone architecture predicts α cannot vary over cosmic time — ever — while some SM extensions allow it.
- **Key content:** P-065; current constraints; atomic clock sensitivity; ESPRESSO results
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the constancy prediction and what would violate it.

### Section 2.7: Neutrino Mass Hierarchy — A Binary Test
- **Topic sentence:** Zone architecture predicts normal hierarchy as the only possibility; the Standard Model allows both.
- **Key content:** P-066; JUNO/DUNE sensitivity; current global fit status
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands this is a clean binary test with resolution expected ~2028–2030.

### Section 2.8: Gravitational Wave Dispersion — The Long Game
- **Topic sentence:** The 6D framework predicts a tiny frequency dependence in gravitational wave speed that GR forbids.
- **Key content:** P-059, P-060; magnitude estimates; LISA + PTA sensitivity projections
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands this is a real prediction but far beyond current sensitivity.

### Section 2.9: Proton Stability — Topology vs. Grand Unification
- **Topic sentence:** Zone architecture says the proton cannot decay — ever — because baryons are topological defects.
- **Key content:** P-067; Super-K/Hyper-K limits; GUT predictions; the long experimental timeline
- **Exit condition:** Reader understands the topological stability argument and experimental prospects.

### Section 2.10: The Honest Scorecard — What a Skeptic Should Think
- **Topic sentence:** A fair summary of where zone architecture stands against the Standard Model, accounting for all differences.
- **Key content:** Summary comparison table; classification by testability timeline; the framework's risk profile; honest assessment of the particle mass problem
- **Exit condition:** Reader has a complete, honest picture and knows exactly which experiments to watch.
