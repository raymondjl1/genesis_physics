## §14.1 — The Hierarchy Problem, Briefly (1.5 pages)

**Beats:**

1. State the hierarchy problem in standard form: why is v_B = 246 GeV so much smaller than M_Planck ~ 10^19 GeV? In the Standard Model, scalar masses are not protected by symmetry and should run to the cutoff; the fact that m_H = 125 GeV is fine-tuned at 1 part in 10^34.
2. In Genesis Physics the hierarchy is not a tuning problem; it is a geometry problem, and Vol 2 Ch 9 already derived the answer. The membrane's two extents — ξ_A ~ 3×10^26 m (Waters Above) and |η_B| ~ 1.3×10^{-15} m (Waters Below) — give two natural length scales separated by forty-one orders of magnitude. The electroweak scale rides on the η-direction; the Planck scale rides on the full 6D curvature; their ratio is fixed by boundary conditions, not fine-tuning.
3. The key equation, inherited from Vol 2 Ch 9, written as (4.14.1):

    v_B / M_Planck ~ (|η_B| / ℓ_Planck)^α

    where α is an O(1) exponent determined by the membrane Lagrangian. The ratio is small because |η_B| is large compared to ℓ_Planck, not because anything is tuned.
4. State what Vol 2 Ch 9 already accomplished and what remains. Vol 2 Ch 9 explained why no fine-tuning is needed. It did not explain why |η_B| has the value it does — that is the moduli-stabilization problem, which is listed in §14.6 as MEDIUM priority.
5. Close with a pointer: "This section recapitulates; it does not re-derive. The reader who wants the full derivation should return to Vol 2 Ch 9 §9.4."

## §14.2 — Dark Matter from the Zone Architecture (4 pages)

**Beats:**

1. Opening: dark matter is the clearest experimental fact the Standard Model cannot accommodate. 26% of the universe's energy budget is not made of anything in the PDG's particle table. Any candidate framework has to say something about where dark matter lives.
2. The zone architecture offers four structurally distinct dark-matter candidate classes, each corresponding to a sector of the 6D manifold that does not directly couple to the electroweak vertices:

   **Class A — Heavy KK excitations in the η direction.** The η-mode tower has n_η = 1 at mass ~ 0.95 GeV (from the membrane scale Q_m), n_η = 2 at ~ 1.9 GeV, n_η = 3 at ~ 2.85 GeV, and so on. Selection rules from Ch 12 §12.4 suppress the coupling of odd-n_η modes to Standard Model fermions. The even modes decay promptly; the odd modes are stable on cosmological timescales. Candidate: the n_η = 1 dark mode at 0.95 GeV.

   **Class B — η-boundary localized modes.** At the Waters Below boundary (η = η_B), there is a tower of ripple modes — the same sector Ch 13 used to build the PMNS matrix. Some of these ripples do not overlap with the Higgs profile and therefore acquire no Yukawa mass. They behave as nearly-massless, weakly-coupled states. Candidate: an additional right-handed neutrino (or "sterile" neutrino) at mass ~ 0.01–1 eV from the seesaw mechanism. This is the same sector that will close the neutrino mass gap in §14.6.

   **Class C — Vortex-sector dark states.** Ch 10's ξ-ladder gave us three bound states. The extended ξ-ladder Hamiltonian has, in addition, a set of continuum states above the well. These do not localize and do not get Yukawa masses; they propagate as free modes in the Waters Above. Candidate: free continuum states in the ξ-direction, interacting with Standard Model matter only gravitationally. These look phenomenologically like WIMPless dark matter.

   **Class D — Radion/dilaton modes.** The distance between the two membranes (the ξ-modulus) can oscillate. Its excitation is a gravitationally-coupled scalar — a radion — with mass set by the moduli-stabilization potential. Candidate: radion at mass ~ meV scale, if moduli are stabilized by Casimir forces (Vol 4 Ch 9).

3. The critical observation: **none of these candidates sits at the typical WIMP mass** (~ 100 GeV). The Genesis framework is not a WIMP framework. If XENONnT, LZ, or PandaX discovers a WIMP at the electroweak scale with a Higgs-portal coupling, it is not one of these four classes and the framework will owe a fifth.
4. Falsification anchor: if the XENON-class experiments exclude all dark-matter candidates below 10 GeV with cross-sections above 10^{-46} cm^2, Class A (the n_η = 1 mode at 0.95 GeV) is in trouble. The framework's Class A prediction is therefore testable by the next generation of direct-detection experiments.
5. Order-of-magnitude interaction cross-sections for each class, labeled APPROXIMATE. Class A couples through the same η-mode vertices that give the Z boson decays; cross-section ~ 10^{-46}–10^{-44} cm^2. Class B couples only through the neutrino sector; cross-section ~ 10^{-50} cm^2 (untestable in the near future). Class C couples gravitationally; cross-section ~ 10^{-60} cm^2. Class D couples as a radion; cross-section ~ 10^{-50} cm^2.
6. Result 14.3 stated formally: Genesis Physics predicts no WIMP at the electroweak scale. This is a falsifiable prediction.

## §14.3 — Dark Energy from the Waters Above (2 pages)

**Beats:**

1. The Waters Above carry a complex scalar field Ψ_A with a VEV v_A. From the Vol 2 Ch 10 running-coupling analysis, v_A sits at a scale of approximately 10^16 GeV — adjacent to but not identical with the classical GUT scale.
2. The potential is

    V(|Ψ_A|^2) = (λ_A/4)(|Ψ_A|^2 - v_A^2)^2,     (4.14.2)

   with λ_A an O(1) constant not yet computed from first principles. In the vacuum |Ψ_A| = v_A, V = 0; the vacuum energy of Ψ_A is, at tree level, exactly zero.
3. This is the tree-level answer. The one-loop answer is not zero. The Ψ_A field contributes a Casimir-like vacuum energy of order v_A^4 ~ 10^64 GeV^4 — which is roughly 186 orders of magnitude larger than the observed cosmological constant Λ_obs ~ 10^{-122} GeV^4. This is the cosmological-constant problem, in the framework's language.
4. State what the framework does *not* know: why the loop-level vacuum energy is so small. State what the framework *does* offer as a candidate mechanism: a cancellation between the Ψ_A vacuum energy and an equal-and-opposite contribution from the η-boundary of the Waters Below. The cancellation is not computed; it is conjectured, and Vol 5 will return to it.
5. What the framework *does* predict at a tree level: the dark energy equation of state w = -1 (cosmological constant-like). A measurable deviation from w = -1 in future surveys (DESI, Euclid) would require a dynamical dark-energy mechanism not present in the current Genesis Lagrangian.
6. Falsification anchor: if w is measured to deviate from -1 by more than 0.05, the framework owes a dynamical dark-energy sector. If w = -1 to better than 0.01, the framework's cosmological-constant interpretation survives.
7. Result 14.4 stated formally: dark energy is static (w = -1) within the framework's tree-level Ψ_A sector. Any measured deviation is a falsifiable signal.
8. Pointer: the cosmological-constant problem is named, not solved. Vol 5 Ch 12 will return to it with the cosmology-specific tools Vol 4 has not yet built.

## §14.4 — Predictions Testable at Colliders (5 pages)

**Beats (five numbered predictions):**

1. **Prediction 14.1 — The boson-mass precision envelope.** From Ch 11 and Ch 10, the framework's current best predictions are m_t = 173.0 GeV (0.06% off observation), m_H = 125.4 GeV (0.25% off), M_W = 80.42 GeV (0.04% off), M_Z = 91.64 GeV (0.50% off). The ρ parameter is 1.000037 (0.1% off). The framework's prediction is that *all four boson masses simultaneously* lie within a 2% envelope of their current PDG values. If any single mass is later measured to deviate by more than 2%, the derivation of Ch 11 §11.5 is wrong, and the framework fails. This is the single sharpest falsifiable prediction in Vol 4.

2. **Prediction 14.2 — Exactly three generations.** Ch 10 §10.3 predicts exactly three normalizable ξ-ladder bound states. No fourth. This is a topological fact about the Vol 1 Ch 5 double-well potential, not a numerical result. A fourth-generation charged lepton (mass below ~ 1 TeV, accessible at the LHC run 3 or FCC) or a fourth-generation quark of any flavor kills the framework outright. This is Result 14.1 restated as a collider test.

3. **Prediction 14.3 — Higgs branching-ratio pattern.** The Ch 11 §11.7 Higgs branching ratios are h→bb̄ 58.0%, h→W⁺W⁻ 21.5%, h→gg 8.6%, h→τ⁺τ⁻ 6.3%, h→cc̄ 2.9%, h→ZZ 2.6%, h→γγ 0.23%. These are tree-level and loop-level predictions inherited from the Yukawa overlap integrals. Any decay mode observed at > 3σ above 0.5% that is not in this list (e.g., h→invisible at 5%, h→new particle at 1%) signals a new sector beyond the four Waters/boundary classes of §14.2. The framework's allowed invisible-decay fraction is below 2%; the current ATLAS/CMS bound is ~11% at 95% CL, so this prediction is not yet tight but will tighten at HL-LHC.

4. **Prediction 14.4 — FCNC rates at Standard Model levels.** Flavor-changing neutral currents (t→cγ, t→cZ, b→sγ, etc.) arise at loop level in the Standard Model and in the framework equally, because they are GIM-suppressed by the same CKM unitarity inherited from Ch 13. The framework predicts no new FCNC contributions from dark-sector exchange at detectable rates. Observation of any FCNC rate more than an order of magnitude above Standard Model prediction requires new physics not present in the current framework.

5. **Prediction 14.5 — The top-quark decay-width gap.** This is the framework's current embarrassment and its most pointed falsifiable prediction. Ch 11 §11.8 gave Γ_t (tree) ≈ 1.42 GeV and Γ_t (NLO) ≈ 1.56 GeV. The PDG value is 2.0 GeV. The gap is ~ 25%. The framework's prediction is that the missing 0.44 GeV comes from NNLO electroweak corrections that have not yet been computed in the zone-QFT formalism. If those corrections, when computed, do not close the gap to within a few percent, the framework's top-sector vertex structure is wrong. This is an internal falsification test: not an experiment to run, but a calculation to do. It is listed in §14.6 as HIGH priority.

6. One paragraph on what these five predictions have in common: they are all *already* testable, not "in principle testable." The LHC has data. The next decade will either validate or kill the boson-mass envelope, the three-generation count, and the Higgs branching pattern. No waiting for new physics programs.

## §14.6 — The Research Roadmap (4 pages)

**Beats:**

1. Opening paragraph. Thirteen chapters of derivations have left behind debts. This section collects them. Every chapter of Part III pointed forward to one or another open problem, and the reader deserves them consolidated in one place, labeled, prioritized, and connected to the framework's GitHub tracker.
2. **CRITICAL-level open problems** (the BLOCKERS):

   - **RR-1. Spin-1/2 fermion derivation (GitHub #1).** The membrane is a bosonic scalar field Ψ_A. Fermions are spin-1/2. Ch 10 used a Jackiw-Rossi–like mechanism to assume fermionic excitations from vortex cores; this was labeled an assumption, not a derivation. The full derivation requires either (a) an auxiliary spinor postulate, which breaks the framework's minimalism, or (b) a topological mechanism on the 6D manifold that the current analysis has not yet found. Effort estimate: 6–12 months of focused work. Impact: closes the foundation gap in the particle spectrum.

   - **RR-2. The 1000× fermion mass-scale problem (GitHub #2).** Ch 10's Yukawa overlap formula gives correct fermion mass *ratios* but absolute masses off by factors up to 1000 at the light-fermion end. The framework is therefore currently an effective theory at the electroweak scale, not a fundamental one. Four proposed resolution paths from `06-REMAINING_DERIVATIONS.md` §9.1:

     - **Path A — Vortex core fine structure.** Solve 6D equations in the vortex core at the ~ 10^{-35} m scale, look for bound states that set absolute mass. Effort: 3–6 months.
     - **Path B — Multi-layer / fractional winding modes.** Extend topological classification beyond π_1(U(1)_A) = ℤ to higher homotopy groups. Effort: 4–8 months.
     - **Path C — Membrane–water coupling running.** Full RG analysis of the membrane-bulk coupling from the KK scale to the electroweak scale. Effort: 2–4 months.
     - **Path D — Planck-scale decoupling and moduli stabilization.** Derive v_B = 246 GeV from a stability condition on the ξ-modulus potential. Effort: 6–12 months.

     Recommended order: C first (shortest), then A, then B, then D.

3. **HIGH-level open problems:**

   - **RR-3. Higgs potential O(1) coefficients (GitHub #25).** Ch 11 §11.4 fits μ^2, λ, and related constants to precision electroweak data. These should be derived from first principles as boundary conditions on the Ψ_A and Ψ_B fields. Effort: 3–6 months.
   - **RR-4. CKM CP phase δ_CP^q (GitHub #3).** Ch 13 §13.3 gives J_CP^quark ~ few × 10^{-5}, an order-of-magnitude estimate only. Precision depends on the complex phases of ξ-ladder overlap integrals, which Ch 10 did not compute. Effort: 3–4 months.
   - **RR-5. Grand unification.** Does SU(3)_c × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y unify at v_A? Into SU(5), SO(10), or a new zone-specific group? Once answered, proton decay rate is predicted. Effort: 4–6 months.
   - **RR-6. NLO/NNLO corrections in zone QFT.** The framework's predictions are mostly tree-level. Full NNLO structure is needed to close the top-decay-width gap (Prediction 14.5) and to tighten the boson-mass envelope. Effort: 3–4 months.

4. **MEDIUM-level open problems:**

   - **RR-7. Running couplings with full precision (GitHub #26).** Vol 2 Ch 10 did the RG analysis at leading order. Two-loop β-functions in zone QFT need completion.
   - **RR-8. Neutrino mass generation.** Ch 13 used the η-boundary ripple sector to build PMNS structure, but did not derive the absolute neutrino masses. Seesaw mechanism needs to be grounded in Waters Below structure.
   - **RR-9. Moduli stabilization.** Why v_B = 246 GeV and not another value? Depends on Path D above.

5. **LOW-level open problems:**

   - **RR-10. Dark sector particle content.** §14.2 gave four candidate classes; their full Lagrangian structure is not derived.
   - **RR-11. Axion-like particles.** Goldstone bosons from approximate symmetries of 6D compactification have not been classified.
   - **RR-12. Matter–antimatter asymmetry precision.** Sakharov conditions are satisfied structurally (Ch 13 §13.3), but the quantitative baryogenesis calculation is not yet closed.

6. Closing paragraph: twelve items. Two BLOCKERS, four HIGH, three MEDIUM, three LOW. The framework has roughly three years of focused work ahead of it before the Vol 6 experimental test suite (§14.5) and the research roadmap are both in a state the Skeptic would sign off on. This is not small. It is also not infinite.

## Problem Set (2 pages, 8–10 problems)

1. (Conceptual) Why is the framework's prediction of exactly three generations a falsifiable prediction rather than a postdiction? What experimental discovery would kill it?
2. (Order of magnitude) Estimate the Compton wavelength of a 0.95 GeV dark-matter candidate and compare to typical direct-detection target nuclei.

9. (Challenge) Construct a Class E dark-matter candidate the framework's current structure does not include, and identify which sector of the 6D manifold it would have to live in. Comment on whether such a candidate is compatible with Ch 10–13.
