## §8.4 The Friedmann Equations: Two Theorems — 1,400 words

**Topic sentence.** The Einstein equations on the Firmament FLRW metric reduce to *exactly two* independent component equations: a constraint (the Friedmann equation) and an evolution equation (the acceleration equation). The continuity equation follows from the Bianchi identity.

**Why entry point.** Why two and not four? Because FLRW symmetry collapses the ten independent components of the metric to a single dynamical variable $a(t)$, and the EFE then collapses to (a) a constraint on first derivatives of $a$ and (b) an evolution equation on the second derivative.

**Key content.**

- Compute $G_{\mu\nu}$ for the FLRW Firmament metric (5.8.4). Display the only nonzero components: $G^t{}_t$ and $G^i{}_i$ (no sum).
- $tt$-component of the EFE: yields the Friedmann constraint
$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G_4}{3}\,(\rho_A + \rho_B + \rho_b + \rho_r) - \frac{kc^2}{a^2}.$$
Equation (5.8.13). **Theorem 5.8.1** stated.
- Spatial-trace component: yields the acceleration equation
$$\frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G_4}{3}\sum_i\left(\rho_i + \frac{3 P_i}{c^2}\right) + \frac{\Lambda_\text{eff} c^2}{3}.$$
Equation (5.8.18). **Theorem 5.8.2** stated.
- Bianchi identity (Vol 5 §1.8 cited) → continuity equation per species:
$$\dot\rho_i + 3 H\left(\rho_i + \frac{P_i}{c^2}\right) = 0.$$
Equation (5.8.21).
- Compatibility check: differentiate Theorem 5.8.1, substitute Theorems 5.8.2 and the continuity equation; verify that the constraint is preserved by the evolution equations. Eq (5.8.22).

**Why entry point.** Why is era structure a useful organizing tool? Because each era is governed by a single dominant species, and the Friedmann equation degenerates to a single power law for $a(t)$ that admits a closed-form solution.
