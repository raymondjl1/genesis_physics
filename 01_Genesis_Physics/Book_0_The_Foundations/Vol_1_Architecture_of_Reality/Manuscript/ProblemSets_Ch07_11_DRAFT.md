**PS-7.23:**
- **Conservation law**: A quantity Q is constant in time: dQ/dt = 0
- **Symmetry**: The laws of physics are unchanged under a transformation

Connection: If the action S is symmetric under a transformation T (meaning S[T(Φ)] = S[Φ]), then Noether's theorem guarantees a conservation law. But a conservation law doesn't require symmetry—it could arise from a specific initial condition. However, universal conservation laws (like energy) DO arise from symmetries.

**PS-7.29:** Start with action S_total = ∫ d⁴x ℒ(Φ, ∂_μ Φ) where Φ represents all fields.

Under time translation t → t + ε (infinitesimal):
$$\Phi(t,\vec{x}) \to \Phi(t+\varepsilon, \vec{x})$$

Invariance of S requires:
$$\int d⁴x \, \mathcal{L}(\Phi, \partial_\mu \Phi) = \int d⁴x \, \mathcal{L}(\Phi', \partial_\mu \Phi')$$

To first order in ε:
$$\delta \mathcal{L} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \Phi} \delta\Phi + \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu \Phi)} \delta(\partial_\mu \Phi) = 0$$

where $\delta\Phi = \varepsilon \partial_t \Phi$ under time translation.

Using Euler-Lagrange equations: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \Phi} = \partial_\mu \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu \Phi)}$

This leads to:
$$\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$$

where the conserved current is:
$$j^\mu = T^{\mu 0}$$
(the energy-momentum tensor component)

Integrating j⁰ over all space:
$$E = \int d^3x \, j^0 = \text{constant}$$

**PS-7.30:** The warp factor β(z) explicitly breaks z-translation symmetry. The action contains β(∂_z h_μν)² terms, which are NOT invariant under z → z + const.

$$\partial_\mu p^\mu_z = \int dz \, \frac{d\beta}{dz} (\partial_z h_μν)^2 \neq 0$$

The "non-conserved current" measures the force exerted by the z-gradient of the warp factor. Only 3 spatial momentum components are conserved (x, y, time). The extra dimension's momentum is not conserved because of the Firmament's geometry.

$$P_i = \frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z(\beta)}, \quad Z(\beta) = \sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}$$

where β = 1/(k_B T). For a two-level system with E₁ = 0 (ground) and E₂ = 0.1 eV at T = 300 K:
(a) Calculate Z(β)
(b) Find P₁ and P₂
(c) At what temperature would P₂ = P₁?

**PS-11.2** [C] The Zeroth Law: if system A is at temperature T_A and system B is at T_B, and they equilibrate, the final temperature is:
$$T_{\text{final}} = \frac{m_A c_A T_A + m_B c_B T_B}{m_A c_A + m_B c_B}$$

(weighted average). System A: m_A = 500 g, c_A = 4.2 J/(g·K), T_A = 80°C. System B: m_B = 200 g, c_B = 0.9 J/(g·K), T_B = 20°C. Calculate T_final.

**PS-11.3** [C] First Law: ΔU = Q − W. A gas undergoes compression where W = 500 J of work is done on it (W < 0). If the process is adiabatic (Q = 0), what is ΔU? If the initial internal energy U₁ = 1000 J, what is U₂?

**PS-11.4** [C] Entropy from multiplicity: S = k_B ln Ω, where Ω is the number of accessible microstates. For an isolated system with N particles in two equal volumes V/2 (left and right):
- Initial (all particles on left): Ω_i = 1
- Final (equilibrium): Ω_f = 2^N (each particle can be left or right)

Calculate ΔS = k_B(ln 2^N − ln 1) = Nk_B ln 2 for N = 10²³ (Avogadro). Compare to 1 J/K.

**PS-11.5** [C] Partition function for harmonic oscillator: Z(β) = Σ_n exp(−β ℏ ω(n + 1/2)) = e^{−βℏω/2} / (1 − e^{−βℏω}).

For ω = 10¹⁴ rad/s and T = 300 K (so βℏω ≈ 50):
(a) Calculate e^{−βℏω} ≈ e^{−50}
(b) Show Z(β) ≈ e^{−βℏω/2} (low temperature limit: first excited state is unpopulated)
(c) Average energy ⟨E⟩ = −∂ln Z/∂β ≈ ℏω/2 (only ground state occupied)

**PS-11.6** [C] Bose-Einstein distribution: n_i = 1/(exp(β(E_i − μ)) − 1), where μ is the chemical potential. For photons, μ = 0 (photons can be created/destroyed). Calculate the average photon number at E = 1 eV and T = 300 K.

**PS-11.7** [C] Fermi-Dirac distribution: n_i = 1/(exp(β(E_i − μ)) + 1). For electrons in a metal at the Fermi energy E_F = μ, show that n(E_F) = 1/2 (half-filled) at any temperature. What is n(E_F + k_B T)?

**PS-11.8** [C] Second Law in Phase 3: dS/dt = L·Δκ, where L = 10 J/(K·s) and Δκ (loss of sustaining power) decays as Δκ(t) = 0.5 exp(−t/100 s).
(a) Integrate to find total entropy production over 1000 s
(b) Compare to maximum possible: if all sustaining failed instantaneously (Δκ → ∞ briefly), would dS be larger?

**PS-11.9** [C] Third Law: entropy approaches S₀ as T → 0. For a crystal with N atoms, at T = 0, S = 0 (perfect order). At finite T, the number of excited states is ~exp(−β·ε_ex), where ε_ex is excitation energy. Show S(T → 0) → 0 if β·ε_ex → ∞.

**PS-11.10** [C] Phase transition via Landau free energy: F = F₀ − aT|M|² + bT|M|⁴ (order parameter M = magnetization).
(a) For T > T_c = a/(2b), show that dF/dM = 0 gives M = 0 (disordered)
(b) For T < T_c, show M ≠ 0 (ordered)
(c) Calculate the latent heat L = F(T, M=0) − F(T, M≠0) at T = T_c

**PS-11.11** [C] Clausius-Clapeyron equation: dP/dT = L/(T·ΔV), where L is latent heat and ΔV is volume change. For water's vapor pressure curve, L = 40.7 kJ/mol and ΔV_vap ≈ 30 L/mol at T = 373 K (boiling point). Calculate dP/dT at the boiling point.

**PS-11.12** [C] Open system extension: for a three-reservoir system (Zone 2.2.3, 2.2.1, 2.2.2), energy rates are:
$$\dot{E}_A = \Phi_A(T_A - T_{\text{eq}})$$
$$\dot{E}_B = \Phi_B(T_B - T_{\text{eq}})$$
$$\dot{E}_C = -(\dot{E}_A + \dot{E}_B)$$
(conservation of total energy). Show that at steady state, T_eq = (Φ_A T_A + Φ_B T_B)/(Φ_A + Φ_B).

**PS-11.13** [C] Quasi-steady state energy budget: the 68%-27%-5% split refers to energy distribution in a three-zone system. If total energy in Zone 2.2 is E_total = 10⁴ J:
- Zone 2.2.3 (Waters Above): 68% = 6800 J
- Zone 2.2.1 (Waters Below): 27% = 2700 J
- Zone 2.2.2 (Firmament/Matter): 5% = 500 J

Verify these satisfy energy conservation: 6800 + 2700 + 500 = 10,000 J.

**PS-11.14** [C] Entropy and information: for a system with W possible microstates of equal probability, S = k_B ln W = k_B ln(2^N) = Nk_B ln 2 (in bits). For a classical computer storing N bits of information, the minimum entropy is S_min = Nk_B ln 2. Calculate S_min for a 1 GB hard drive (8 × 10⁹ bits).

**PS-11.15** [C] Mode freezing (Third Law): as T → 0, modes with energy ε > k_B T freeze out (become unpopulated). For T = 1 K, k_B T ≈ 10⁻²³ J. Calculate the ratio ε/k_B T for a vibrational mode with ω = 10¹³ rad/s (ε = ℏω ≈ 10⁻²⁰ J). Is this mode frozen?

**PS-11.29** [X] **Entropy Maximization and Boltzmann Distribution**: Derive the Boltzmann distribution from the principle of maximum entropy subject to constraints.

Given:
- Constraint 1: Σ_i P_i = 1 (normalization)
- Constraint 2: Σ_i P_i E_i = ⟨E⟩ (fixed average energy)

Use Lagrange multipliers to maximize S = −k_B Σ_i P_i ln P_i:

(a) Write the constrained Lagrangian: L = S − λ₁(Σ P_i − 1) − λ₂(Σ P_i E_i − ⟨E⟩)
(b) Vary w.r.t. P_i and set ∂L/∂P_i = 0
(c) Show this gives P_i ∝ e^{−βE_i} where β = λ₂ (identify β = 1/(k_B T))
(d) The partition function Z = Σ_i e^{−βE_i} normalizes the distribution

**PS-11.30** [X] **Derivation of the Second Law from Zone Architecture**:

In the zone framework, three separate reservoirs (Waters Above, Waters Below, Firmament) can exchange energy. Show that the Second Law emerges naturally.

(a) Define the multiplicity Ω_A, Ω_B, Ω_C for each zone as functions of their internal energies
(b) Total multiplicity: Ω_total = Ω_A(E_A) · Ω_B(E_B) · Ω_C(E_C), subject to E_A + E_B + E_C = E_total (constant)
(c) Equilibrium: maximize Ω_total w.r.t. E_A, E_B (treating E_C as dependent on E_A, E_B via constraint)
(d) Show that ∂ln Ω_A/∂E_A = ∂ln Ω_B/∂E_B = ∂ln Ω_C/∂E_C (equal "temperatures")
(e) The entropy increase when moving toward equilibrium follows from Ω_eq > Ω_initial

**PS-11.31** [X] **Phase Dependence of Entropy Growth**: Construct S(t) such that:
- Phase 1 (Creation, 0 < t < t₁): dS/dt < 0 (entropy decreases, order emerges)
- Phase 2 (Edenic, t₁ < t < t₂): dS/dt = 0 (entropy held constant by sustaining)
- Phase 3 (Fall, t > t₂): dS/dt > 0 (entropy increases, order decays)

(a) Propose a functional form: S(t) = A sin(πt/(2t₁)) − B·θ(t − t₁)·(t − t₁) − C·θ(t − t₂)·(t − t₂)²
(where θ is the step function)

(c) Calculate total entropy change: ΔS_total = S(∞) − S(0)

**PS-11.32** [X] **Third Law and Quantum Ground States**: The Third Law states S → S₀ as T → 0. For a system with ground state degeneracy (multiple states at lowest energy E₀), S₀ = k_B ln g₀ where g₀ is the degeneracy.

(a) Show that for a non-degenerate ground state (g₀ = 1), S₀ = 0
(b) For a degenerate ground state (g₀ > 1), explain why S₀ > 0
(c) Example: a paramagnetic salt with spin-1/2 moments has 2^N ground states (one for each spin orientation) at T = 0 in zero field. Show S₀ = Nk_B ln 2.

**PS-11.33** [X] **Open System Thermodynamics — Three Reservoirs**: The zone system (A, B, C) exchanges energy but not particles. Derive the equilibrium condition and energy distribution.

Given energy rates:
$$\dot{E}_A = \kappa_A (T_A - T_{eq})$$
$$\dot{E}_B = \kappa_B (T_B - T_{eq})$$
$$\dot{E}_C = -(\dot{E}_A + \dot{E}_B)$$

(a) Define the heat capacity of each zone: C_A = dE_A/dT_A, etc.
(b) Show that T_eq = (C_A T_A + C_B T_B + C_C T_C)/(C_A + C_B + C_C) at equilibrium (dE_i/dt = 0)

**PS-11.34** [X] **Landau Theory of Phase Transitions**: Near a critical temperature T_c, the free energy can be expanded in the order parameter M (magnetization, density, etc.):

**PS-11.35** [X] **Entropy Production Rate and Sustainability**: In Phase 3, dS/dt = L·Δκ where L is the entropy generation rate and Δκ measures loss of sustaining power.

## Pedagogical Design Notes

1. **Computational Problems (C)**: Build skills in applying mathematical concepts, numerical reasoning, and quantitative manipulation. Progress from simple (single-formula application) to complex (multi-step calculations with standard formulas).

2. **Conceptual/Why Problems (W)**: Develop deep understanding of underlying principles. Ask "why does X follow from Y?" Encourage synthesis across topics and connection to zone architecture.

3. **Challenge Problems (X)**: Extend students to the frontier. Some require derivations from first principles, others probe connections to theology and philosophy. A few are open-ended or controversial, designed to provoke thought.

4. **Voice**: Feynman-style throughout—clear statement of the problem, hints for the curious, occasional playfulness, always an eye toward the "why" not just the "how."

5. **Consistency**: All problems reference only material from the current chapter and prior chapters (no forward references to later chapters). This allows students to work sequentially without spoilers.

6. **Theological Integration**: Every chapter includes at least 2-3 problems that connect physics to Genesis, divine attributes, or the zone framework's metaphysical foundations. This reinforces the core vision.
