### Selected Solutions — Chapter 5

**PS-5.1:** A codimension-2 hypersurface in 6D has intrinsic dimension $6 - 2 = 4$. The Firmament is 4-dimensional (three spatial + one temporal).

**PS-5.3:** The induced metric $h_{\mu\nu}$ on a 4D Firmament is a 4×4 symmetric tensor with $\frac{4 \cdot 5}{2} = 10$ independent components.

**PS-5.5:** For a flat 2D membrane embedded in 3D (as a plane in ordinary 3D space), the membrane is not curved relative to the ambient space. Thus $K_{ab} = 0$ everywhere.

**PS-5.7:** $\sqrt{-\det h}$ is the proper area element on the brane. The negative sign accounts for the fact that the determinant of a metric with Lorentzian signature is negative (similar to the spacetime volume element $\sqrt{-g}$).

**PS-5.13:** Units check:
- $[\sigma] = \text{kg/(m·s}^2\text{)}$ (force per unit length)
- $[\mu] = \text{kg/m}^3$ (mass per volume)
- $[\sigma/\mu] = \frac{\text{kg/(m·s}^2\text{)}}{\text{kg/m}^3} = \frac{\text{m}^3}{\text{m·s}^2} = \frac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2}$
- $[\sqrt{\sigma/\mu}] = \text{m/s}$ ✓ (velocity)

**PS-5.15:** For a displacement $\delta x^\mu = \varepsilon(x^\alpha) n^\mu$, the first-order change in induced metric is:
$$\delta h_{\mu\nu} = -2\varepsilon K_{\mu\nu}$$

(The change is proportional to the extrinsic curvature, as expected.)

**PS-5.17:** For a massless scalar mode (dispersion relation $\omega^2 = c^2 k^2$):
$$\omega = ck$$

**PS-5.19:** For a stable equilibrium (a minimum of the action), the second variation is positive: $m_{\text{eff}}^2 > 0$.

**PS-5.23:** The trace $K = h^{ab}K_{ab}$ is the mean curvature of the Firmament. It represents the average curvature in all directions perpendicular to the Firmament.

**PS-5.27:** For a flat membrane, the normal is constant everywhere. If the membrane is the plane $z = 0$ in 3D, then $n^\mu = (0, 0, 1)$ (or normalized).

**PS-5.31:** Converting $\sigma = 6 \times 10^{98}$ kg/(m·s²) to J/m²:
$$\sigma = 6 \times 10^{98} \text{ J/m}^2$$

(Since 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s², we have 1 kg/(m·s²) = 1 J/m².)

For a Firmament with area $A \sim (L_A)^2 \sim 10^{53}$ m² (cosmological scale), the total energy is $E \sim \sigma A \sim 10^{151}$ J (enormous, reflecting the fundamental energy scale of the universe).

**PS-5.33:** For $\omega = ck$:
$$v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = c$$
$$v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = c$$

Phase and group velocities are equal (non-dispersive, as expected for massless modes).

**PS-5.37:** For a flat Firmament with induced metric $h_{ab} = \eta_{ab}$ (Minkowski), the scalar curvature is:
$$R_{\Sigma} = 0$$

**PS-5.49:** The formula $R_{\Sigma} = R - 2\mathcal{R}(n) - K^2$ decomposes as:
- $R$: scalar curvature of the ambient 6D space
- $-2\mathcal{R}(n)$: contribution from curvature in the normal directions
- $-K^2$: contribution from extrinsic curvature (how the Firmament bends)

**PS-5.53:** For a brane-localized scalar field, the equation of motion is:
$$\square_\Sigma \phi - m^2 \phi + \xi K \phi = 0$$

where $\square_\Sigma = h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu$ is the d'Alembertian on the Firmament, $m^2$ is the mass squared, and $\xi K \phi$ is a coupling to the mean curvature.

### Selected Solutions — Chapter 6

**PS-6.1:** The Lagrangian density for the Waters fields is schematically:
$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\Psi_A\partial_\nu\Psi_A - \frac{1}{2}m_A^2\Psi_A^2 - V_A(\Psi_A)$$
(and similarly for $\Psi_B$), plus cross-terms if the fields couple.

**PS-6.3:** The Euler-Lagrange equation for $\Psi$ is:
$$\square \Psi + m^2 \Psi + \frac{\partial V}{\partial \Psi} = 0$$

where the d'Alembertian is $\square = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g} g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu)$.

**PS-6.7:** For a scalar field with kinetic term $T_{\text{kin}} = -\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\Psi\partial_\nu\Psi$, the stress-energy is:
$$T^{\mu\nu} = \partial^\mu\Psi\partial^\nu\Psi - \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho\Psi\partial_\sigma\Psi$$

The trace is $T^\mu_\mu = \partial^\mu\Psi\partial_\mu\Psi - 2 \times \frac{1}{2}g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho\Psi\partial_\sigma\Psi = 0$. (Massless scalar field has zero trace.)
