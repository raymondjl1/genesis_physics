### Selected Solutions — Chapter 3

**PS-3.1:** The negative signature in the first component represents the timelike direction. In signature $(-,+,+,+,+,+)$, the first coordinate (usually $t$) is timelike, and the remaining five are spacelike. Timelike intervals have $ds^2 < 0$; spacelike intervals have $ds^2 > 0$.

**PS-3.3:** The hierarchy forms nested regions:
- $Z_0$ (outermost, transcendent source)
- $Z_1$ (Heaven Prime, causality)
- $Z_2$ (Earth Prime, material cosmos)
  - $Z_{2.1}$ (Atemporal Domain, branching off)
  - $Z_{2.2}$ (Firmament Domain, temporal)
    - $Z_{2.2.1}$ (Waters Below, dark matter)
    - $Z_{2.2.2}$ (Condensed Matter, baryons)
    - $Z_{2.2.3}$ (Waters Above, dark energy)

**PS-3.5:** If the zone manifold is 6D and the Firmament is codimension-1, then the Firmament is $6 - 1 = 5$-dimensional.

**PS-3.9:** For $n = 6$:
$$N = \frac{n^2(n^2-1)}{12} = \frac{36 \cdot 35}{12} = \frac{1260}{12} = 105$$

**PS-3.13:** In coordinates $(t, x, y, z, \xi, \eta)$ with signature $(-,+,+,+,+,+)$, the coordinate $t$ is timelike (associated with the negative signature).

**PS-3.19:** For vacuum ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$) with cosmological constant:
$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$$

Equivalently:
$$R_{\mu\nu} = \left(\frac{R}{2} - \Lambda\right) g_{\mu\nu}$$

**PS-3.21:** For flat $\mathbb{R}^4$, the metric is $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ (the Minkowski metric), which is constant. The Riemann tensor vanishes everywhere, so the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature both vanish:
$$R = 0$$

**PS-3.22:** Under the rescaling $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = \lambda^2 g_{\mu\nu}$:
$$\tilde{R} = \lambda^{-2} R$$

The scalar curvature scales inversely with the square of the metric rescaling.

**PS-3.23:** For a block-diagonal metric $g = \text{diag}(g_{00}, g_{11}, g_{22}, g_{33}, g_{55}, g_{66})$:
$$g = g_{00} \cdot g_{11} \cdot g_{22} \cdot g_{33} \cdot g_{55} \cdot g_{66}$$

**PS-3.27:** For a flat hyperplane in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the hyperplane is not curved within the ambient space. The extrinsic curvature is:
$$K_{\mu\nu} = 0$$

**PS-3.33:** Yes, the zone manifold is orientable if it admits a consistent global choice of orientation. A pseudo-Riemannian manifold with signature $(-,+,+,+,+,+)$ is orientable (like Minkowski spacetime).

**PS-3.35:** An induced metric on a codimension-1 hypersurface is a 5×5 matrix (5D submanifold), which has $\frac{5 \cdot 6}{2} = 15$ independent components (symmetric matrix).

**PS-3.37:** For block-diagonal metrics:
$$g = \text{diag}(g_A, g_B, g_C) \quad \Rightarrow \quad |g| = |g_A| \cdot |g_B| \cdot |g_C|$$

where $g_A, g_B, g_C$ are the determinants of the diagonal blocks.

**PS-3.41:** A spherically symmetric metric in 4D has three metric functions: $\Phi(r)$, $\Lambda(r)$, and the spatial metric (which depends on the angular coordinates through the factor $r^2$). So typically three functions must be determined.

**PS-3.45:** The event horizon radius is where $g_{tt} = 0$:
$$1 - \frac{2GM}{r} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad r_s = 2GM$$

This is the Schwarzschild radius.

**PS-3.49:** In 6D, the total number of independent Riemann components is $N = 105$. The Ricci tensor has $\frac{6 \cdot 7}{2} = 21$ independent components. The Weyl tensor is the trace-free part:
$$N_{\text{Weyl}} = 105 - 21 = 84$$

**PS-3.51:** For a translational symmetry perpendicular to the Firmament (say, in a direction $\xi$ normal to the boundary), the Killing vector is:
$$K = \partial_\xi$$

This generates translations along the normal direction while preserving the metric.

**PS-4.1:** The 4D spacetime metric is recovered by restricting the 6D metric to the 4D subspace (setting the extra-dimensional coordinates to specific values or integrating them out). Schematically, if the 6D metric is $G_{\mu\nu}$ (with $\mu, \nu = 0, \ldots, 5$) and the 4D subspace is coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$, then the 4D metric is $g_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu}|_{\text{4D subspace}}$ (with $\mu, \nu = 0, 1, 2, 3$).

$$ds^2 = e^{2A(\xi,\eta)}[-c^2 dt^2 + a(t)^2 d\vec{r}^2] + e^{2B(\xi,\eta)}[d\xi^2 + d\eta^2]$$
