## "Why" Chain

1. **Why do we need a chapter on the speed of light and gravity in a family-edition book?** — Because the two most famous numbers in science — *the speed of light is 299,792,458 meters per second, and gravity makes things fall at 9.8 meters per second squared* — are numbers every schoolchild is handed as *given facts of the universe.* Standard physics' posture is: here are the constants; measure them; memorize them; move on. Scripture's posture is different. The Psalmist says the heavens declare *the glory of God,* not the glory of the constants. If the numbers are really foundational, and if Scripture is telling us Someone laid those foundations, then the numbers should trace back to a *reason.* The framework gives the family that reason.

2. **Why is the speed of light what it is?** — Because light is a ripple in the firmament, and every ripple in every stretched surface has a speed determined by two things: how tight the surface is stretched (its *tension*) and how heavy the surface is per unit area (its *density*). Pluck a guitar string — the note you hear is determined by the string's tension and density. The same physics governs the firmament. The speed of light is the propagation speed of the firmament membrane. If the firmament were stretched differently, light would travel at a different speed. Far from being a free parameter God tuned in isolation, *c* is the firmament's *voice* — the natural tone of the fabric He stretched out in Genesis 1:6–8.

3. **Why is gravity what it is?** — Because mass presses into the firmament and dents it. A bowling ball on a trampoline. A heavy boot on a tensioned tarp. The dent is what we call *gravity,* and its depth is set by the same membrane properties that set the speed of light. We do not feel a mysterious "force" pulling us toward the earth; we live inside the dent the earth makes in the firmament, and the dent's geometry is what we experience as weight. The strength of gravity — the gravitational constant *G* — is the firmament's *stiffness* to dents. If the firmament were stiffer, gravity would be weaker. If the firmament were more compliant, gravity would be stronger.

4. **Why are *c* and *G* cousins?** — Because both emerge from the *same* membrane. The speed-of-light *c* comes from the tension-to-density ratio; the gravity-constant *G* comes from the stiffness. Both are properties of *one* fabric, and the framework's derivation shows the relationship explicitly. They are not independent constants God tuned in separate rooms. They are two characteristics of one artifact He stretched out on the morning the morning stars sang together.

5. **Why does Job 38 fit this chapter?** — Because the rhetorical question the Lord puts to Job is *exactly* the chapter's question read from the other direction. *"Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?"* presupposes that the earth's foundations were *laid* — set down with intent — and asks Job (and the reader) whether he was present when it happened. The right answer, in Job's day and in ours, is *No.* But the posture the answer leaves us in is not ignorance for its own sake; it is the posture of someone encountering work done by Somebody, in our absence, that we are now beginning to understand well enough to describe. Humility informed by the fact that we are starting to see.

6. **Why not just say "God made it this way"?** — Because that answer, offered without the mechanism, teaches the child to stop at the wall. "God made it" is *true,* but it is not the whole truth the Scripture itself invites us to learn. Proverbs 8 shows Wisdom *present* at the laying of the foundations — *rejoicing before him always,* the text says — a scene that is clearly an invitation to look at the work and see what it is. The framework's job is to make that look possible in the family register. The child should grow up knowing that "God made it" and "here is how the firmament does this" are the same sentence in two vocabularies.

7. **Why does this matter beyond the chapter?** — Because every future conversation the family will have about physics — relativity, cosmology, quantum mechanics, spaceflight — sits on top of *c* and *G.* If the child has already been given the picture that these are derived properties of a specific fabric that God stretched out, every later topic lands in a different theological landscape than it does for a child who was handed the constants as brute facts. The cosmos is no longer an unreadable machine. It is a fabric whose Weaver is named.
