## §1. The Question My Wife Asked That Changed How I Read Day 1

It was a Saturday morning on the porch. Early enough that the coffee was still too hot to drink but late enough that the sun had cleared the ridge and the cows had stopped complaining about breakfast. My wife had her Bible open on her knees. I had mine next to my cup. We were reading slowly, the way we sometimes do on weekends when nothing is on the calendar and the wind is gentle in the fir trees behind the shop.

She looked up after a minute and said, *"Can I ask you the thing I have never asked you?"*

I said of course.

*"How is there light on Day 1 when God doesn't make the sun until Day 4?"*

She wasn't trying to corner me. She wasn't quoting a skeptic. She was a homeschool teacher asking the honest question that rises up in every Christian household that reads Genesis 1 slowly enough to notice. I had been waiting for this question from her for a while. I took a long drink of the coffee, set the cup down, and told her what I want to tell you in this chapter.

In Chapter 1 we learned how to read Genesis. We learned to read it the way an engineer reads a requirements document — not as poetry, not as myth, but as the description of a built system by the One who built it. We met three Hebrew words that changed what the first two verses said: *bereshit, bara, tohu va-bohu.*

In Chapter 2 we read Day 2. We met two more words — *raqia* and *mayim* — and the architecture of the whole cosmos opened up under our hands. A stretched expanse. Two fields of pre-structured substance. Three layers: Waters Above, the Firmament, Waters Below. We saw that what modern physics calls dark energy and dark matter, the Bible has been calling by other names for three thousand years.

Now I want to walk you back to Day 1. I held Chapter 2 in front of Chapter 3 on purpose. You cannot easily answer my wife's question about the light until you know there is an *architecture* for a field of light to live in. Now that you have the architecture, I can answer her question. I can answer it with the careful confidence of an engineer who has read the specification against the product, and found — with a kind of quiet astonishment — that they match.

Let me show you what I told her.

## §2. Read Day 1 Again, Slowly

Here is what the text says. Read it once for beauty, and then read it again for precision.

> *"And God said, Let there be light: and there was light. And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness. And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day."* — Genesis 1:3–5 (KJV)

Five short verses. Five small acts. Let's walk them the way Chapter 1 taught us.

*"And God said, 'Let there be light.'"* That is a spoken command. Not a machining operation. Not a chemical reaction. A *word,* and then a thing. Chapter 1 taught us that the Hebrew verb for the creating act in Genesis 1:1 is *bara* — a verb used in the Hebrew Bible only with God as the subject, describing the origination of something that was not there before. Day 1 is *bara* in action. The word *says,* and the thing *is.*

*"And there was light."* The capacity appears. Notice what the text does not say. It does not say, *and the sun rose.* It does not say, *and a fire was kindled.* It does not say, *and a lamp was lit.* It says the plainest possible thing. *There was light.* The Hebrew noun is *or* — pronounced *ohr.* It is the ordinary Hebrew word for light, the same word you will find later in the Bible for the sun's light, for the light of a lamp, for the light of the eyes, and — in several of its most load-bearing moments — for the presence of God Himself. In Genesis 1:3 the word is unadorned. It does not specify a *source.* It names a *condition.*

*"And God saw that the light was good."* That is an acceptance criterion. Chapter 1 showed us that the Hebrew word for *good* in this chapter — *tov* — is doing the functional work an engineer does when he signs off a system as *meeting spec.* It is not an aesthetic judgment. It is a checkbox. The light met what it was made to do. *Passes acceptance.*

*"And God separated the light from the darkness."* This is where Day 1 becomes structure. Before this sentence, the text had *tohu va-bohu* — unstructured, unshaped, a primordial condition. After this sentence, there are two things where there had been an undifferentiated state. *This* is light. *That* is darkness. A division has been imposed. The first division in the whole Bible is not a division of substances — it is a division of *states,* right through the middle of what had been a single condition.

*"And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And there was evening and there was morning, the first day."* Now the division is named. The two states get vocabulary. *Day* and *Night.* And then — this is what most readers miss — the text closes the first day with the cadence it will use for every day that follows: *and there was evening, and there was morning.* The order-of-time is already working. Not because the sun is rising and setting; it will not exist for three more days. Because a *rhythm* has been imposed on the cosmos. The rhythm will later be *measured* by the sun and the moon, but the rhythm itself — the movement of day into night and back into day — is prior to any measuring body.

Now pause, and hold what you just read next to Day 4.

> *"And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night… and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years… And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also."* — Genesis 1:14–16 (KJV, abridged)

The Bible itself sets up the order-of-days question. It gives you the light on Day 1 and the luminaries on Day 4 and lets you feel the gap. It is not trying to hide it from you. It is pointing at it.

The Bible is telling the order in a way you would not have thought to tell it if you were making this up. If you were making this up, you would put the sun on Day 1. You would have to. Sun first, then light. That is the common-sense order. Children draw it that way. Every culture except one drew it that way.

Genesis 1 draws it a different way. Light first. Sources later.

## §3. Light Is Not a Property of the Sun

Here is the claim the rest of this chapter is built on. I want to state it plainly, because once you see it you cannot un-see it.

*The sun is not the source of light. The sun is a specific kind of object that uses light's capacity to do a specific job.*

Read that twice.

Most people walk through life thinking light is what the sun does. The sun is on, and therefore there is light. The sun goes down, and therefore it is dark. The light is a consequence of the sun, the way the whirring is a consequence of the fan. Turn the fan off and the whirring stops. Turn the sun off and — surely — the light stops.

That is not what modern physics has found. That is not what the Bible said. And I want to show you, with two pictures from your own house, what the Bible said and what physics has confirmed.

[FIGURE: Fig 2.3.2 — Light is a capacity of the field, not a property of the source. Two panels. Left: a guitar with its string drawn under visible tension, a small caption reading *the string's capacity to vibrate — prior to any specific note.* Right: a lamp plugged into a wall outlet, with the wiring shown as a continuous warm line running through the wall behind it, a small caption reading *the wiring is hot even if no lamp is plugged in.* Center label across the bottom: *The sun is a lamp. The field is the wiring.*]

Think first about the guitar in your living room. The string is the source of the sound, right? You pluck the string and the string rings and there is a note in the air. Take away the string and there is no note.

But that is not quite right. The string does not *create* the capacity for sound. The string *uses* the capacity. The air around the string, and the tension in the string itself, and the rules that govern how vibrations move through air — all of that was there before you plucked anything. The string is not the source of the possibility of sound. The string is a specific thing that the possibility of sound gets to act through. If you had a thousand other kinds of objects under tension in the same room, they could all ring too, each with their own note. The *capacity* for the air to carry a vibration is prior to any particular vibration in it.

Now think about the lamp on your nightstand. You flip the switch and the lamp is on. You think, *the lamp is the source of the light.* But go unplug the lamp for a minute and look at the outlet. The wiring behind the wall is still hot. There is voltage sitting there. The capacity for current to flow is not something the lamp creates. The capacity is in the wiring. The lamp is a specific device that gets to use the capacity when you plug it back in. Plug in a different lamp and the new lamp lights up instead. Plug in a television and a different kind of thing happens. The *wiring* is prior. The *device* is later.

The electromagnetic field is the wiring. The sun is a lamp.

The electromagnetic field is what physicists call the invisible, pervasive structure of our universe that lets electric charges push and pull on one another and lets ripples in that pushing-and-pulling travel at the speed of light through space. Every ripple is what we call a photon. Every photon is carrying energy from one place to another at that speed. A star, a candle, a phone screen, a firefly, a lightning bolt, a microwave oven, the radio in your truck — every one of those things is *matter* coupling to the electromagnetic field. The field is prior. The emitters are later.

> **What we mean by *field* in this book.** This is the first time the word *field* appears, and you will meet it many more times — the *electromagnetic field* here, the *Waters Below* (dark matter) in Chapters 6 and 7, the *Waters Above* (dark energy), the sustaining coupling in Chapter 8. So let me say once what the word means, and you can carry it the whole way. A *field* is not a thing sitting in a place; it is a property that space itself carries at every point — a capacity, present everywhere, that can be stirred into ripples and patterns. The surface of a pond is a homely picture: calm, it is still everywhere; disturbed, it carries waves. These are not all the *same* field — the electromagnetic field and the Waters Below are genuinely different fields, with different jobs. But they are the *same kind of thing:* properties woven into the fabric of the cosmos that can hold a ripple or a standing pattern. When you read *field* anywhere in this book, that is what is meant — not a fog floating in space, but a feature space itself has, everywhere, all at once.

And the way we know the field is prior is not just theoretical. In the late 1940s a handful of physicists predicted that if the universe had been very hot and very dense in its earliest moments, there should be a faint microwave glow filling all of space today — left over from when the early cosmos cooled enough for light to stream freely for the first time. They predicted a specific temperature, about three degrees above absolute zero, the same in every direction.

In 1964, two engineers at Bell Labs in New Jersey, Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson, were chasing a persistent hiss on a sensitive microwave antenna. They cleaned it. They scraped the pigeon droppings off it. The hiss stayed. It was coming at them from every direction in the sky, at exactly the temperature the theorists had predicted. It won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1978. We call it the cosmic microwave background, and it is the oldest light in the universe. It was emitted roughly three hundred and eighty thousand years after the beginning — long before any star, galaxy, or sun. Light filled the cosmos before there were any *things* in the cosmos shaped to emit it.

Read that next to Genesis 1:3 and Genesis 1:14.

The Bible said light on Day 1 and sources on Day 4 because that is the order the universe actually unfolds. Not the order a storyteller would guess. The order a specification would give.

Ask a physicist what lives *on* that kind of membrane, and the first thing he will tell you is: it can carry waves. A drumhead can carry waves. The surface of a pond can carry waves. ## §5. God Is Light

I want to take you now to the part of this chapter I took longest to write. It is the part that is heavier than anything we said in Chapter 1 or Chapter 2, because it is the part where the Bible is making a claim about God Himself, and I want to handle that claim the way an engineer handles a critical specification — with care, and without overclaiming, and without dodging.

The Bible does not only say that God *made* light. In several of its most load-bearing moments, it says that God *is* light.

Listen to the Apostle John, writing to the early church:

> *"This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all."* — 1 John 1:5 (KJV)

Listen to the Apostle Paul, writing to Timothy about the God who has always been:

> *"...Who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; whom no man hath seen, nor can see: to whom be honour and power everlasting. Amen."* — 1 Timothy 6:16 (KJV)

Listen to Jesus Himself, in the temple courts:

> *"I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life."* — John 8:12 (KJV)

And listen to how John opens his gospel — deliberately echoing the opening of Genesis, deliberately reaching back to Day 1:

> *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… In him was life; and the life was the light of men. And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not."* — John 1:1, 4–5 (KJV)

Four different writers. Four different centuries. Four different occasions. They all reach for the same vocabulary. *Light.* Not as a metaphor they invented after discovering electromagnetism, which they did not live long enough to do. As the Bible's own word for the nature of God, carried forward from Genesis 1 through the Psalms through the prophets through the Gospels through the letters to the churches.

Here is the thing to see, and I say it carefully because I do not want to say more than the evidence supports. The biblical vocabulary of *light* was not reverse-engineered from physics. It was there first. When John says *God is light,* he is not saying *God is like a lamp.* The Greek word behind it is *phos* — the same word used for sunlight, for the light of the eye, for the light of the dawn. John is saying that the capacity we see most clearly in created light — the capacity for *revelation,* for making things visible and *knowable* — originates in the divine nature itself. Light is not a thing God happens to make. Day 1 is not God creating something foreign to Himself. Day 1 is God extending a capacity that He Himself, in His own nature, already is.

That claim is larger than anything we have said so far, and I do not want to overreach. I do not want to tell you that the cosmic microwave background *proves* that Jesus Christ is the light of the world. Physics does not prove theological claims. What physics has done is a different and quieter thing. It has confirmed that the *vocabulary* the Bible has been using for three thousand years — *light, darkness, expansion, sustenance, stretching* — is the vocabulary a careful observer would reach for if he were trying to describe the cosmos modern instruments are actually measuring. The words line up. They line up on Day 1 as precisely as they lined up on Day 2. The cosmos the Bible has been naming is the cosmos we are finding.

And the Bible, from Genesis through Revelation, says that the light at the center of this whole cosmos originates in God. It says it before there was a sun for it to say this *about.* It says it at the opening of John's gospel where the Logos — *logos,* the Word — and the Light are named as the same reality. It says it in Paul's letter to the Corinthians when he writes, *"For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ"* (2 Corinthians 4:6, KJV). And it says it finally in Revelation — *"And the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof"* (Revelation 21:23, KJV). The Bible begins with light on Day 1 and ends with a city where the sun and the moon are not needed because God Himself is the light. The vocabulary is consistent. The architecture is consistent. The claim is consistent.

You do not have to accept that larger claim to finish reading this book. ## §6. But What About…?

I promised you in Chapter 1 that I would not dodge the hard questions. This chapter has three of them, and I want to take them on in order. A version of each is going to show up in a humanities class or a dinner-table conversation eventually. When it does, you will have the language.

**First: "Isn't it a contradiction that the Bible puts light on Day 1 and the sun on Day 4?"**

No, and that sequence is one of the places the Bible is most scientifically prescient. Light is a *capacity* of the electromagnetic field. A glowing object is a localized coupling to that field — a specific piece of matter, shaped to oscillate in a particular way, radiating energy into the field that surrounds it. The field is prior to any particular oscillator. Physics says so. The cosmic microwave background is observational evidence that light was filling the universe hundreds of millions of years before the first star. Genesis said light on Day 1 and sources on Day 4 because *that is the order the actual universe unfolds.* The objection reads Genesis as if it were written by someone who had never seen the universe; it assumes the author should have done the obvious thing and put the sun first. Genesis does the non-obvious thing, which is also the correct thing. That is worth a second look, not an eye-roll.

**Second: "How can physics even describe light without a source? Every light you've ever seen came from a source."**

It can, and this is not a technicality. The mathematical backbone of electromagnetism — the equations that describe how electric and magnetic fields behave, how they propagate, how they interact — is written down in terms of the *field itself,* independent of any particular emitter. An emitter shows up in those equations as a charge-and-current term that you plug into the field equations after they have already been stated. In plainer language: physics first writes the rules that the field follows, and then asks what happens when you put a charged object into the field. The rules are prior to the objects. A universe with *no* emitters — no stars, no candles, no fireflies, nothing radiating — still has electromagnetic waves in it if the initial conditions put energy into the field. Those waves are what the universe had for its first few hundred thousand years. We can see them. We did see them, in 1964, and we have been measuring them in ever-finer detail ever since. (For the formal treatment of field-before-source, see *Foundations Vol 2 Ch 3;* for the skeptical-friend version, see *The Hidden Architecture,* Chapter 10.)

**Third: "Aren't 'light' and 'darkness' in Genesis 1 just moral metaphors? Isn't this a spiritual passage, not a physics passage?"**

In Genesis 1:3–5, no. Later in Scripture, sometimes — but those later uses are built on top of the physical meaning, not in place of it. Genesis 1:3–5 is doing something careful. It quotes the creative act — *Let there be light.* It describes a physical outcome — *there was light.* It records an acceptance judgment — *it was good.* It imposes a physical division — *separated the light from the darkness.* And it *names* the division with the two ordinary words for the two ordinary physical states: *Day* and *Night.* Those are timekeeping words. They are the words we use for the part of the day that is lit and the part of the day that is dark. The text never says *good* and *evil* on this page. It never frames light and darkness as moral opposites in Genesis 1. The moral uses of light and darkness in Isaiah, in the Psalms, in the Gospels, and in Paul's letters come *later,* and they work because they sit on top of the physical reality the Bible has already established on its first page. The metaphor is built on the physics. The physics was not a metaphor. When Isaiah says, *"The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light"* (Isaiah 9:2, KJV), the power of that line depends on the reader already knowing what physical darkness is, and what the physical arrival of a light into it feels like at dawn. Genesis 1 is the chapter that gave the reader that knowing.

Three objections. Three answers. None of them a crisis. Two of them, in fact, are places the Bible is stronger than the skeptical version assumed it was.

## Key Terms

**Or** (*ohr*) — Hebrew noun translated *light.* The common, unadorned Hebrew word for light in both physical and figurative senses; context narrows it. In Genesis 1:3 it names the first *condition* God calls into being — a capacity of the cosmos, not a property of any particular source.

**Phos** (*fohs*) — Greek noun translated *light.* Used in the New Testament for physical light (sunlight, lamplight), for the light of the eye, and for the divine nature in passages like 1 John 1:5 and John 8:12. The same functional vocabulary as Hebrew *or.*

**Logos** (*LOH-goss*) — Greek noun translated *Word.* Used in John 1:1 for the creative, sustaining Word through whom all things were made. In the opening of John's gospel, *logos* and *phos* are paired the way *God said* and *Let there be light* are paired in Genesis 1 — one act, seen from two angles.

**Electromagnetic field** — The invisible, pervasive structure of space that carries electric and magnetic interactions, and that hosts the traveling ripples we call light. In the Genesis Physics framework (*Foundations Vol 2 Ch 3*), the electromagnetic field is the simplest oscillation mode of the Firmament (the *raqia* of Genesis 1:6–8). Light is what the Firmament does when it ripples.

**Photon** — A single traveling ripple in the electromagnetic field. Carries energy from one place to another at the speed of light. Every glowing object — sun, candle, firefly, phone screen — is matter that is coupling to the field and emitting photons. The field hosts the photons; the photons are not the field.

**Cosmic microwave background (CMB)** — The oldest light in the universe. A low-level microwave glow filling all of space in every direction, emitted roughly 380,000 years after the beginning of the cosmos, when the early universe cooled enough for light to decouple from matter and stream freely for the first time. Predicted in the late 1940s; discovered by Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson at Bell Labs in 1964; mapped in increasing detail ever since. Observational evidence that the electromagnetic field was filled with radiation long before any star existed — which is, in its own vocabulary, exactly what Genesis 1:3 describes.

## Family Discussion Questions

1. *(For the youngest reader.)* Look around the room. Name one thing that glows. Now name one thing that does not glow. What would happen if every glowing thing stopped glowing — would the *ability* for light to be here go away too? Why not?

2. *(For elementary readers.)* This chapter says the Bible describes light on Day 1 and the sun, moon, and stars on Day 4. Draw a simple picture of the four days of creation in order: Day 1, Day 2, Day 3, Day 4. Put the light in a different color. Where does the sun belong in your picture?

3. *(For middle-school readers.)* The author compares the sun to a *lamp* and the electromagnetic field to the *wiring in the wall.* Can you think of one other pair of objects from everyday life where one object is the *capacity* and the other object is the *device that uses the capacity*? What does this comparison help you understand about Genesis 1?

4. *(For high-school readers.)* The chapter argues that when the Bible calls God *light* in 1 John 1:5 and John 8:12, it is not using a metaphor Christians invented after discovering electromagnetism. What evidence does the author give for that claim? Do you find it convincing? What would make it more convincing or less convincing?

5. *(For parents, over coffee.)* The chapter takes three common objections to Day 1 and answers them without dodging. Which of the three objections had you heard before? Which answer do you think your children will find the most helpful when they hear the objection themselves? Are there other objections to Day 1 you have run into that were not covered here?

## Family Activity: The Prism on a Sunny Afternoon

This chapter's central claim is that light is a capacity of the whole electromagnetic field, and that what we call *white light* from the sun is actually a mixture of many different wavelengths — many different notes, if we borrow the guitar-string picture from §3 — all of them ripples in the same underlying field. You can see this for yourself in about ten minutes, and it is one of the most memorable things a child will ever witness.

Here is the activity.

Get a clear prism. Any shape will do; an inexpensive acrylic prism from a hobby store or a craft section works perfectly, and a glass prism from an older science kit works even better if you have one. On a sunny day, find a room with a window where direct sunlight is streaming in. Hold the prism in the beam. Turn it slowly. Let the light pass through it and project onto a nearby wall or a piece of white paper.

A rainbow will appear.

The single beam of sunlight, which looked *white* to your eye, split into a spread of colors — red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. Each of those colors is a different wavelength of the electromagnetic field. The prism did not *make* the colors; the colors were already present in the sunlight, mixed together. The prism separated them so your eye could see them.

After you have watched the rainbow for a minute, ask your family this. *If the sun is the only source of light in this room, how did the prism know to make a rainbow? Where were the colors before the prism was there? Were they in the sun, or were they in the field the sun was ringing?*

You do not have to answer that question to finish the activity. You just have to watch the rainbow appear and feel the size of what you are watching. The sun is one kind of device. The electromagnetic field is larger than any device. And on a sunny Saturday, in a quiet room in your own house, a piece of clear plastic can show your children a slice of the Day 1 capacity — the one the Lord spoke into being before a single star existed to use it.

If you do not have a prism on the day you read this chapter, there is a second activity you can do instead — and I recommend you do it *in addition to* the prism on another afternoon. Open a concordance (a paper one at the back of a study Bible, or a free online concordance such as Blue Letter Bible at blueletterbible.org, or a Bible app like e-Sword or Logos). Search for every verse in the Old and New Testaments that uses *light* as a name or attribute of God Himself. Write every verse you find on a family whiteboard. Discuss them in the order they appear in Scripture.

Then ask your family this. *If the Bible calls God light from Genesis to Revelation, across roughly fifteen hundred years of different writers in different languages, writing to different audiences for different reasons — why do they all keep reaching for the same word?*

You do not need to answer that question either. You just need to see, with your own eyes, that the Bible is remarkably consistent about this. From Day 1 to the New Jerusalem, the vocabulary does not change.

## What Comes Next

Chapter 4 returns to the order of the creation days and picks up with Day 3 — *"And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so"* (Genesis 1:9, KJV). Day 3 is the day matter condenses out of the Waters Below into the forms you can touch — continents, oceans, mountains, and eventually, by the end of the day, the first living things. We will meet the Hebrew word for *gather* and we will see how modern physics describes the emergence of baryonic matter as a *phase transition* — which looks, in its own vocabulary, exactly like a gathering. Expect the same rhythm you just read: one Hebrew word studied closely; one "But What About…?" objection taken on the chin; one Family Activity; one Scripture Memory Verse; and one bridge to the next day.

You have the capacity now. You have the architecture. In the next chapter, things start to take shape.
