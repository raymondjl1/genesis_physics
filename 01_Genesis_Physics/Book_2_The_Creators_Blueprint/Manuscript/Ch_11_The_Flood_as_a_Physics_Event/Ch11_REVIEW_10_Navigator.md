## ARCHITECTURAL STRENGTHS

### Strength 1: The Sabbath-Boundary Extension

Chapter 10 established the Sabbath Boundary as the division between creation-mode and sustaining-mode physics. Chapter 11 extends this to show that sustaining mode is *not* perfectly uniform — it has been perturbed by named commanded acts (the Fall, the Flood, the Cross, the Consummation). This is sophisticated and necessary logic that prevents the objection "but Ch10 said the universe became stable at the Sabbath Boundary, so how can the Flood happen?"

Answer: Stability (sustaining mode is the rule) does not mean uniformity (perturbations at named moments are the exception). The cascade is clean:

**Ch8 (Lawgiver does not change) → Ch10 (Sabbath Boundary) → Ch11 (perturbations are exceptions within sustaining mode, not violations of it) → Ch12 (applies same principle to post-Flood history)**

### Strength 2: The "No Boundary Breach" Principle

The chapter correctly and repeatedly disavows the reading that would make the Flood a "tear in the firmament" (line 63: "The firmament — which Chapter 2 told us is the stretched membrane God made on Day 2 — is *not* described as torn. The text describes channels *opening* in it."). This is a critical distinction that prevents the framework from being accused of pantheism (God breaking His own creation) or of dismissing the firmament as unreal.

The Hebrew verb *baqa* ("broke open") is carefully distinguished from *shavar* (rupture/breaking in the sense of shattering). The framework reads it as "commanded opening," not "catastrophic failure." This semantic precision prevents a cascade of subsequent errors.
