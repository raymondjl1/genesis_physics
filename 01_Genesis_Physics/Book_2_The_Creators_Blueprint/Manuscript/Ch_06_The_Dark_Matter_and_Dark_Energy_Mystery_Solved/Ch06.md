## §1. The Afternoon My Wife Asked What 95 Percent Means

It was late autumn. The cottonwoods by the irrigation ditch had gone yellow, and the first hard freeze had dropped the south pasture to brown stubble. The cows had been fed. The mule had come over to the fence to see if I had anything in my hand and, finding nothing, had wandered off to stand in a patch of sun.

I was hanging gate wire when my wife came down the lane with a magazine folded under her arm. She leaned against the post next to me until I finished the loop.

*"Jeff,"* she said. *"This Smithsonian article says scientists don't know what ninety-five percent of the universe is made of. Is that for real?"*

I read the paragraph she had her finger on. A careful science writer was explaining that roughly 68 percent of the cosmos is *dark energy,* 27 percent is *dark matter,* and the remaining 5 percent is everything you and I can actually see — stars, planets, galaxies, the coffee in her cup, the cows in the back pasture, the two of us leaning on the fence.

I folded the magazine closed.

*"Yes,"* I said. *"The numbers are real. They have been measured six ways from Sunday, and they come out the same every time. Ninety-five percent of the cosmos is invisible to our instruments."*

*"So they really don't know what it is?"*

*"They know what it does,"* I said. *"They've been watching it do what it does since the nineteen-thirties. They just don't have names for it. They call it* dark *because they don't know what else to call it."*

She looked up at the sky the way a person does when she is thinking about something she cannot quite make fit. *"That feels like a pretty big gap in human knowledge."*

*"It is,"* I said. *"But sweetheart — the Bible already told us what it is. Three thousand years ago. On the second page."*

That is the chapter you are about to read. The 95 percent of the cosmos our textbooks can only label with placeholder words is the architecture God gave us in Genesis 1:2 and 1:6–7 — the *Waters Above* (dark energy, ~68% of the cosmic energy budget) and the *Waters Below* (dark matter, ~27%), with the Firmament — the visible five percent where we live — between. ## §2. The Numbers You Keep Hearing

Let me give you the modern physics picture first, in plain language. Then we will lay Scripture next to it.

When astronomers add up what *must* be pulling on everything in the visible cosmos, they find it is not enough. Galaxies spin too fast for the visible matter inside them. Galaxy clusters hold together more tightly than their stars can explain. The scale of the cosmos itself is expanding faster now than it did a few billion years ago, and no amount of visible matter can account for the acceleration. When astronomers total up *what must be there,* then subtract *what they can see,* three numbers come out — and five independent kinds of measurement agree to within a percent or two.

The numbers are roughly 68, 27, and 5.

Sixty-eight percent of the cosmic energy budget is *dark energy.* It is a pressure everywhere at once, pushing outward, accelerating the scale of space. It does not clump, does not absorb light, does not glow. It just pushes.

Twenty-seven percent is *dark matter.* An enormous amount of mass that does not emit, absorb, or interact with any electromagnetic instrument. You cannot photograph it directly — but it pulls. It pulls galaxies together, holds galaxy clusters in place, and structures the scaffolding that keeps the visible cosmos from flying apart.

Five percent — the thin sliver at the edge of the chart — is everything you have ever seen. Every star, every planet, every atom of every tree, every body of every dog, every coffee cup and every barn. Everything you have ever held in your hand or looked at through a telescope is inside that five percent.

## §4. Waters Above — The Outer Reservoir That Pushes

Let me take you through the two invisible layers one at a time. We start with the one above, because that is the one the Bible names first. In modern physics this layer is called *dark energy.*

The story of how it was found is worth telling. In the late nineteen-nineties, two teams of astronomers were racing each other to measure how fast the expansion of the cosmos was *slowing down.* Everyone assumed it had to be slowing down. Gravity pulls; an expanding cosmos full of galaxies pulling on each other should decelerate, like a ball thrown upward. The only question was *how much.* Two teams, different telescopes, different samples of supernovae, different software, different lead scientists. Both went looking for deceleration. Both came back with the same answer.

The expansion is not slowing down. It is *speeding up.*

Nobody had predicted it. Both teams checked each other and published within months in 1998. Something, everywhere at once, is pushing the scale of the cosmos outward, faster and faster. Not a force like gravity, which depends on mass and gets weaker with distance — a pressure, uniform, everywhere, getting *more* important as the cosmos gets bigger because there is more of it.

They called it *dark energy.* That placeholder word has been hanging there for a quarter of a century. Einstein had guessed at something like it in 1917, called it the *cosmological constant,* and later called it the biggest blunder of his career. The 1998 measurements revealed he was right the first time and wrong about being wrong.

Now read Genesis 1:7 in that light.

> *"And God made the expanse and separated the waters that were under the expanse from the waters that were above the expanse."*

*Waters above the expanse.* A reservoir above the Firmament that has been there from Day 2. The Waters Above are the repulsive reservoir that keeps the Firmament stretched. Think of a drumhead. The taut skin is the Firmament; the pressure outside keeps it stretched. Let the pressure go and the skin falls slack. Keep the pressure up and the skin stays pulled taut in every direction. That is what the Waters Above are doing right now — the outer reservoir pressing on the Firmament, accelerating every corner of the visible cosmos away from every other. Astronomers measured it in 1998 and have been measuring it ever since from at least five independent kinds of observation. The number is as real as your coffee cup.

And the Bible said it would be there. Not in equations. In architecture. *The waters that were above the expanse.* The Psalmist sang the same fact from the other side: *"Praise Him, you highest heavens, and you waters above the heavens!"* (Psalm 148:4). You can hear a man whose universe has room for a field above the sky he cannot see but knows is there. He calls it to praise. And we, three thousand years later, have measured what he was calling to.

One thing worth noticing now, before we turn to the Waters Below. The Waters Above are *uniform.* They push with the same pressure everywhere — in the voids between galaxy clusters, in the space between you and the wall of the room where you are reading this, at the edge of the observable universe. Dark energy does not cluster. It does not pile up where the galaxies are. It is the same outward pressure at every address in the cosmos, the way air pressure in a room pushes the same in every corner. That uniformity is precisely *why* dark energy pushes the entire scale of the cosmos outward, rather than holding particular things together. The next section shows how the Waters Below are the mirror image: they cluster in specific places, pull locally, and hold structure together. Two invisible reservoirs. Two utterly different behaviors. Both named by Moses on the same day.

## §5. Waters Below — The Inner Scaffold That Holds

The other invisible layer — the one beneath — was found first and has been accumulating evidence for ninety years.

It started in 1933, with a Swiss astronomer named Fritz Zwicky. Zwicky looked at the Coma cluster — a swarm of about a thousand galaxies bound together by gravity — and calculated how fast the individual galaxies were moving. The answer came back *way too fast.* If the cluster had only as much mass as its visible stars, the galaxies would long ago have flung themselves apart. They hadn't. Something was holding them together that he could not see. Zwicky called it *dunkle Materie* — dark matter. *Dark* did not mean *evil.* It meant *we cannot see it.* He named the thing by what it did: it had mass, and it did not shine.

For forty years Zwicky's observation sat on the edge of acceptance. Then in the nineteen-seventies, an American astronomer named Vera Rubin started measuring the rotation speeds of individual galaxies. Rubin expected stars near the edge of a spiral galaxy to orbit more slowly than stars near the center — the way outer planets orbit more slowly than inner ones when the mass is concentrated in the middle. The stars did not slow down. Every galaxy she looked at showed the same thing. The outer stars were moving as fast as the inner ones. They should have been flying off into intergalactic space. They weren't. Something with mass, something that did not glow, something enormously more plentiful than the visible stars, was holding them in.

The pattern Vera Rubin found is worth picturing precisely, because the *shape* of dark matter is as important as its presence. The visible part of a spiral galaxy — the luminous pinwheel of stars and gas — is concentrated in a flat disk, maybe a hundred thousand light-years across. But the Waters Below extend far beyond that disk, in a vast roughly-spherical cloud astronomers call a *halo.* The halo is not uniform. It is denser near the center, where the visible galaxy sits, and thins gradually outward — the way a hill is steeper near the peak than near the base. A star orbiting in the outer edge of a galaxy is not orbiting just the visible stars; it is orbiting *inside the whole dark-matter halo.* That halo, denser toward the center and fading outward, provides exactly the extra gravitational pull that keeps outer stars moving at the same speed as inner ones. The Waters Below do not just hold galaxies together. They hold them together in a specific shape that produces flat rotation curves — the same orbital speed all the way to the visible edge — galaxy after galaxy, the same answer every time.

By the nineteen-eighties the pattern was unmistakable. And then, in 2006, the clearest single piece of evidence ever recorded was captured by the Chandra and Hubble telescopes — the Bullet Cluster.

Two galaxy clusters had collided and passed through each other in the deep past. When clusters collide, the visible hot gas drags against itself and slows down. You can see it sitting between the two clusters, glowing pink in x-ray images. But the *gravity* of each cluster did not stay where the gas was. Mapped by watching how light from background galaxies bent around the clusters — a technique called gravitational lensing — the gravity streamed right past the collision and ended up *offset* from the hot gas on either side.

The visible matter was slowed. The invisible matter went straight through. That is not what a fudge factor does. A fudge factor is a number you add to a formula; it does not show up on a photograph, in the wrong place, offset from the visible gas by tens of thousands of light-years. What shows up on a photograph, in the wrong place, is a real thing that got photographed.

Now read Genesis 1:7 one more time. *"And God made the expanse and separated the waters that were under the expanse from the waters that were above the expanse."* A reservoir beneath the Firmament, there from Day 2. The Waters Below are the scaffolding beneath the membrane — the inner field that binds galaxies, holds clusters, and structures what Ezekiel saw as *"terrible crystal"* and the astronomers now call the *cosmic web.* It is the Waters Below that kept the Coma cluster together when Zwicky looked at it, that held the outer stars of Vera Rubin's galaxies in orbit, and that went straight through the Bullet Cluster collision in 2006 while the visible gas was snagged in the middle.

The Bible said it would be there. The physicists have been measuring it for ninety years. *Same field. Two vocabularies.*

## §6. Why 68, 27, and 5

You may have been holding a question since you looked at the chart in §2. Why those numbers? Why two-thirds dark energy, a quarter dark matter, a sliver of visible stuff? Is that how it happens to come out, or is there a reason? There is a reason, and it comes from the architecture.

Think of a house. A huge attic — the whole top half, open to the rafters. A smaller basement, maybe a quarter of the vertical extent. And one thin main floor between them, where you actually live — kitchen, bedrooms, the dog bed by the door. Most of the house is in the attic. Next most is in the basement. The main floor is the smallest slice — but the main floor is where the life happens.

Now look at the chart again. Two-thirds in the Waters Above. A quarter in the Waters Below. A thin sliver for the Firmament where life lives. The architecture of the cosmos is the architecture of that house. You do not live in the attic and you do not live in the basement; you live on the main floor. But the attic and the basement are there, and they are most of the house, and the main floor is thin *because that is what a main floor is.*

I am not saying the cosmos is literally a house. I am saying that when God builds a cosmos in which two great reservoirs sit on either side of a thin stretched membrane, and He fills the reservoirs with most of the substance and makes the membrane thin by design, the *fractions* come out looking like a large slice, a medium slice, and a sliver. Big reservoir up top, smaller reservoir below, thin membrane between. The specification produces the numbers.

Engineers call this *dimensional reasoning.* You do not have to solve the full equations to see that a system with one big region, one smaller region, and a thin surface between them will always have most of its mass-energy in the big region, next-most in the smaller region, and least on the thin surface. That is a geometric fact. It has been one since the second page of the Bible. The 68/27/5 split is not a coincidence; it is a consequence of the floor plan.

I want to be honest about what I do not know. The full mathematical treatment — the exact reason the dark-energy reservoir is larger than the dark-matter reservoir by the ratio it is, the fine geometry that fixes 68 and 27 to the decimal — lives in *Foundations Vol 5 Ch 11* and the Foundations Series (in preparation), and some of those answers are still being refined. The Bible gives the architecture. The physicists are filling in the details. So far, every time a detail has come in, it has fit.

## §7. "But What About…?" — Three Questions

I promised in Chapter 1 that I would not dodge the hard questions. This chapter has three waiting.

**First. "If all this is in the Bible, why haven't the scientists figured it out already?"**

Two moves. First, scientists *have* figured out the measurements. They have been watching the two invisible reservoirs for ninety years — Zwicky in 1933, Rubin in the 1970s, the Bullet Cluster in 2006, the two supernova teams in 1998. The physicists are not wrong about what is out there. They are right. What they lack is the *name.*

Second, why they have not landed on the name. Most physicists are not looking at the book in which the names are written. They have been hunting a *particle* to give dark matter a name — with detectors underground, accelerators in Switzerland, experiments in Antarctica — for forty years, with nothing to show. They have been arguing for a *reason* behind dark energy for a quarter of a century with no candidate winning. The strategy has been to find the dark sector in the particle zoo of the Standard Model. It is not there. It is two *fields,* not two particles — an architecture, not a bestiary — and the architecture is in Genesis 1:6–7. Most physicists were never told to look there, and our culture for two centuries has told them they would find it foolish if they did. That is not their fault. Most of them are honest people wrestling with hard data. I have worked with them my whole career and I respect them. What I am asking you to see is that the answer they are reaching for is the answer Moses wrote down on page two, and nobody told them to look.

**Second. "Is it fair to say the Bible 'predicted' dark matter and dark energy?"**

This is the question the skeptical uncle will ask at Thanksgiving. Here is the careful answer.

If by *predict* you mean *the Bible had an equation with a decimal value* — no. The Bible did not have equations. It did not name the ratio 68 to 27 to 5. That is not what prophets do.

If by *predict* you mean *the Bible described the architecture and the observations later confirmed it* — yes. In any other field of engineering we call that a prediction. When a bridge designer draws a specification in which two massive foundations sit on either side of a thin deck, and an engineer measures the finished bridge decades later and finds most of the weight in the foundations and the deck indeed thin, we do not say the designer got lucky. We say the design produced the measurement. That is the meaning of *prediction* in engineering. The Bible gave the architecture — three layers, waters above, Firmament, waters below. The physicists discovered what the architecture requires — accelerating expansion, galaxies bound by the invisible, most of the mass-energy in the two invisible sectors. The design was written first. The measurements came later. They fit.

**Third. "Isn't the cosmological constant just a fudge factor?"**

It *was* one when Einstein first wrote it in 1917 to make his equations yield a static universe. He later called it his greatest blunder. For seventy years the term sat unwanted in the corner of physics.

Then in 1998, two independent teams of astronomers — *looking for deceleration,* not for a cosmological constant — came back with numbers that could only be explained by a cosmological constant of a very specific value. Since then, four other kinds of measurements — the cosmic microwave background, galaxy surveys, baryon acoustic oscillations, and the bending of light around large-scale structure — have confirmed the same value to within a few percent.

Five independent methods. Same answer. A fudge factor makes one observation come out right. Five independent observations, five different techniques, thirty years, converging on the same number — that is what a *feature of reality* looks like. The cosmological constant stopped being a fudge the day those 1998 papers were published. That hardened, measured number is the energy density of the Waters Above. It is Psalm 148:4 as a number.

## Key Terms

**Dark Energy** *(physics term)* — The invisible field exerting a repulsive pressure that accelerates the expansion of space. Discovered observationally in 1998. Roughly 68% of the cosmic energy budget. Corresponds to the Waters Above.

**Dark Matter** *(physics term)* — The invisible mass that holds galaxies and galaxy clusters together by its gravitational pull. Inferred from the 1930s, confirmed through the 1970s, photographed indirectly in the 2006 Bullet Cluster observation. Roughly 27% of the cosmic energy budget. Corresponds to the Waters Below.

**Waters Above** — The upper *mayim* field named on Day 2 (Genesis 1:7). The repulsive reservoir above the Firmament. Canonical zone label: **Z 2.2.3.** The physicist's name for it is *dark energy.*

**Waters Below** — The lower *mayim* field named on Day 2. The structural scaffold beneath the Firmament. Canonical zone label: **Z 2.2.1.** The physicist's name for it is *dark matter.*

**Firmament** — The stretched expanse between the two Waters fields, where all visible matter lives. Canonical zone label: **Z 2.2.2.** Modern physics calls it the *observable universe.*

**Cosmic energy budget** *(physics term)* — The total energy content of the cosmos, broken down into what fraction is in each component. Multiple independent methods converge on roughly 5% visible matter, 27% dark matter, 68% dark energy.

**Cosmological constant** *(physics term)* — The measured energy density of dark energy. Written down by Einstein in 1917, abandoned for most of the twentieth century, reintroduced after the 1998 supernova measurements. In this framework, the cosmological constant *is* the energy density of the Waters Above.

**Bullet Cluster** *(physics term)* — A pair of galaxy clusters photographed in 2006 that had recently collided and passed through each other. The visible hot gas was slowed by the collision; the dark matter kept going. The clearest single image of the Waters Below in action.

**Open system** — A system that exchanges energy and structure with something beyond itself. The biblical cosmos is an open system, sustained by Christ (Colossians 1:17). Contrasted with the *closed-system* assumption that, by itself, cannot account for the 95 percent.

**Mayim** (*MAH-yeem*) — Hebrew for *waters.* Always plural in Hebrew grammar. The broad word for *fluid, formless material, primordial substance* divided on Day 2 into the Waters Above and the Waters Below.

## Family Discussion Questions

1. *(Youngest.)* Most of the universe is invisible — only a tiny slice of everything is the stuff we can see. Can you name three invisible things that are real anyway? (Hint: the air, the wind, the love your mom has for you.)
