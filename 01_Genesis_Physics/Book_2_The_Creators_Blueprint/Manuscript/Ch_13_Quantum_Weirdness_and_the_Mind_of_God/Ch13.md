## §3. By Him All Things Consist

There is a third passage that adds a piece neither of the first two has by itself.

> *"Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature: for by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: and he is before all things, and by him all things consist."*
> — Colossians 1:15–17, KJV

Read the last clause again. *By him all things consist.*

The Greek on *consist* is συνέστηκεν — a perfect-tense form of *synistemi.* The verb means *to hold together, to cause to cohere, to compose into a single whole.* The perfect tense in Greek is the tense of *completed action with continuing result.* So Paul is saying: by Christ, all things were composed-into-a-whole, and they remain composed-into-a-whole *because of him.*

If Hebrews 1:3 names the *upholding* — the active load-bearing — Colossians 1:17 names the *cohering* — the structural integrity. *Pheron* says the load is being borne. *Synistemi* says the parts are holding together as one.

Three Greek tenses have now passed in front of us in two verses, and they are easy to blur together on a first read. Here they are side by side, so you can hold them apart:

| Greek tense | Where it appears | What it means |
|-------------|------------------|---------------|
| *Aorist* (one-time, completed past) | *"when he had... purged our sins"* (Heb 1:3) | A finished act. Done once. The purging is complete. |
| *Present participle* (ongoing, right now) | *pheron* — *"upholding all things"* (Heb 1:3) | A continuing act. Happening this second. The bearing has not stopped. |
| *Perfect* (completed, with a result that still stands) | *synistemi* — *"all things consist"* (Col 1:17) | A past act whose result holds now. Composed once; still holding together. |

The purging is *done.* The upholding is *ongoing.* The cohering is *done-and-still-holding.* Keep those three apart and the rest of the chapter reads cleanly.

Why does this matter for a chapter on quantum mechanics?

Because the strangeness physics has been wrestling with for a hundred years is, in its deepest form, a question about *cohering.* What holds an electron, mid-flight, as a *single thing* when it can interfere with itself like a wave through two slits? Those are good and useful names. They describe the phenomenon. They do not, by themselves, name the *source.* The Greek of Colossians 1:17 names the source.

I want to be careful about how strong a claim this is. I am *not* saying physics has discovered that *synistemi* is a force in its equations. I am saying physics has been describing, in its own technical vocabulary, the same architectural feature scripture named two thousand years ago. *Coherence is real. The coherence has a source.*

The Bible names the Son as the one upholding (Hebrews 1:3) and as the one in whom all things consist (Colossians 1:17), but it does not isolate the Son from the Father and the Spirit in the work of creation or sustaining. ## §5. The Double Slit — Wave on the Way, Particle on the Readout

*A note for the reader before we start. The next three sections (§5, §6, §7) are the most technical pages in this book — three famous quantum experiments, walked through carefully. If the physics gets too thick for you or for a younger listener, you can skim them and jump straight to §8, "One Sustaining at Every Scale." That section carries the chapter's thesis, and it stands on its own without the experiment details. Nobody loses the heart of the chapter by skipping ahead. But if you want to see the strangeness for yourself first, read on.*

The most famous experiment in quantum mechanics is the *double-slit experiment.* Let me describe it in family register, then give the framework's reading.

Imagine a wave on a calm pond. You drop a stone in the middle and concentric ripples spread out. Place a board with two narrow slits in it between the stone and the far shore. The wave hits the board. Two thin slices pass through the slits. On the far side those slices spread out, overlap, and reach the far shore as a *superposition* — physicists' word for *two waves added together at the same place.* Where two crests meet, the water rises higher. Where a crest meets a trough, the water flattens out. The far shore receives a *striped pattern* of high and low water — what physicists call an *interference pattern.* Every kid who has watched a stone hit a pond has seen this. It is wave physics. It is no surprise.

Now do the experiment with *electrons.*

Fire individual electrons, one at a time, at a barrier with two narrow slits in it. On the far side, place a detector that records where each electron arrives. Run the experiment for hours, until you have a million electron arrivals on the screen. Look at the pattern.

The pattern is *the striped interference pattern from the pond.* Even when only one electron is fired at a time. Even when the gap between electrons is so long that no two electrons could possibly be talking to each other. The single electrons, accumulating one by one, arrive *as if* each electron is a wave passing through both slits at once and interfering with itself.

That is the famous result. It has been replicated thousands of times across photons, electrons, neutrons, atoms, and large molecules with tens of atoms in them. The result is iron-clad. *On the way through, the electron behaves like a wave. On the readout, it behaves like a particle — a single dot on the screen.* This is what physicists call *wave–particle duality,* and the secular interpretive community has been arguing for a century about what it *means.*

The framework's reading is direct, and it does not require a paradox.

In Chapter 7 we said matter is *standing-wave excitations of the Firmament.* The electron is *a wave on a medium* — the medium being the Firmament the architecture set up on Day Two. So when the electron approaches the slits, *what is approaching is a wave.* It samples both slits because that is what waves do — they extend through whatever they encounter. On the far side, the wave continues to propagate, the two new wave-pieces interfere just as they did on the pond, and the resulting interference pattern describes *where the next readout is more likely to land.* The *readout* — the detector clicking — localizes the wave at one point. The wave was always there. The localization is a feature of how a detector samples a Firmament excitation.

*Wave on the way, particle on the readout.* The duality is not a paradox. It is what you would expect if matter is what scripture's architecture says it is.

The framework's reading of the wave function — that the wave function is the local pattern of the Firmament's standing-wave configuration at that location — is *MODERATE confidence on the precise mathematical mapping* (the full development is in *Foundations Volume 4, Chapter 2,* and it is research-stage); *HIGH confidence on the interpretive coherence with the architecture.* The standard physics community has not converged on what the wave function *is.* Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot-wave, QBism — five or six interpretations are all alive. The framework's reading deserves a seat at that table.

For a teenager who comes home and asks *Mom, what is an electron?* the right answer is now in your hand. *An electron is a standing-wave excitation of the Firmament God stretched out on Day Two. On the way through an experiment, it behaves the way a wave behaves. At a detector, the readout localizes it. The strangeness is the strangeness of waves on a real medium, not a paradox at the heart of reality.*

## §6. Uncertainty — A Trade-Off, Not a Limitation

The second famous strangeness is the *uncertainty principle,* named for Werner Heisenberg, who described it in 1927. The principle says, roughly: you cannot simultaneously pin down both the *position* of a particle and its *momentum* (its mass times its velocity) to arbitrary precision. The more precisely you know one, the less precisely you can know the other. The relation has a famous symbolic shorthand involving Planck's constant divided by two, which you have probably seen on a coffee mug.

The most common popular-science misreading of this principle is that *we just don't know yet, and someday with better instruments we will know.* That misreading is wrong. The principle is not about instruments. It is about the *underlying geometry* of what a wave is. Let me give you the analogy.

Pick up a guitar.

Pluck a single string sharply, very briefly — almost a percussive *thwack.* You can pin down very precisely *when* the note happened — a definite moment in time — but the *pitch* of the note is fuzzy. A short pluck spreads its energy across many frequencies; the listener hears more of a *click* than a clean tone. Short time; wide pitch.

Now bow the string slowly with a violin bow, sustaining the tone for several seconds. The pitch is exquisitely precise — a clear, ringing tone you could tune to. But *when* the note happened is fuzzy: it was happening across the whole sustained interval. Long time; narrow pitch.

You cannot have both. You cannot have a sound that has *both* an exact moment of origin *and* an exact pitch, because those two descriptions are different mathematical aspects of the same wave, and the geometry of waves requires the trade-off. *It is a property of how waves work.*

Read the uncertainty principle through that analogy. *Position* and *momentum* in quantum mechanics are exactly like *time* and *frequency* for the guitar string — different descriptions of the same underlying wave, and the geometry of standing waves on the Firmament requires a trade-off between them. Pin position; get fuzzy momentum. Pin momentum; get fuzzy position. The limit is not in the instrument. It is in the geometry.

The framework's reading is just this: *of course there is a trade-off.* Position and momentum are aspects of a standing-wave configuration on a real medium. Standing waves have lengths and frequencies, and lengths and frequencies are linked by the same kind of mathematical inevitability that links time and frequency for the guitar.

This matters pastorally. A teenager who tells you *physics has discovered that nothing can be known precisely* is misreading the principle. *Many things can be known with extraordinary precision.* The atomic clock at the National Institute of Standards and Technology is precise to roughly one part in ten to the eighteenth. That is enough precision to detect altitude differences of less than a foot through general-relativistic time dilation. The world is not foggy. It has a *specific structure,* and one feature of that structure is that certain pairs of measurements trade off against each other.

Confidence: *HIGH* on the geometric (not instrumental) character of the uncertainty principle. *MODERATE* on the framework's specific reading — that the geometry in question is the standing-wave geometry of the Firmament; the precise mathematical mapping is in *Foundations Volume 4, Chapter 2* and is research-stage.

The buzz on the oscilloscope at two A.M. is the small-scale member of a family of irreducible structural facts. It is real. It is not the absence of knowledge. It is the *shape* of what is there.

## §7. Entanglement — One Wave, Two Readouts

The third famous strangeness is *quantum entanglement,* and it is the one popular-science writing has done the most damage with.

Take a pair of particles created together in a single quantum process — say, two photons emitted by a single atom in a particular kind of decay. The two photons share what physicists call a *single quantum state.* Some property of the pair is correlated: if one is measured *up,* the other will be measured *down,* in a strict mathematical sense. The pair is *entangled.*

Now separate the two photons. Send one to a detector at one end of the laboratory. Send the other to a detector at the opposite end. The detectors can be a few meters apart. They can be a few hundred kilometers apart in some recent experiments. Measure both at almost the same instant. The correlation holds. If you set the detectors a particular way and measure many pairs, you get exactly the correlations a single shared quantum state predicts, even though no signal could have traveled from one detector to the other in the time between the measurements.

Einstein hated this. He called it *spooky action at a distance.* He was sure there had to be a *hidden variable* — some classical property each photon carried in its luggage, like a coin showing heads on one side and tails on the other, that determined the outcomes locally without any communication after separation. In 1964, John Bell wrote down a mathematical inequality that any *local hidden-variable* theory would have to satisfy, and he showed that quantum mechanics predicted the inequality would be *violated.* This turned a philosophical disagreement into a measurable experimental question.

Starting in the 1970s and 80s, three experimentalists — Alain Aspect, John Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger — ran versions of the experiment that successively closed the loopholes Bell's analysis had left open. Each experiment showed Bell's inequality violated, in line with quantum mechanics, against any local hidden-variable account. In 2022, the three of them shared the Nobel Prize in Physics for this work. Entanglement is real. The correlations between widely separated entangled particles cannot be explained by anything local each particle is carrying with it.

What the secular interpretive community has done with this is what they always do — argued. The framework's reading is direct.

In Chapter 7 we said matter is standing-wave excitations of the Firmament. Two entangled particles share *a single underlying excitation pattern* on the Firmament. They are not two separate things that exchanged a hidden variable at their birth and then forgot to talk. They are *two readout points sampling one excitation.* When you measure at point A, you are not sending a signal to point B; you are reading out an excitation that has *both* A and B as its endpoints. The correlations between the readouts are correlations between two ends of the same underlying wave.

Picture two children at opposite ends of a long taut rope. Wiggle the rope at the middle. Both ends move in coordinated ways, instantly. Not because one end signaled the other across the room — because *both ends are part of the same rope.* That is the framework's reading of entanglement, in family register.

This reading does not require any new physics. It does not require faster-than-light signaling (and indeed, the no-signaling theorems of standard quantum mechanics are honored — you cannot transmit information through entanglement). It just requires that what looks, in our four-dimensional projection, like *two separated particles* is, in the framework's six-dimensional architecture, *one excitation with two readout points.* Entanglement stops being spooky. It becomes geometry.

## §9. "But What About…?" — Three Questions

Three questions families ask.

**But does the framework say God plays dice?**

Einstein famously said *God does not play dice with the universe,* objecting to what he saw as the irreducibly *probabilistic* character of quantum mechanics. The Bohr / Heisenberg / Copenhagen camp answered, more or less, *He plays dice; that's just how the world is.* The argument has been going on for a century.

The framework's answer has two parts.

The *first* part: at the deepest level, the framework is *deterministic.* The sustaining coupling is a definite field. The Waters fields obey definite equations. *Foundations Volume 4* lays out the mathematics, and at the deepest level it is not random. The Maker is not throwing dice in his study. The *second* part: at the level where we actually live — where we measure outcomes, make decisions, and answer for our choices — the architecture is *genuinely open.* Not because God is uncertain. ## Key Terms

- **Wave function** — the mathematical description of a quantum particle's state; in the framework, the local pattern of the Firmament's standing-wave configuration at that location.
- **Double-slit experiment** — the experiment, replicated thousands of times, that shows individual particles producing wave-style interference patterns even when fired one at a time.
- **Uncertainty principle** — Heisenberg's 1927 result that certain pairs of measurements (notably position and momentum) cannot both be pinned down to arbitrary precision; the limit is geometric, not instrumental.
- **Quantum entanglement** — the phenomenon in which two or more particles share a single quantum state such that measurements at one location are correlated with measurements at another, in ways that cannot be reproduced by any local hidden-variable theory.
- **Bell inequality** — a mathematical inequality, derived by John Bell in 1964, that any local hidden-variable theory must satisfy; quantum mechanics predicts the inequality will be violated, and the violation has been measured.
- **Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger experiments** — the loophole-closing entanglement experiments, run from the 1970s through the 2010s, that confirmed Bell-inequality violations and ruled out local hidden-variable theories; recognized with the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- **Sustaining coupling** — the framework's name for the field that carries the active sustaining of the architecture, formalizing what Hebrews 1:3 names as *upholding* and Colossians 1:17 names as *cohering;* developed in detail in *Foundations Volume 4, Chapter 1.*
- **Pheron (φέρων)** — Greek present-tense participle of *phero,* "to bear, carry, uphold continuously"; used in Hebrews 1:3 of Christ's continuous active sustaining of all things.
- **Synistemi (συνίστημι)** — Greek verb "to hold together, to cause to cohere"; in Colossians 1:17 the perfect tense names a completed action with continuing result — Christ as the structural cohering of all things.
- **En autō (ἐν αὐτῷ)** — Greek "in him," locative dative; used in Acts 17:28 of every creature's existence within the sustained architecture.
- **Yasad (יָסַד)** — Hebrew verb "to lay a foundation, to establish"; used in Job 38:4 of the LORD's founding of the architecture of the earth.
- **Choq (חק)** — Hebrew noun "ordinance, decree, prescribed limit"; used in Job 38:33 of the laws of the heavens.
- **Mystery vs. fog** — the distinction between *real-but-partial knowing* of a real architecture (mystery) and *the claim that nothing can be known* (fog); 1 Corinthians 13:12's bronze mirror is the right image for the first.

## Family Activity — Reading Hebrews 1:1–3 Together

This chapter's activity is a *reading and a conversation,* not a hands-on demonstration. That is intentional. The chapter is about the smallest scale of physical reality, where there is nothing useful for a family to *do with their hands* — but plenty to *think about and talk about together.*

Set aside thirty minutes. Light a candle on the kitchen table. Have a Bible open to Hebrews 1, and have Hebrews 1:1–3 marked. Have a notebook or sheet of paper for each member of the family. Keep phones away from the table.

**Step One — Read Hebrews 1:1–3 aloud, slowly, twice.** The first time, read it as one continuous passage. The second time, read it again, and emphasize verse 3: *upholding all things by the word of his power.* Have one reader the first time, a different reader the second time.

**Step Two — Pause for one minute of silence.** Let the verse sit. Do not rush.

**Step Three — Use these prompts in turn.** Each member of the family answers each one — even the youngest gets a turn. Listen well. The point is not to get *the right answer.* The point is for the family to think together.

> *"What does it mean for God to* uphold all things*?"*

> *"What if he stopped, even for one second?"*

> *"What is the smallest thing you can think of that is being held together right now by God's upholding?"*

> *"What is the largest thing you can think of that is being held together right now by God's upholding?"*

> *"How does it change the way you feel about today, knowing the answer to the last two questions has the same Person on both ends?"*

**Step Four — Two minutes of silent journaling.** Each family member writes one sentence — just one — describing the most important thing they heard from the conversation. Younger family members can draw a picture instead.

**Step Five — Close by reading Hebrews 1:1–3 one more time.** Then the Lord's Prayer or a brief family prayer. Blow out the candle. Done.

This activity does not need a science demonstration. The chapter has been the demonstration. The activity is the family putting language around what they have just read, and that language is what the kid will reach for the next time he hears something on a YouTube video about quantum mechanics, or about the universe, or about whether God is needed at all.
