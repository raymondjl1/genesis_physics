## 1. The Question on the Back Porch

It is eleven at night in late September, and my wife's cousin is sitting in the chair on my right.

The dogs are asleep at our feet. The cows are a dark line against a darker pasture. The sky is the kind you only get on a farm with the house lights off — one of those nights where the Milky Way is not an idea but an actual smear of salt across the black.

We have been looking for Andromeda. It is a soft oval smudge just above and to the right of Cassiopeia, and tonight we can see it with naked eyes. My wife's cousin has never seen it before. I am telling him this smudge is a whole other galaxy — a trillion stars in a spiral the size of ours, about two and a half million light-years away.

He lets that sit for a second. Then he asks the question.

"Okay. So if it takes light two and a half million years to get here from that thing — and you're telling me the universe is only six thousand years old — then how am I seeing it?"

This is the question. Not *a* question. *The* question. Every honest reader of Genesis who has ever taken an astronomy class has sat with this one. Every parent whose child came home with a textbook that said 13.8 billion years has felt its weight. Every believer who loves both the Bible and the night sky has wondered whether the two were going to shake hands or whether someone, somewhere, was going to have to fudge.

I want to give you, in this chapter, the answer I gave my wife's cousin that night. Not a slogan. Not a dodge. An engineering-honest answer that does not require me to lie about what the Bible says, and does not require me to lie about what the light is doing.

The short version is this: the question assumes something that was not true during the first week. It assumes the fabric the light was traveling through was the same size the whole time. It was not. Genesis, and seventeen other passages after it, describes the heavens as something that was **stretched out** — a membrane the Maker beat thin. If the road the light is traveling on is itself growing fast enough in the early days, the light can arrive here on time, without breaking any physics we know about, and without requiring God to plant fake starlight He never actually sent.

Two guardrails before we start. I am not going to tell you "God just made the light already on its way." Some sincere Christians have taught it. It is the wrong answer, and in section 7 I will tell you exactly why. And I am not going to tell you "creationism wins, secular cosmology loses." That framing is a trap. The question itself was malformed. When you ask it rightly, honest physics gives an honest answer, and nobody has to lose.

Let us go.

## 2. Why the Two Easy Answers Are Not Enough

Before I tell you the answer I believe is right, I want to tell you why the two most common answers are wrong. Both were offered in good faith by Christians trying to defend Scripture. But neither holds up under the same rigor we would apply to any other engineering problem.

### The first easy answer: "God made the light already on its way."

You have heard this one. When God made the stars on Day 4, He also made a stream of photons already in flight between the stars and Earth. The photons arriving tonight from Andromeda were never actually emitted from Andromeda. They were created in transit, so that Adam could look up on Day 6 and see a full sky.

It is attractive. It is quick. It preserves a young cosmos without requiring anybody to do any physics.

But what we see from Andromeda is not just *light*. We see supernovas. We see stars being shredded by its central black hole. We see, in galaxies even farther away, collisions between whole galaxies with debris trails hundreds of thousands of light-years long. We see gravitational lensing — the light from one galaxy bending around a closer one in precise geometric shapes we can measure.

If the light was fabricated in transit, then those events never happened. They are a movie on the sky with no actors behind it. The supernova we saw in 1987 in the Large Magellanic Cloud would be a fiction. The neutrinos we detected from it — on Earth, in real detectors, three hours before the visible light arrived — would be a fiction. The entire history written in the light would be a history the Maker made up.

Scripture tells us God does not do that.

> *"God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?"*
> — Numbers 23:19, KJV

A Maker who paints a fake history across the sky — supernovas that never happened, collisions that never happened, lensing patterns for masses that were never there — is being deceptive about the events of His cosmos. The God of Scripture is not like that. He can make things mature, as we will see in section 5, but mature is not the same as *falsified*. A tree with rings is not a lie; a tree with rings of fires that never burned is.

The "light in transit" answer runs headlong into the character of God. I cannot use it.

### The second easy answer: "The speed of light used to be faster."

This one is called *c-decay*, and it had serious Christian defenders in the 1980s — most notably the Australian physicist Barry Setterfield. The idea: light was once enormously faster and has been slowing ever since. If the creation-week photons raced at a billion times *c*, distant starlight fits.

Two problems.

Empirically, *c* is tied by relativity and quantum electrodynamics to essentially everything else — the mass of the electron, the strength of the electromagnetic force, atomic binding energies. If *c* were dropping by even a tiny fraction, every atom would silently rewrite its chemistry as it went. Stars would brighten and dim. Nuclear decay rates would shift. The cosmic microwave background would have a different spectrum. None of this is observed. As Chapter 9 laid out, the fabric's speed constant is what it is, all the way to the edge of what we can see.

Internally, Chapter 8 spent a whole chapter on the fact that the Lawgiver does not change. If I turn around two chapters later and say the most famous constant in physics used to be a million times different, I have pulled the rug out from under my own book.

So: *c* is constant. Both easy answers are out.

What is left is the thing Scripture itself keeps pointing at, and modern cosmology keeps independently confirming. The *fabric* is not constant. The speed of light through the fabric is constant; the size of the fabric is not.

The road was growing under the light.

## 3. What the Hebrew Word Actually Means

To say that with a straight face, we have to go back to Genesis 1 and look at what the text actually says.

On Day 2, God makes something called a *raqia*.

> *"And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters."*
> — Genesis 1:6, KJV

The English word *firmament* comes from the Latin *firmamentum*, from *firmare*, to make firm. When Jerome translated the Hebrew Bible in the fourth century, he reached for *firmamentum* because Greek translators before him had used *stereōma*, meaning a solid or hardened thing. Those translations gave many later readers the impression that the firmament was a hard dome.

That is not what the Hebrew word means.

The Hebrew word is **רָקִיעַ** — *raqia*. It comes from the verb **רָקַע** — *raqa* — meaning *to beat out thin, to hammer flat, to stretch by pounding*. If you have ever watched a goldsmith take a nugget of gold and work it under a hammer until it becomes a shimmering leaf a hundred times wider than it started, you have seen *raqa* happen.

This is not my creative interpretation. The verb is used elsewhere in Scripture for exactly that kind of work.

> *"And they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in the blue, and in the purple, and in the scarlet, and in the fine linen, with cunning work."*
> — Exodus 39:3, KJV

That is the tabernacle craftsmen, beating gold thin for thread. *Raqa*.

> *"And Eleazar the priest took the brasen censers, wherewith they that were burned had offered; and they were made broad plates for a covering of the altar."*
> — Numbers 16:39, KJV

Bronze censers hammered flat into a plating. Same verb.

So when Genesis 1 says God made a *raqia*, the root picture is not a dome. It is *a thing that was beaten thin and stretched out*. The firmament is a membrane worked by the Maker's hand — taken from something thicker, hammered into a wide, thin expanse between the waters.

Once you have seen *raqa* in Genesis 1, you start to find the same picture — God stretching out the heavens — everywhere.

> *"I have made the earth, and created man upon it: I, even my hands, have stretched out the heavens, and all their host have I commanded."*
> — Isaiah 45:12, KJV

> *"It is he that sitteth upon the circle of the earth, and the inhabitants thereof are as grasshoppers; that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in."*
> — Isaiah 40:22, KJV

> *"Thus saith God the LORD, he that created the heavens, and stretched them out; he that spread forth the earth, and that which cometh out of it..."*
> — Isaiah 42:5, KJV

Seventeen different Old Testament passages describe the heavens as stretched out. Isaiah alone uses the phrase five times. Jeremiah uses it. Zechariah uses it. Job uses it. The Psalms use it. This is not a footnote. It is a recurring, insistent, central image. God made the heavens by **stretching them**.

That ought to stop us. Until the twentieth century, Western cosmology did not have this picture. Aristotle did not. Ptolemy did not. Newton did not. Einstein did not until 1915, and even then he added a fudge factor (the cosmological constant) specifically to keep the universe from stretching, because he could not imagine a cosmos that did. Then in 1929, Edwin Hubble pointed a telescope at distant galaxies and found them all moving away from us at speeds proportional to their distance. The fabric was stretching. Einstein called his fudge factor his "greatest blunder" and accepted the data.

Cosmology caught up to Isaiah in 1929. That took a while.

### Two Hebrew verbs, not one

One more detail in the Hebrew, because it is load-bearing. The Old Testament actually uses **two** verbs for the stretching heavens. One is *raqa* — the verb behind *raqia* — which has the sense of *beat out, hammer thin*. The other is *natah*, meaning *to stretch, to extend, to pitch a tent*.

More importantly, Hebrew grammar uses two different tenses in different verses. Some verses use the *perfect* tense — a completed action. Others use the *participle* — an ongoing action. Read them side by side and a picture emerges.

The *perfect* tense verses — Isaiah 42:5, Isaiah 44:24, Isaiah 45:12 — describe the stretching as **done**. Completed. Past.

The *participle* verses — Isaiah 40:22, Jeremiah 10:12, Zechariah 12:1 — describe it as **ongoing**. Present.

Scripture distinguishes a completed stretching event from an ongoing slow stretch. Creation week and today. Modern cosmology, with no knowledge of the grammar, has independently arrived at the same two-phase picture: an early period of extraordinarily rapid expansion (they call it *inflation*) followed by the much slower ongoing expansion we measure with Hubble's law. Scripture described it three thousand years before anybody pointed a telescope at the sky.

I do not think that is a coincidence. I think that is the Author telling us, in advance, how He built the room we are sitting in.

## 4. What the Firmament Was Doing While the Light Was Traveling

Now we put the pieces together.

On Day 2, God made the *raqia* — the membrane between the waters. On Day 4, He placed the sun, the moon, and the stars in it:

> *"And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth."*
> — Genesis 1:17, KJV

The stars are placed *in* the *raqia* — in the membrane that was just beaten thin and stretched wide. They are not behind it or above it. They are embedded in it, the way raisins are embedded in a loaf as it rises.

Picture a ball of raisin-bread dough, each raisin a galaxy. Put it in the oven. The dough rises. Every raisin moves away from every other raisin — not because the raisins are crawling through the dough but because the dough between them is getting bigger. The farther apart two raisins started, the faster they separate, because there is more dough between them to expand.

Now imagine the bread rising very fast for a short while, then slowing to a gentle ongoing rise. That is the two-phase picture we just drew from Hebrew grammar.

During Days 2–4, the firmament was stretching at a rate not comparable to anything we see in normal post-creation physics. The research behind this chapter (Foundations Vol 5 Ch 8 and Ch 12) models the early expansion rate as roughly fourteen orders of magnitude faster than the modern Hubble rate — about a hundred trillion times faster than today. That is not a small tweak. That is a fundamentally different regime — creation mode — which ended when the Maker rested.

During that rapid stretching, stars emitted light in the ordinary way. Photons left stellar surfaces at the speed of light through the fabric. But the *fabric itself* was growing underneath them. The road was getting longer behind them, and the destination (Earth) was being carried with the fabric too.

Today, we measure Andromeda at two and a half million light-years. That is the distance across the fabric *now*, in its present stretched-out size. Run the cosmos backwards — unstretched the fabric — and that distance was, during Day 4, a tiny fraction of today's. The light that left Andromeda on Day 4 had a much shorter road to travel.

### The ladybug on the balloon

Take an uninflated balloon. On its rubber, put a ladybug walking from one point to another. She walks at her own steady ladybug speed. Her speed relative to the rubber beneath her is a constant.

While she is walking, start inflating the balloon. The rubber under her feet is stretching. Every point on the surface is moving away from every other point. She is still walking at her steady speed relative to the rubber. But the distance she can cover is much greater than if the balloon were sitting still, because the rubber behind her is being pulled back while she keeps going.

Inflate the balloon fast enough, and she crosses from one side to the other in a fraction of the time it would have taken on a static balloon — not because she sped up, but because the geometry changed under her.

That is the picture. Light is the ladybug. The fabric is the balloon. Constant speed through the fabric. Enormously fast expansion of the fabric itself during the early days. The light arrives on time because the road it is traveling is shrinking behind it in the creation frame, even while it is growing in our after-creation frame.

No speeding up of light. No fabricated photons. Only the thing Scripture has been saying since Isaiah: God stretched out the heavens. Let Him actually stretch them, and the starlight-and-time problem stops being a problem.

## 5. He Calls the Stars By Name

There is one more piece to set down, because the text itself thinks it is central. The stars in Genesis 1 were not made as a ball of fusing hydrogen and left to evolve for a few hundred million years. They were made to function, on Day 4, as signs and seasons and lights upon the earth. They were made *mature*.

This is what the framework calls **functional integrity**. On Day 6, when Adam opens his eyes, he does not look up at an empty sky and wait a hundred thousand years for the first starlight. He looks up and sees a full sky. Genesis 1:14–15 spells the requirement out:

> *"...let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years: and let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth..."*
> — Genesis 1:14–15, KJV

Stars that cannot be seen cannot be signs. A dark sky cannot mark seasons. The requirement is a functioning sky by Day 6. Whatever the Maker did on Days 2, 3, and 4, He did it in a way that delivered a working sky on time.

And He did not just make a smear of glowing dust. He made each star with intention.

> *"He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names."*
> — Psalm 147:4, KJV

Every star — every one of a hundred billion in every one of two trillion galaxies we can see — has a name given to it by the Maker. When my wife's cousin and I stood on that porch and looked at Andromeda, we were looking at a trillion named stars. And that was one galaxy of two trillion.

Isaiah says it like this, and this is the verse I want your family to memorize:

> *"Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number: he calleth them all by names by the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power; not one faileth."*
> — Isaiah 40:26, KJV

Hear two things in that verse. The invitation: *Lift up your eyes*. The Bible is not embarrassed by the night sky. Turn the house lights off. Find a dark stretch of road. Look. And the claim: *He calleth them all by names*. Every one of them. The reason not one faileth is that He is holding them there.

The question on my back porch was scientific — how does the light get here. The bigger question underneath is always a worship one — *who* is behind it. The answer to the small question: the fabric was stretching. The answer to the big one: the Maker who stretched it calls every light in it by name.

## 6. The Sabbath Boundary

Now a principle that is going to come back several times in this book.

Creation week was not the ordinary running of the cosmos. It was a different regime. On Day 7, something changed:

> *"And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made. And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made."*
> — Genesis 2:2–3, KJV

The Hebrew verb is **שָׁבַת** — *shavat* — from which we get *Sabbath*. It does not mean *stopped being tired*. It means *ceased, finished, put down the tools*. Hebrews 4 picks up the same picture with the Greek verb **τελέω** — *teleō* — the verb Jesus uses on the cross in John 19:30: *It is finished*. The creation work is done. The Maker is not currently making new kinds of matter, new kinds of stars, new kinds of laws. He is sustaining the things He made.

That gives us a principle I am going to call the **Sabbath Boundary**:

> *Creation-mode physics (Days 1–6) and sustaining-mode physics (Day 7 onward) are not the same regime. Since the resting, the laws we observe are constant. During creation week, however, the Maker was performing acts by definition not repeatable from inside the room He was building.*

This is not "science ends here and magic begins." It is a modeling statement. A good engineer knows the difference between the boundary condition and the ongoing equation. The Maker's activity during the six days is the **boundary condition**. The laws we measure today are the ongoing equation. Both are real; both are from His hand; but they are not the same process.

Peter attaches a warning:

> *"Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts... saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation. For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water: whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished."*
> — 2 Peter 3:3–6, KJV

Peter names the specific error: the assumption that "all things continue as they were from the beginning." Today that assumption has a name — *uniformitarianism*. It is the working principle of modern geology and cosmology, and inside the sustaining era it is reasonable. But Peter tells us, by name, that treating it as universal is *willful ignorance*. There have been discontinuities. The Flood was one. The creation week was another.

When a secular cosmologist says "today's expansion rate, projected backward, gives 13.8 billion years," the arithmetic inside the sustaining era is fine. But the moment you extrapolate that rate back through the boundary, you are asking a sustaining-era ruler to measure creation-mode activity. It is the wrong instrument for the job.

This is the core misunderstanding under the starlight-and-time question. The question assumes a single, uniform rate stretching back unbroken to the beginning. Scripture's own two-phase grammar — perfect tense *then*, participle *now* — specifically rejects that. Once you respect the Sabbath Boundary, the contradiction dissolves.

## 7. But What About…?

Every chapter in this part of the book has a "But what about" block. You are going to hear objections from neighbors and cousins and the occasional uncle, and I want you to have answers on the tip of your tongue.

**But what about the 13.8 billion years number in my child's textbook?**

The arithmetic is real. Astronomers measure today's Hubble rate, extrapolate it backward under the assumption of uniform expansion, and get about 13.8 billion years. The math is not wrong. The assumption in the middle — that the rate has been constant back to the beginning — is the one Peter warns against and the one Hebrew grammar specifically splits into two phases. Take the assumption out, and the age number changes.

When your child comes home with the textbook, do not attack it. Say: *That number comes from running the clock backward. The clock only covers one phase of the history. Scripture tells us there was another phase before this one, and physics is starting to notice there was too.*

**But what about radiometric dating? Doesn't it also give billions of years?**

Yes, and we will take that up in Chapter 11, because it deserves its own chapter. Short answer for tonight: same Sabbath-Boundary issue, plus a second issue about the apparent maturity of matter at the moment of creation. *Same family of question, different mechanism, same kind of answer.*

**But what about Christians who say the days were long ages?**

These are our brothers and sisters. The *day-age* reading has been held by sincere believers who read their Bibles closely. I am persuaded by the six-ordinary-days reading for reasons laid out in Chapter 4, but Christians who read it differently are not faithless. They love the same Jesus. Section 8 walks through the main honest readings side by side.

**But what about the idea that the speed of light used to be faster?**

Covered in section 2 — *c-decay*. You will still meet Christians who hold it. Handle them gently; the idea was offered in good faith. But *c* is constant, and our rapid-stretching answer does not need it to change. It only needs the fabric to stretch, which Scripture told us from the beginning it did.

**But what about the cosmic microwave background — doesn't that prove a Big Bang?**

The CMB is a faint hiss of radiation coming from every direction at 2.7 Kelvin. Secular cosmologists read it as the afterglow of an early hot dense state. Our framework reads it as compatible — the vibrational signature of the firmament membrane, imprinted when the fabric was still in its rapid-stretching phase. Its uniformity across the sky, which is a puzzle for standard Big Bang cosmology (requiring an add-on called *inflation*), is not a puzzle in a framework where the membrane was made uniform by the Maker and then stretched together.

**But what about James Webb telescope images of mature galaxies at the edge of the visible universe?**

This one is a gift. Webb has been returning pictures of fully-formed spiral galaxies at distances that, under standard cosmology, correspond to just a few hundred million years after the Big Bang — too early, on the standard timeline. Cosmologists are rewriting their models. In a framework where galaxies were *made* mature — functional from Day 4 — this is exactly what we would expect. A prediction confirmed.

## 8. Three Honest Readings

I want to pause to honor something important. The Christian family has more than one honest reading of how to line Genesis up with what we see in the sky. I believe the reading I have been giving — young cosmos, rapid creation-week expansion, Sabbath Boundary — is the best fit for both Scripture and physics. But I am not the only one who loves this Book.

**The young-cosmos, rapid-expansion reading** — the one this book defends. Creation week was about six thousand years ago. The six days were ordinary days. The fabric was stretched rapidly during Days 2–4 in a creation-mode regime, delivering a functioning sky by Day 6.

**The day-age reading.** Creation is billions of years old. Each *yom* represents a long epoch, matching the sequence modern cosmology and geology describe. Christian geologists from the nineteenth century onward — Asa Gray, B.B. Warfield, Francis Collins today — have held it. The Bible tells a true sequence in language fitted to the listener.

**The framework / literary reading.** Creation is billions of years old. The six days are a literary structure, a sevenfold poetic frame revealing the *why* of the world without making a calendar claim. Hebrew scholars like John Walton have taught this. Genesis is teaching us whose world it is, not when.

These three readings have different implications for starlight and time, and they weight scientific evidence differently. But all three take the text seriously. All three take the science seriously. All three confess Christ. All three have a place at the family table.

I am going to keep writing from inside the first reading, because that is where I have landed and where the physics we have been developing fits most naturally. But the other two are not unbiblical. Good families can hold different readings here without breaking fellowship. The starlight question is not the hill on which the gospel stands or falls. That hill has a different Name on it, and we will get there in Book 4.

## 9. Honest About What We Know and What We Do Not

The one thing that kills credibility faster than being wrong is being more certain than the evidence warrants.

**High confidence:**

- Scripture, in at least seventeen passages, describes the heavens as stretched out.
- Hebrew grammar distinguishes a completed rapid stretching from an ongoing slow stretching.
- Modern cosmology has independently confirmed that the fabric is expanding today, and our best models require an early phase of very rapid expansion (*inflation*) in addition to the slow one we measure now.
- The speed of light through the fabric is constant, as near as we can measure.
- Maturity in distant galaxies — fully formed structures, orbital dynamics, heavy-element chemistry — fits stars made functional from Day 4 and is a headache for models that require billions of years of slow evolution.

**Moderate confidence:**

- The creation-week expansion rate was about fourteen orders of magnitude greater than today's Hubble rate. The Foundations Vol 5 model gives this; the math depends on assumptions about membrane tension and density we are still refining.
- The CMB is best read as a membrane vibrational signature rather than an afterglow of a Big Bang singularity. I do not claim the case is closed.

**Low confidence — we do not know yet:**

- The exact mechanism by which the membrane's tension and density were set during Day 2.
- Whether phenomena like the detailed CMB power spectrum and the abundances of the lightest elements find their best explanation in our framework or the standard model. Testable questions. Work ongoing.

None of this unsettles the main point. Scripture describes a stretched cosmos. Honest physics describes a stretched cosmos. The starlight-and-time question goes away when you let the stretching actually happen. Anyone who tells you all the details are settled — in any direction — is selling you something.

Know what you know. Know what you do not know. Tell the difference honestly. That is the engineer's posture.

My answer: it means the question is hard. It means the Bible leaves room for more than one faithful reading of this particular aspect of Genesis 1. The right posture is not *who is right* but *what can we all say together, what do we differ on, and how do we love each other across the difference*.

That is the posture I want in the Raymond house. I want it in yours too.

## 11. Lift Up Your Eyes

Let me close where my wife's cousin and I ended that night.

By about twelve-thirty we had talked through all of it. The raisin bread. The ladybug. The two Hebrew verbs. The Sabbath Boundary. The 2 Peter warning. He was quiet for a minute, and then he said something I have thought about many times since.

That is exactly right.

The Maker beat out a membrane thin and wide. He placed lights in it. He stretched it during the building days in a regime we are not inside of anymore. When He finished, He put down His tools. He blessed the seventh day. The fabric we sit in now is the finished fabric, still stretching gently the way Isaiah's participle says, but no longer in the rapid creation-mode we needed for the early light to reach us. The stars He made on Day 4 were made mature. The light we see tonight is real light, from real stars, that traveled a real but much shorter early road. Nothing is fake. Nothing is in transit. It is the honest work of the honest Hand that beat it out.

When we lift up our eyes, we are not looking at a problem. We are looking at a testimony.

Not one faileth. That is the line I want in your mouth the next time somebody tells you science has disproved Genesis. Go outside. Turn off the lights. Lift up your eyes. He calleth them all by names. Not one faileth.

If you have understood this chapter, the next one will go down easier.

For tonight, put this book down. Get your family. Go outside. Look up.

He is still holding them there.

## Key Terms

- **Raqia (רָקִיעַ)** — Hebrew for *firmament*. From the verb *raqa*, *to beat out thin*. Not a solid dome; a stretched-out membrane.
- **Raqa (רָקַע)** — The verb behind *raqia*. Used in Exodus 39:3 (gold beaten thin) and Numbers 16:39 (bronze censers hammered flat).
- **Natah** — Second Hebrew verb for stretching the heavens: *to stretch, extend, pitch as a tent*.
- **Shavat (שָׁבַת)** — Hebrew for *Sabbath*: *to cease, to finish* (Genesis 2:2–3).
- **Teleō (τελέω)** — Greek *to finish, complete*. Used in Hebrews 4:3 of creation and in John 19:30 of the cross.
- **Sabbath Boundary** — Creation-mode physics (Days 1–6) and sustaining-mode physics (Day 7 onward) are different regimes. Extrapolating sustaining-era rates across the boundary gives wrong answers about creation-era events.
- **Uniformitarianism** — The assumption that present rates and conditions have always held. Useful inside the sustaining era; rejected by 2 Peter 3:3–6 as a universal claim.
- **Hubble expansion** — The ongoing slow expansion measurable today; the *participle* stretching in Scripture's two-verb grammar.
- **Rapid firmament expansion** — Creation-mode stretching during Days 2–4; the *perfect-tense* completed stretching.
- **Functional integrity** — Creation made mature to function from the moment it was made.
- **"Light in transit" hypothesis** — Disavowed. Requires fabricated history of events that never happened.
- **c-decay hypothesis** — Disavowed. *c* is tightly coupled to all other constants, and nothing in the data supports it.
