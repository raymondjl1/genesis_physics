## 18.2 Standard Model of Particle Physics

### 18.2.1 Overview and Achievements

**The Standard Model** (SM): Framework describing fundamental particles and three of four forces (electromagnetic, weak nuclear, strong nuclear; excludes gravity)

**Particle content**:
- **Quarks** (6 flavors): up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom
- **Leptons** (6): electron, muon, tau, + 3 neutrinos
- **Force carriers** (gauge bosons): photon (EM), W/Z bosons (weak), gluons (strong)
- **Higgs boson**: Gives mass via Higgs field

**Interactions governed by**:
- **Quantum Electrodynamics** (QED): Electromagnetic force
- **Quantum Chromodynamics** (QCD): Strong nuclear force
- **Electroweak theory**: Unifies EM and weak forces

### 18.2.2 Extraordinary Successes

**1. Predictive precision** (unmatched in science):
- **QED**: Predicts electron magnetic moment (g-factor) to **10 decimal places**
  - Theory: g/2 = 1.001 159 652 181 78 (13)
  - Experiment: g/2 = 1.001 159 652 180 73 (28)
  - **Agreement to 1 part in 10^10** ✓✓✓

- **Electroweak**: Predicted W/Z boson masses before discovery
  - Predicted: MW ≈ 80 GeV, MZ ≈ 91 GeV
  - Discovered (1983): MW = 80.4 GeV, MZ = 91.2 GeV ✓✓✓

- **Higgs**: Predicted ~50 years before observation
  - Predicted: Mechanism for mass generation
  - Discovered (2012): Higgs boson at 125 GeV ✓✓✓

**2. Technological applications**:
- **Semiconductors**: Quantum mechanics → transistors, computers, smartphones
- **GPS**: Special + General relativity corrections (40 μs/day accuracy)
- **PET scans**: Antimatter (positrons) in medical imaging
- **Lasers**: Quantum transitions, coherent light
- **MRI**: Nuclear magnetic resonance, medical diagnostics

**3. Experimental verification**:
- Thousands of experiments over 50+ years
- Particle accelerators (LHC, Tevatron, LEP)
- Cosmic ray detectors
- Precision measurements (g-2, atomic spectra)
- **No confirmed violations** of SM predictions (within tested regimes)

**4. Mathematical elegance**:
- Gauge symmetry (SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1))
- Renormalizability (infinities removed systematically)
- Unification (EM + weak → electroweak)

### 18.2.3 Known Limitations

**Despite successes, SM has major gaps**:

**1. Gravity not included**:
- SM describes three forces, not four
- Cannot unify with General Relativity (GR)
- Quantum gravity unsolved (strings, loops, other approaches all speculative)

**2. Dark matter** (27% of universe):
- SM particles insufficient (all detected particles account for ~5%)
- WIMPs, axions proposed but not detected
- Either new particles needed or modified gravity

**3. Dark energy** (68% of universe):
- SM doesn't explain cosmological constant
- Vacuum energy calculation off by **120 orders of magnitude** (worst prediction in physics)
- "Fine-tuning problem"

**4. Matter-antimatter asymmetry**:
- SM predicts equal matter/antimatter (symmetric)
- Observed: Universe is matter-dominated
- CP violation in SM too small (~10^-10), need ~10^-8 to explain asymmetry

**5. Neutrino masses**:
- SM predicts massless neutrinos
- Observed: Neutrinos have tiny mass (oscillation experiments)
- Requires extension (right-handed neutrinos, seesaw mechanism)

**6. Hierarchy problem**:
- Why is Higgs mass so light (125 GeV)?
- Quantum corrections should push to Planck scale (10^19 GeV)
- Requires fine-tuning to 1 part in 10^32 (naturalness problem)

**7. Free parameters** (~26):
- 6 quark masses
- 3 lepton masses
- 3 neutrino masses
- 4 CKM mixing parameters
- 4 PMNS mixing parameters
- 3 coupling constants (strong, weak, EM)
- Higgs mass, vacuum expectation value
- QCD vacuum angle (θ)

**These are measured, not derived** (no explanation why these values)

**8. No explanation for values**:
- Why 3 generations of fermions (not 2, or 4)?
- Why this mass hierarchy (electron: 0.5 MeV, top quark: 173 GeV)?
- Why these coupling constants (α ≈ 1/137, αs ≈ 1)?

**9. Philosophical silence**:
- SM describes **what** and **how** but not **why**
- No meaning, purpose, or teleology
- Silent on consciousness, free will, morality

### 18.2.4 Our Assessment of Standard Model

**Strengths** (where SM dominates):
✓✓✓ **Precision**: Unmatched numerical accuracy  
✓✓✓ **Technology**: Enables entire modern infrastructure  
✓✓✓ **Verification**: Experimentally tested to extreme rigor  
✓✓ **Unification**: Electroweak (partial unification achieved)

**Weaknesses** (where SM struggles):
✗ **Incompleteness**: Gravity excluded, dark matter/energy unexplained  
✗ **Parameters**: ~26 free parameters (measured, not derived)  
✗ **Fine-tuning**: Hierarchy problem, cosmological constant problem  
✗ **Meaning**: Silent on purpose, significance, teleology  
✗ **Origin**: Cannot explain why laws exist or why these specific laws

**Overall**: SM is **spectacularly successful** for its domain (particle physics, tested energy scales) but **admittedly incomplete** and **philosophically silent**.

## 18.3 General Relativity and Cosmology

### 18.3.1 General Relativity (GR)

**Einstein's field equations** (1915): Describe gravity as spacetime curvature

**G_μν + Λg_μν = (8πG/c^4) T_μν**

**Spectacular successes**:

**1. Perihelion precession of Mercury**:
- Newtonian gravity: Predicts 531 arcsec/century
- Observed: 574 arcsec/century (43 arcsec/century discrepancy)
- GR prediction: **Exactly 43 arcsec/century** ✓✓✓

**2. Gravitational lensing**:
- Predicted (1915): Massive objects bend light
- Confirmed (1919): Eddington eclipse observations ✓
- Modern: Countless examples (galaxies, clusters, black holes)

**3. Gravitational time dilation**:
- Predicted: Clocks run slower in gravity wells
- Confirmed: Pound-Rebka experiment (1959), GPS satellites ✓✓✓
- Technological necessity (GPS corrections essential)

**4. Gravitational waves**:
- Predicted (1916): Ripples in spacetime
- Confirmed (2015): LIGO detects black hole merger ✓✓✓
- Nobel Prize 2017

**5. Black holes**:
- Predicted: Event horizons, singularities
- Observed: X-ray binaries, galactic centers, M87 image (2019) ✓✓

**6. Cosmology**:
- Expanding universe (Hubble's law, 1929)
- Big Bang cosmology (CMB radiation, 1965)
- Age of universe (~13.8 billion years)

### 18.3.2 Cosmological Puzzles

**Despite successes, cosmology has major mysteries**:

**1. Dark matter** (27% of universe):
- Needed to explain galaxy rotation curves
- Needed for gravitational lensing observations
- Needed for large-scale structure formation
- **But**: No particle detected after 40+ years searching

**2. Dark energy** (68% of universe):
- Needed to explain accelerating expansion (Type Ia supernovae)
- **Cosmological constant problem**: SM predicts Λ ~ 10^120 times observed value
- Worst prediction in physics

**3. Flatness problem**:
- Universe is spatially flat (Ω_total ≈ 1.00) to high precision
- Requires fine-tuning of initial conditions to 1 part in 10^60
- **Why so flat?**

**4. Horizon problem**:
- Distant regions of CMB have same temperature
- But no causal contact (separated beyond light travel distance)
- **Why so uniform?**

**5. Initial singularity**:
- GR predicts t=0 singularity (infinite density, curvature)
- Physics breaks down (GR cannot describe)
- **What happened before Big Bang?** (Ill-defined question in GR)

**6. Low-entropy initial state**:
- Universe started extremely low-entropy (highly ordered)
- Probability: ~1 in 10^(10^123) (Roger Penrose)
- **Why so ordered?** (Standard cosmology has no answer)

**7. Inflation hypothesis** (proposed solution to flatness, horizon):
- Rapid expansion in first 10^-32 seconds
- Elegant mathematically
- **But**: Unverified (no direct evidence), many variants, fine-tuning problems of its own

### 18.3.3 Our Assessment of GR/Cosmology

**Strengths**:
✓✓✓ **Precision**: Verified to extreme accuracy (GPS, gravitational waves)  
✓✓✓ **Prediction**: Black holes, gravitational waves predicted decades before observation  
✓✓ **Cosmology**: Big Bang, expansion, CMB all explained

**Weaknesses**:
✗ **Dark sector**: 95% of universe unexplained (dark matter + dark energy)  
✗ **Initial singularity**: Physics breaks down at t=0  
✗ **Fine-tuning**: Flatness, low-entropy start, cosmological constant  
✗ **Quantum incompatible**: Cannot merge with quantum mechanics

**Overall**: GR is **extraordinarily successful** for macroscopic gravity but **incomplete** (dark sector, initial conditions, quantum gravity).

## 18.9 Which Model Is "True"?

### 18.9.1 Philosophical Perspectives on Truth

**Instrumentalism**: Theories are tools (useful for predictions, not necessarily describing reality)
- Example: Ptolemaic epicycles vs. Copernican heliocentrism (both "worked" for predictions)
- View: Theory is "true" if it makes accurate predictions

**Realism**: Theories describe actual reality (approximate truth about what exists)
- Example: Atoms really exist (not just useful fiction)
- View: Theory is "true" if it corresponds to reality

**Constructivism**: Theories are human constructions (socially negotiated, not discovered)
- View: "Truth" is consensus, not correspondence

**Pragmatism**: Theories are true if they "work" (produce desired outcomes)
- View: Truth is practical success
