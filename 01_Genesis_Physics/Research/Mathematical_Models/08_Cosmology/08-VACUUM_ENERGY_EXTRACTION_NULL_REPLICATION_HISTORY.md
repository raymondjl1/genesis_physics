# The Empirical Record of Vacuum-Energy Extraction Claims
## A Null-Replication History, and Where the MRG Concept Stands Relative To It

**Author**: Genesis Physics Research Team
**Status**: Supporting note for Vol 6 Ch 10 (§10.7). No framework physics is changed here. Purpose: give the manuscript an honest, citable account of the external null-replication record so that the MRG section is not written in a vacuum (so to speak) of prior failed attempts.

---

## 1. Why this note exists

Vol 6 Ch 10 proposes a Firmament Resonance Generator (MRG) that couples to a vacuum-tension reservoir via a modulated-boundary (dynamic Casimir) stage. Any such proposal lands in a field with a long record of **extraordinary claims followed by null replications**. A graduate-textbook treatment must engage that record directly rather than ignore it. This note characterizes the external literature so the manuscript can cite an existing Research file (per the project's sourcing rule) instead of introducing outside references inline.

This note does **not** claim the MRG works. It claims only that (a) the prior-failure record is real and must be stated, and (b) the MRG's *falsification protocol* is what distinguishes a responsible proposal from the failed ones — not a promise of success.

---

## 2. The external record (characterized, not endorsed)

The following are the principal touchstones in the public record. They are summarized here at the level needed for an honest caveat; the manuscript should present them as cautionary precedent, not as supporting evidence.

1. **NASA Eagleworks / quantum-vacuum thrusters (White et al., ~2014–2021).** A NASA Johnson Space Center laboratory reported small anomalous thrust signals from RF resonant cavities ("EmDrive"-class and "Q-thruster" tests). Subsequent higher-fidelity work — notably the independent test campaign at TU Dresden (Tajmar and co-workers, ~2018–2021) — attributed the measured thrust to **thermal expansion and mounting/seismic artifacts** rather than any vacuum-energy interaction, and the apparent signal vanished when those error sources were controlled. **Net status: null.** The episode is the canonical modern example of a real laboratory producing a real-looking signal that did not survive artifact control.

2. **Maclay and the dynamical/zero-point energy-extraction analyses (G. J. Maclay and collaborators, ~1999–2010s).** Careful theoretical treatments of whether useful work can be extracted from the quantum vacuum via Casimir-type cycles. The recurring conclusion: the **static** Casimir effect is conservative (closed-cycle work is zero), and any net extraction requires a genuinely **non-conservative (dynamic, time-dependent-boundary)** process that does *external* work on the field. There is no free lunch; energy "extracted" is energy supplied to modulate the boundaries plus whatever reservoir the system is genuinely open to. **Net status: not a perpetual-motion loophole.**

3. **Davis / advanced-propulsion vacuum-energy reviews (E. W. Davis, and the AIAA/USAF-era "Teleportation/Advanced Propulsion" survey literature, ~2004–2010s).** Broad surveys of zero-point-energy and exotic-vacuum propulsion concepts. The consistent verdict across these reviews: the theoretical energy density of the vacuum is enormous on paper, but **no demonstrated mechanism converts it to usable, net-positive continuous power**, and most concrete proposals founder on either conservation accounting or unmeasurably small effect sizes. **Net status: open theoretically, unrealized experimentally.**

4. **The dynamic Casimir effect itself (Wilson et al., 2011).** The one *positive* and *replicated* result in this cluster: photon generation from a rapidly modulated boundary (a superconducting circuit emulating a relativistically moving mirror) was demonstrated. Crucially, this is **not** free energy — the photons are paid for by the work done modulating the boundary. It establishes that modulated boundaries *do* couple to the vacuum field (the physical basis the MRG's DISRUPT stage relies on), while establishing nothing about net-positive extraction.

## 3. The honest lesson from the record

Three invariants emerge, and the framework must respect all three:

- **(L1) Conservation is never violated.** Every credible result reduces to an *open-system* energy balance: output = (reservoir draw) − (losses), with the modulation work and any genuine reservoir explicitly accounted. Static/closed cycles give zero. (Consistent with the framework's own §10.10 thermodynamic-consistency requirement and Axiom 1 / Axiom 5.)
- **(L2) Small anomalous signals are usually artifacts.** Thermal drift, mounting strain, EMI, and seismic coupling have repeatedly masqueraded as new physics (Eagleworks → Dresden). Any positive MRG result must survive the *same* artifact controls before it counts.
- **(L3) The vacuum's paper energy density does not imply accessible power.** A huge ρ_vac on paper is not a usable reservoir; the framework's own partition (§10.7.1: accessible ρ_Λ ≈ 6×10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ vs. structural ~10¹¹³ J/m³ locked into membrane tension) is the framework-internal statement of exactly this caution.

## 4. Where the MRG concept claims to differ — and where it does not

The MRG concept does **not** claim to evade L1–L3. Its claim to differ is narrower and is *conditional on the zone-architecture axioms being correct*:

- **It is openly an open system.** Unlike most "extract energy from the vacuum" proposals, the MRG does not claim a closed cycle. It draws on the sustaining coupling κ(t) (Axiom 5), an explicitly external reservoir. If κ(t) is zero or inaccessible at laboratory scale, the MRG produces no net power — and that is a *falsifiable* outcome, not an excuse.
- **Its differentiator is the DIRECT (symmetry-breaking) stage, not the DISRUPT stage.** The DISRUPT stage is ordinary dynamic-Casimir physics (Wilson 2011), which is real but net-zero by itself. The framework's *additional* claim is that a gravity-aligned magnetic bias breaks time-reversal symmetry so that the dynamic-Casimir photon flux is rectified into a net direction. This is the part with no external precedent — and therefore the part most in need of falsification, not the part to lean on.
- **It must pass the artifact gauntlet that sank Eagleworks.** The framework's strongest honest position is *procedural*: the §10.8 falsification protocol (net-energy balance, magnetic-field dependence, orientation dependence, dielectric scaling, spectral fingerprint) is designed precisely to discriminate a real effect from the thermal/mounting/EMI artifacts that produced and then dissolved the historical signals. Orientation dependence (§10.8.3) is the most discriminating test because no known artifact reproduces a gravity-aligned signature.

**Why the framework is *expected* (conditionally) to do better than the historical attempts:** the prior null results were either (i) closed-cycle proposals that conservation forbids (Maclay's verdict), or (ii) open-but-unmodeled setups whose apparent signal was an uncontrolled artifact (Eagleworks). The MRG is neither closed-cycle (it names its reservoir, κ(t)) nor artifact-blind (it ships the discriminating test suite). That is a reason to *test* it, not a reason to believe it. **If the §10.8 tests fail, the MRG joins the null record — and the chapter says so in advance.**
