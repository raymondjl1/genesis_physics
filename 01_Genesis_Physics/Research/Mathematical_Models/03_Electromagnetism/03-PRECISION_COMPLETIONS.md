## Part 2: Faraday Cage — Exponential Field Decay
### Test ID: 3.10

### 2.1 Physical Principle

A Faraday cage is a closed conducting enclosure. External time-varying electric fields induce charge redistribution on the conductor surface. Inside, the field decays exponentially with the skin depth:

$$E(z) = E_0 e^{-z/\delta}$$

where δ is the penetration depth (see Section 3 for full derivation).

### 2.2 Maxwell Equations at the Conductor Boundary

At the conductor surface (z = 0), the boundary conditions are:

**Tangential E-field** (Faraday's law):
$$E_{\parallel, \text{inside}} = 0$$

(In electrostatic equilibrium, E = 0 inside a conductor; for time-varying fields, E decays exponentially.)

**Normal D-field** (Gauss's law):
$$\mathbf{n} \cdot (D_{\text{outside}} - D_{\text{inside}}) = \sigma_{\text{surface}}$$

where σ_surface is the induced surface charge density.

### 2.3 Derivation of Exponential Decay Inside Conductor

Inside a conducting medium (z > 0), the wave equation is:

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} = \mu_0 \sigma \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

(For a good conductor, the displacement current is negligible.)

For a plane wave with time dependence e^{−iωt} and spatial dependence e^{ikz}:

$$\mathbf{E}(z,t) = E_0 e^{ikz} e^{-i\omega t}$$

Substituting into the diffusion equation:

$$-k^2 E_0 = -i\omega \mu_0 \sigma E_0$$

$$k^2 = i\omega \mu_0 \sigma$$

The complex wavenumber is:

$$k = \sqrt{i\omega\mu_0\sigma} = \sqrt{\omega\mu_0\sigma} e^{i\pi/4} = \sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu_0\sigma}{2}}(1 + i)$$

Writing k = k_r + ik_i:

$$k_r = k_i = \sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu_0\sigma}{2}} = \frac{1}{\delta}$$

where

$$\boxed{\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu_0\sigma}}}$$

is the **skin depth** (penetration depth).

### 2.4 Field Amplitude Inside the Cage

With k = (1+i)/δ, the field becomes:

$$\mathbf{E}(z,t) = E_0 e^{i(1+i)z/\delta} e^{-i\omega t} = E_0 e^{-z/\delta} e^{i(z/\delta - \omega t)}$$

**Amplitude decay**: $E(z) = E_0 e^{-z/\delta}$

**Phase**: The wave propagates into the conductor with wavelength λ_c = 2πδ.

### 2.5 Shielding Effectiveness

The ratio of transmitted to incident field is:

$$\frac{E_{\text{transmitted}}}{E_{\text{incident}}} = e^{-t/\delta}$$

For a conductor of thickness t, the **shielding effectiveness** (in dB) is:

$$\boxed{SE = 20\log_{10}\left(e^{t/\delta}\right) = 8.686 \frac{t}{\delta} \text{ dB}}$$

### 2.6 Numerical Example: Copper Faraday Cage at 60 Hz and 1 MHz

**Copper Properties**:
- σ_Cu = 5.96 × 10⁷ S/m
- μ = μ₀ ≈ 1.26 × 10⁻⁶ H/m

**At 60 Hz (f = 60 Hz, ω = 2π × 60 = 377 rad/s)**:

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{377 \times 1.26 \times 10^{-6} \times 5.96 \times 10^7}}$$

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{2.83 \times 10^3}} = \sqrt{7.07 \times 10^{-4}} = 0.0266 \text{ m} = 2.66 \text{ cm}$$

For a 2 mm thick copper sheet:

$$\frac{E}{E_0} = e^{-0.002/0.0266} = e^{-0.0752} = 0.927$$

**Shielding**: SE = 8.686 × (0.002/0.0266) = 0.65 dB (weak attenuation at low frequency)

**At 1 MHz (f = 10⁶ Hz, ω = 2π × 10⁶ rad/s)**:

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{2\pi \times 10^6 \times 1.26 \times 10^{-6} \times 5.96 \times 10^7}}$$

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{2.37 \times 10^8}} = \sqrt{8.44 \times 10^{-9}} = 9.2 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m} = 0.092 \text{ mm}$$

For a 2 mm thick copper sheet:

$$\frac{E}{E_0} = e^{-2/0.092} = e^{-21.7} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-10}$$

**Shielding**: SE = 8.686 × (2/0.092) = 189 dB (excellent attenuation at high frequency)

## Part 4: Superconductivity from Membrane Phonons
### Test ID: 3.12

### 4.1 Physical Mechanism: Cooper Pairing

Superconductivity emerges from an instability in the Fermi sea when electrons interact via an attractive potential. In a normal metal at T < T_F, electrons near the Fermi surface can pair via **phonon exchange**: electron → phonon → electron, resulting in a net attractive interaction.

The pairing creates a **Cooper pair** (two electrons with opposite momenta and spins) bound by the phonon potential. Below a critical temperature T_c, all states near the Fermi surface become paired, and the system develops a **gap** Δ, preventing excitations.

### 4.2 Derivation from Firmament Mechanics

In Genesis Physics, the attractive electron-electron interaction arises from the periodic potential of the ion lattice (lattice vibrations = phonons). The Debye model describes lattice vibrations:

**Phonon dispersion relation** (acoustic mode):
$$\omega_{\mathbf{q}} = v_s |\mathbf{q}|$$

where v_s is the sound velocity and q is the phonon wavenumber.

**Phonon energy** ranges from 0 to ℏω_D, where ω_D is the Debye cutoff frequency:

$$\omega_D = v_s \frac{(6\pi^2 N)^{1/3}}{V}$$

**Electron-phonon coupling**: When an electron near the Fermi surface emits a phonon, it creates a potential well that attracts another electron. The effective attractive interaction for pairs within ℏω_D of the Fermi surface is:

$$V_{\text{eff}} \approx -V_0 \quad \text{(constant, attractive)}$$

where V₀ depends on the electron-phonon coupling strength.

### 4.3 BCS Gap Equation

At T = 0, the BCS theory gives the **gap equation**:

$$\boxed{1 = N(0) V_0 \int_0^{\omega_D} \frac{d\omega}{\sqrt{\omega^2 + \Delta^2}}}$$

where N(0) is the density of states at the Fermi surface and Δ is the superconducting gap.

**Solving the gap equation**:

For weak coupling (N(0)V₀ ≪ 1), the integral gives:

$$1 = N(0) V_0 \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega_D}{\Delta}\right) \approx N(0) V_0 \ln\left(\frac{2\omega_D}{\Delta}\right)$$

Solving for Δ:

$$\Delta = 2\omega_D e^{-1/(N(0)V_0)} \approx 2\omega_D \exp\left(-\frac{1}{N(0)V_0}\right)$$

**Dimensional Check**:
$$[\Delta] = [\omega_D] = [Hz] = [\text{energy}/\hbar]$$ ✓

### 4.4 Critical Temperature

The **critical temperature** T_c is where the gap closes. From BCS theory:

$$\boxed{T_c = \frac{\omega_D}{\pi k_B} e^{-1/(N(0)V_0)} = \frac{\Delta(T=0)}{\pi k_B}}$$

where k_B is Boltzmann's constant.

**Dimensional Check**:
$$[T_c] = [\omega_D] / [k_B] = [K]$$ ✓

## Part 5: Meissner Effect — Magnetic Field Expulsion
### Test ID: 3.13

### 5.1 Superconducting Response to Magnetic Fields

In a **normal conductor**, magnetic flux penetrates freely. In a **superconductor**, two phenomena occur:

1. **Meissner Effect** (T < T_c): Magnetic field is expelled from the interior. This is an **active** effect — existing flux is pushed out.
2. **Perfect Conductivity**: A perfect conductor would also exclude changing flux (but would preserve existing flux).

The Meissner effect is more subtle: even static magnetic field (applied before cooling below T_c) is expelled.

### 5.2 London Equations

The response of a superconductor to electromagnetic fields is described by the **London equations**:

**First London equation** (force on superconducting electrons):
$$\mathbf{E} = \rho_n \mathbf{J}_n + \frac{m_e}{n_e e^2} \frac{\partial \mathbf{J}_s}{\partial t}$$

where J_s is the supercurrent and ρ_n is the resistance of normal carriers.

**Second London equation** (relating supercurrent to magnetic field):
$$\boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf{J}_s = -\frac{n_e e^2}{m_e} \mathbf{B}}$$

In the superconducting state, the supercurrent adjusts to maintain ∇ × J_s = −(n_e e²/m_e) B.

### 5.3 Derivation of London Penetration Depth

From Ampère's law:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J}_s$$

Taking the curl:

$$\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) = \mu_0 \nabla \times \mathbf{J}_s = -\mu_0 \frac{n_e e^2}{m_e} \mathbf{B}$$

Using ∇ × (∇ × B) = ∇(∇·B) − ∇²B = −∇²B:

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \frac{n_e e^2}{m_e} \mathbf{B}$$

**Define the London penetration depth**:

$$\boxed{\lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{m_e}{\mu_0 n_e e^2}}}$$

Then:

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{B} = \frac{\mathbf{B}}{\lambda_L^2}$$

### 5.4 Solution for Magnetic Field Inside Superconductor

For a semi-infinite superconductor (z > 0) with external field B₀ applied at z = 0:

$$\mathbf{B}(z) = B_0 e^{-z/\lambda_L}$$

The magnetic field **decays exponentially** with penetration depth λ_L.

**Dimensional Check**:
$$[\lambda_L] = \sqrt{\frac{[kg]}{[H/m][A/C]^2[C^2]}} = \sqrt{\frac{[kg]}{[kg·m/A^2·s^2][A/C]^2[C^2]}} = [m]$$ ✓

### 5.5 Numerical Calculation: London Depth for Aluminum

**Aluminum superconductor**:
- n_e (carrier density) = N_A ρ / M where N_A = 6.02×10²³, ρ = 2700 kg/m³, M = 27 g/mol
- n_e = (6.02×10²³ × 2700) / (27×10⁻³) ≈ 6.0 × 10²⁸ carriers/m³

$$\lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}{1.26 \times 10^{-6} \times 6.0 \times 10^{28} \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2}}$$

$$\lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}{1.94 \times 10^{-6}}} = \sqrt{4.7 \times 10^{-25}} = 6.9 \times 10^{-13} \text{ m}$$

Wait, this is far too small. Let me recalculate with correct units.

**Correct calculation** (using SI units):

$$\lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{m_e}{\mu_0 n_e e^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}}{1.26 \times 10^{-6} \text{ H/m} \times 6.0 \times 10^{28} \text{ m}^{-3} \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2 \text{ C}^2}}$$

$$= \sqrt{\frac{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}{1.26 \times 10^{-6} \times 6.0 \times 10^{28} \times 2.57 \times 10^{-38}}}$$

$$= \sqrt{\frac{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}{1.94 \times 10^{-15}}} = \sqrt{4.7 \times 10^{-16}} = 2.2 \times 10^{-8} \text{ m} = 22 \text{ nm}$$

**Experimental value for Al**: λ_L ≈ 50 nm

The theory gives the right order of magnitude (tens of nanometers). The discrepancy arises from:
1. The effective mass m* (not bare m_e) in the London formula
2. Non-local effects not captured in the local London model

**Field decay at depth z = 100 nm**:
$$B(100 \text{ nm}) = B_0 e^{-100/22} = B_0 e^{-4.5} \approx 0.011 B_0$$

The field is reduced to ~1% at 100 nm depth.

### 5.6 Summary: Meissner Effect

The Meissner effect demonstrates that **superconductors are not perfect conductors** — they actively expel magnetic flux via the London response. The characteristic length scale λ_L (tens to hundreds of nanometers) sets the thickness of the surface layer through which the field penetrates.
