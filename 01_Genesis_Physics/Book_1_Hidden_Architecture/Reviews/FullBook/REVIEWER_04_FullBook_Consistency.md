**CON-FB-02: Zone One (Heaven Prime) — Atemporal vs. Transcendent Language**  
**Location:** Chapters 3, 5, 6, 13, 14  
**Concern Tags:** C4 (Clarity)  
**Severity:** MINOR  
**Status:** APPROVED FOR RETENTION

**Finding:** Zone 1 is described as both "transcendent" (Ch3 §2) and "atemporal" (Ch3 §2 and throughout). These are technically distinct properties in physics (transcendent = beyond temporal-material cosmos; atemporal = outside the time dimension). Both descriptions are used correctly in context, but the relationship between them could cause reader confusion.

**Verification:**
- Ch3 §2: "It is atemporal in the same sense Zone zero is atemporal, and it is transcendent in the sense that the word's Latin root implies"
- Zone_Architecture.md: Confirms Zone 1 is "atemporal in the same sense Zone zero is atemporal"
- Usage consistency: ✓ "Atemporal" and "transcendent" are used in complementary ways

**Assessment:** The author explicitly links the two concepts in Ch3 §2, establishing them as related properties of the same zone. The distinction is intentional and explained. No error detected.

**Recommendation:** Retain as-is. The dual description is technically accurate and properly contextualized.

### MODERATE-LEVEL ISSUES (Flagged for Author Awareness)

**CON-FB-05: Chapter 12 — Zone-Boundary FTL Mechanism Forward Reference Timing**  
**Location:** Chapter 12, §2 (Claim A statement)  
**Concern Tags:** C4 (Clarity)  
**Severity:** MODERATE  
**Status:** AUTHOR AWARE

**Finding:** Chapter 12 introduces three zone-boundary FTL mechanism classes (dimensional bypass, field distortion, zone tunneling) with the statement "Three of them walk at this book's density — dimensional bypass, field distortion, zone tunneling — and I will walk them."

However, Chapter 12 walks all three of these mechanisms in §7 of the same chapter. This creates no logical inconsistency, but the forward reference at the opening (§2) uses future tense ("and I will walk them") that readers naturally interpret as introducing material in later chapters.

**Verification:**
- All three mechanisms are indeed walked in Ch12 §7
- No forward reference is violated
- The five mechanisms are correctly listed (three walked here, two deferred to Foundations)
- No reader would be misled by the forward reference; it resolves within the same chapter

**Assessment:** The forward reference structure, while technically correct, could be slightly clarified for reader expectation-setting. This is a minor prose optimization, not an error.

**Recommendation:** Consider rewording §2 opening sentence to read "Three of them I can walk at this book's density..." to make clear the walking happens within the chapter. Not required for publication.

**CON-FB-06: Metaphor Strain in Fiber-Optic Analogy**  
**Location:** Chapter 13, §4 (starlight-in-expanding-medium section)  
**Concern Tags:** C4 (Clarity)  
**Severity:** MODERATE  
**Status:** AUTHOR AWARE

**Finding:** The fiber-optic-link analogy (Ch13 §4) is introduced with physical stretching of the fiber during pulse transit. The analogy explicitly carries the caveat: "Real fibers are stretched slowly, in sustaining-mode physics... The creation-mode firmament stretched at a rate vastly larger... The structural lesson carries. The engineering hardware does not."

The analogy is appropriate and well-caveated. However, the strain on the analogy is slightly sharper than the book typically allows, because the creation-mode expansion rate is not merely "vastly larger" but operates in fundamentally different conservation regimes (Ch11). The analogy, while structurally sound, does not capture this regime-shift aspect.

**Verification:**
- The caveat is explicit and appropriate (Ch13 §4, final paragraph)
- The structural correspondence (separation-growth vs. signal-propagation) is accurate
- The regime-shift limitation is acknowledged in Ch11 but not foregrounded in Ch13
- No logical inconsistency; the analogy performs its intended work

**Assessment:** The analogy is correct and properly caveated. The strain is noted by the author but could be slightly more emphatic to prevent readers from over-extending the analogy to conservation-law behavior.

**Recommendation:** Consider adding a single clarifying sentence in Ch13 §4 after the caveat: "Additionally, the conservation laws governing the two regimes differ, which the simple fiber analogy cannot represent." This would prevent the most dangerous over-extension while keeping the analogy clean. Not required; current version is acceptable.

**CON-FB-07: Sustaining Mode vs. Creation Mode — Phase-Naming Clarity**  
**Location:** Chapters 5, 11, 13  
**Concern Tags:** C4 (Clarity)  
**Severity:** MODERATE  
**Status:** APPROVED FOR RETENTION

**Finding:** The two operational modes are named using different linguistic patterns:
- "Creation mode" — compound adjective + noun
- "Sustaining mode" — compound adjective + noun (parallel structure)

However, Chapter 11 introduces them with temporal phase terminology:
- "Fall phase transition" (Ch11)
- "Sabbath boundary" (Ch11, Ch13)

The "Fall" phase name appears only in Ch11 §6 and is not reused elsewhere in the book. The "Sabbath boundary" label becomes the canonical phase-transition term in Ch13 and Ch14.

**Verification:**
- "Sustaining mode" vs. "creation mode" are the operational-state names: consistent
- "Sabbath boundary" is the phase-transition marker: consistent throughout Ch11, 13, 14, 15
- "Fall phase" is a single usage that does not recur or conflict
- No reader would be confused by the distinction

**Assessment:** The naming is consistent. The Fall terminology is a single reference in Ch11 that is not contradicted elsewhere. The Sabbath boundary becomes the dominant phase-transition terminology and is used consistently. This is not an inconsistency but a terminological hierarchy (operational modes + phase-transition boundary).

**Recommendation:** Retain as-is. The naming hierarchy is appropriate.
