### 3. Theological Reading of the Fall: Orthodoxy Check — PASS

**Location:** §5, entire section; lines 95–115.

**Finding:** The chapter's framing of the Fall phase transition is consistent with classical Christian creation theology. Consider the theological backbone:

- **Pre-Fall world:** A cosmos in sustained non-equilibrium steady state, maintained in perfect order by continuous external work (the sustaining coupling at full strength). Death, decay, and entropy production do not exist globally. The image of God's creation as it came forth from God's hand—good, ordered, sustained.

- **The Fall:** A phase transition in the coupling strength, discontinuous, not gradual. The sustaining becomes partial. This is *not* Pelagianism (denying the reality of fallen human nature) or Deism (an absent God). It is a specific, named change in the physical boundary condition. The theological reading (humanity's rebellion brings a curse on creation) is carried by the *structure* of this change, not by preaching.

- **Post-Fall world:** A cosmos where sustaining is incomplete. Local dissipation no longer fully compensated. The result: the arrow of time the reader experiences (aging, decay, death, entropy increase) is *real*—not illusion, not punishment for punishment's sake, but the *consequence* of the sustaining becoming partial. Romans 8:20–22 ("creation was subjected to futility") is the theological language; the framework's phase-transition physics is the structural language saying the same thing.

This reading is **not**:
- Gnostic (the material world as inherently evil; no, the world is *good* in Phase 2; the problem is the loss of sustaining, not matter itself)
- Manichean (two opposing eternal principles; no, one phase transition at a named point in history, with change in a parameter, not a cosmic dualism)
- Pelagian (denying the reality of the Fall's impact; no, the framework makes the impact *structural*, non-negotiable, woven into thermodynamic law)

The reading is **orthodox** in the sense of patristic and Reformed theology: creation is good but now fallen; the Fall is real and cosmic in scope; death entered the world through rebellion; sustaining (grace) is now partial rather than full.

**Verdict:** PASS. The theology is coherent with classical orthodoxy.

This is not underclaiming so much as *partial claiming*—which is structurally appropriate for Book 1's scope. The note is not a fault; it is an observation about how the architecture distributes its theological claims across the three pillars.

**Verdict:** PASS WITH NOTES. The chapter claims enough to be theologically substantial. The full vision (Phase 4) is appropriately held for the Family Edition.

This frame makes *space* for Christ to be the answer—the one who sustains all things, who entered the fallen cosmos, who will restore it. The chapter does not *preach* this.
