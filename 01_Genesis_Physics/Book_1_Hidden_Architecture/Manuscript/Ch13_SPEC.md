## Mission

Ch 12 cashed the fourth and most dangerous payoff check: the 95% of the cosmos most physicists call *dark* is identified as the two reservoirs Ch 3 drew, and the *c* of relativity is the nominal value of a local quantity that the framework predicts departs at specific zone boundaries. Ch 13 takes the single sharpest consequence of those identifications and points it at a specific, long-standing public question — the distant-starlight question — that serious lay readers bring to any cosmology book and that no mainstream framework resolves without postulating inflation or accepting a thirteen-point-eight-billion-year timeline that appears, on a plain reading of the first page of Genesis, to be in tension with the text. The chapter's job is not to vindicate young-earth creationism. It is not to vindicate mainstream cosmology. It is to show the reader that the question itself — *which number is right, six thousand years or thirteen point eight billion?* — is malformed. The framework's answer: the observable universe's age depends on the reference frame the observer is standing in and on the phase history of the firmament. In the framework, the firmament's Hebrew name (*raqia*, literally "the stretched-out thing," from the root meaning to beat out or spread) is not a poetic decoration; it is a mechanical specification. Expansion is intrinsic to what the firmament *is*. The architecture makes expansion not optional but constitutive. Combine that with Ch 11's Fall-phase transition — the regime change that activated the conservation laws and put the cosmos into sustaining mode — and two phases of expansion emerge: a rapid-expansion phase in which the firmament stretched under creation-mode physics, followed by a sustaining-mode phase in which a residual slow stretching (what mainstream physics measures as Hubble expansion, H₀) persists. Light that appears, on a straight-line-at-nominal-*c* calculation, to have been traveling for billions of years has in fact traveled through a medium that was stretching underneath it; the comoving distance it covered vastly exceeds the proper-time interval during the rapid-expansion phase. That mechanism — rapid firmament expansion during a creation-mode phase, followed by a Sabbath-phase transition into sustaining-mode slow expansion — is what dissolves the dichotomy. The thirteen-point-eight-billion-year timeline is the answer a standing-in-sustaining-mode observer gets when they extrapolate today's H₀ backward through the Sabbath boundary — which, per Ch 11, is exactly the extrapolation the architecture forbids. Radiometric dating carries the same structure: decay constants are constants of sustaining-mode physics, and extrapolating them backward through the Fall phase transition produces apparent ages that reflect functional maturity at creation plus post-Sabbath decay, not continuous temporal duration. The chapter must be scrupulously careful on two fronts. First, it must *not* come off as triumphalist young-earth creationism. The framework does not name a specific age number at either end; it reframes the question. The framework is compatible with a range of timelines depending on how the reference frame is set, and the chapter commits to the architectural answer (age is observer-dependent through the Sabbath boundary) rather than to a specific scalar. Second, the *functional maturity* framing of radiometric evidence is subtle and needs care — it must not slide into "it just looks old, don't trust the rocks," which is a posture a Greene reader will rightfully reject. The correct framing: rocks do carry real information. The isotope ratios are real. The decay rates today are real. What standard interpretation adds to the data is an assumption — that the initial conditions (N_D/N_P ratios) of the created matter were zero-daughter, and that decay operated identically through the Sabbath boundary — and the framework challenges only that assumption, not the measurements. The chapter walks the reader through this with the respect a physicist-reader deserves. Math density: zero equations; named constants (*c*, H₀, the 13.8-billion-year standard-cosmology age number, the 2.72548 K CMB temperature) can appear in words. One controlling analogy: a signal sent over a link that is itself being stretched during transmission — a variant of Ch 12's stratified-media analogy pushed into the time axis. Word count target: **5,500–6,500 words** per chapter prompt. Operator voice. Builder's honesty. Cleared-community discretion. Three numbered skeptic objections anticipated and answered. Clean handoff to Ch 14 (*What This Changes*).
