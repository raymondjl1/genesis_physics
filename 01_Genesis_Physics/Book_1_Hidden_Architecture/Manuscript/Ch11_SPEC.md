## Mission

Ch 9 cashed the first payoff check: particles are stable standing-wave patterns on the firmament. Ch 10 cashed the second: gravity and electromagnetism are two behaviors of the same membrane. Ch 11 is the third payoff — the rules every physics student is told to memorize (conservation of energy, conservation of momentum, conservation of charge; the four laws of thermodynamics; causality and the speed-of-light limit) are not axioms handed down from above. They are consequences of the architecture. Energy conservation falls out of the membrane action's symmetry under shifts in time. Momentum conservation falls out of its symmetry under shifts in space. Charge conservation falls out of gauge symmetry. This is the payoff of Noether's theorem applied inside zone architecture: symmetries of the 6D action produce conservation laws in 4D, and the symmetries are themselves geometric properties of the membrane — not postulates. Thermodynamics is the harder and more interesting part of the chapter. The first law (energy conservation) survives intact when the frame is widened to the open system Ch 5 established; the second law (entropy increase) is correct inside every domain the reader has ever measured but is *not* a timeless global statement about the cosmos. The framework reads the second law's familiar irreversible form as a consequence of a specific phase transition — what the framework calls the Fall phase transition, cite AXIOM_PHASE_TRANSITION_FALL.md — that changed the sustaining coupling from full to partial. Before the transition the cosmos was in a sustained non-equilibrium steady state with zero net entropy growth; after it, entropy production is irreversible at the global scale. This is a large claim, and the chapter handles it with cleared-community discretion — naming what is well-established (the second law holds everywhere we have ever measured), what is specific to the framework (the Fall phase transition as the mechanism behind the second law taking the form it does), and what is open (the global-scale measurements that would further constrain the transition's signatures). Causality — the rule that effects do not precede their causes — comes from the membrane's finite wave speed: nothing on the membrane can propagate faster than the membrane's own disturbances, and the membrane's disturbances propagate at c. The speed-of-light limit is not an arbitrary cosmic speed limit; it is the wave speed of the medium everything in the cosmos is a disturbance of. This chapter is where the framework's explanatory power at depth becomes felt — the reader should walk away understanding that the rules they memorized in high-school and college physics are not rules the universe was handed; they are rules the architecture forces the universe to have. Word count target: **5,000–6,000 words** per the chapter prompt. Math density: zero equations; the four laws of thermodynamics can be stated in plain English; the named theorems (Noether's theorem) are named, not written. One controlling analogy that carries both the conservation-law story and the thermodynamics story — a well-tuned engine, or a well-tuned RF oscillator, where the invariances of the hardware force the conserved quantities. Operator voice. No preening. Clean handoff to Ch 12.

## "Why" Chain

1. **Why this chapter after Ch 10?** — Because Ch 9 established what particles are and Ch 10 established two of the forces between them. The natural next question is what *rules* particles and forces must obey. The reader has been carrying, since high school, a memorized list — energy conservation, momentum conservation, the second law, the speed-of-light limit — and has been taught to treat them as axioms. The chapter's job is to show they are not axioms; they are consequences of the architecture.

2. **Why Noether's theorem?** — Because Noether's theorem is the century-old mathematical result that tells you how conservation laws come out of symmetries. The framework does not replace Noether; it embeds her result in a specific architecture. The symmetries of the 6D action — time-translation, space-translation, rotation, gauge — produce the conservation laws of 4D physics. The framework's contribution is not the theorem; it is that the symmetries themselves are geometric properties of the zone architecture rather than postulates. The architecture gives you the symmetries; Noether gives you the conservation laws.

3. **Why is energy conservation unchanged in the widened frame?** — Because the textbook statement ("energy is conserved in an isolated system") is exactly correct about the domain it specifies. Inside any lab-scale or stellar-scale subsystem, energy is conserved. The open-system frame Ch 5 established widens the accounting at the cosmic scale: energy flows from the waters-above reservoir through the sustaining coupling to the membrane, and the *cosmos plus reservoir plus coupling* is conservative. What the framework adds is not a revision of energy conservation but a correct statement of what the conserved system is. The first law of thermodynamics, read carefully, already has this structure — it refers to closed systems; the framework is making the system's boundary explicit.

4. **Why is the second law more complicated?** — Because the second law, as usually stated, carries two claims: (a) entropy increases in every isolated system, and (b) the cosmos is running down toward heat death. Claim (a) is correct and unchanged — every isolated system we have ever measured does obey it. Claim (b) is the framework's departure: the cosmos is not isolated, so its global entropy balance has terms claim (b) ignores, and the specific *irreversible form* the second law takes at cosmic scale is a consequence of a specific phase transition in the sustaining coupling — what AXIOM_PHASE_TRANSITION_FALL.md calls the Fall phase transition. Before that transition (Phase 2), the coupling was κ_full and entropy growth was zero globally. After it (Phase 3, the reader's phase), the coupling is κ_partial and entropy growth is irreversible globally. The framework reads the reader's experience of the second law as operating in Phase 3.

5. **Why is this not ad hoc?** — Because phase transitions that change the effective thermodynamic behavior of a system without changing the microscopic physics are routine in physics: electroweak symmetry breaking, QCD confinement, Bose-Einstein condensation, the superconducting transition. The Fall phase transition is a transition in the same mathematical category — a first-order transition in the sustaining coupling. The framework does not invent the category; it places the cosmos in it.

6. **Why is causality easier than the second law?** — Because causality, in the framework, is a direct consequence of the membrane's finite wave speed. The speed of light as an upper bound on propagation is the speed at which disturbances of the membrane propagate, and since everything in the cosmos (including its particles from Ch 9, its forces from Ch 10, and its conservation laws from this chapter) is a disturbance of the membrane in some way, nothing can propagate faster than the medium's wave speed. The relativistic causal cone falls out — Foundations Vol 5 Ch 1. No extra axiom is needed.

7. **Why does the chapter close into Ch 12?** — Because the framework's statement that *c* is the membrane's wave speed opens the door to the question Ch 12 answers: are there regions of the membrane where the local wave speed differs from the nominal value? The framework's answer is yes — specific zone-boundary cases where the wave speed is not the nominal c — and those are the predictions Ch 12 unpacks. The chapter closes pointing there.

### Analogies

One controlling analogy — a *well-tuned engine* or *RF oscillator*, the author's actual engineering experience. Used twice:

1. **The engine's invariances force its conserved quantities.** The engine does not care what absolute time its timing belt starts running; only the intervals matter (time-translation invariance → energy conservation). It does not care what absolute location the engine is bolted at; only the relative motions of parts matter (space-translation invariance → momentum conservation). It does not care what absolute orientation the block is in; only the rotations about its axis matter (rotation invariance → angular-momentum conservation).

2. **The engine's thermodynamics has a strict local domain and a widened global frame.** Inside the engine bay, the second law is exactly the second law the reader knows: every local process in the engine dissipates. Expanding the frame to include the fuel tank, the cooling system, the ambient air — as any mechanical engineer would — the first law's accounting is complete and exact. The cosmos-scale analogue is Ch 5's reservoir-and-dissipation picture.
