## Verdict: PASS WITH NOTES

The chapter executes a bold reframe: rather than defending a specific age for the cosmos, it shows the question itself — "which number is right, 6,000 years or 13.8 billion?" — is malformed. The question has a reference frame attached to it. Both numbers are correct in their respective frames; neither is "the" cosmic age without qualification. This is exactly the work Chapter 12 set up (zone boundaries where local wave speed differs; phase transitions where dynamics change), and Chapter 13 cashes it with clarity and architectural precision.

The depth calibration is exactly right for Book 1. The Hebrew word study on *raqia* (§1) is sophisticated enough to reward a reader who has grappled with Genesis scholarship, accessible enough for a reader encountering that grammar for the first time. The Sabbath-boundary phase transition (§2) is re-anchored cleanly from Chapter 11 without re-deriving. The fiber-optic-link analogy (§3) is carried far enough for the reader to internalize it (a signal traveling through a medium that stretches) and released at exactly the moment the reader lands conceptually. The radiometric-dating section (§4) is the chapter's subtlest move — it distinguishes data (real) from interpretation (assumption-dependent) and defends against the "deception" objection head-on.

Two navigational notes prevent a straight PASS: (1) the CMB section (§5) names the CMB as an anchor but treats it more lightly than the radiometric-dating case; the precision CMB predictions that live in Foundations are flagged as "live research," which leaves the reader uncertain whether the framework claims a specific CMB prediction or only a structural fit; (2) the skeptic objections (§6) are well-answered but compress three important rebuttals into a space where more signal clarity would help — the answer to "phase dependence, not frame privilege" is subtle and might need one more sentence to land firmly. These are editorial refinements. The core work is sound.
