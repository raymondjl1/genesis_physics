### What Changed (and Why It Works)

**Pivot point (line 10):** The original text said "What they lacked was a derivation. A first-principles account of why those two subsystems coupled..." The revised text says "The coupling constant on the whiteboard traced back, through a half-dozen layers of electromagnetic theory, to the fine-structure constant of physics — 1/137.036 — and the fine-structure constant, in three-dimensional physics, is not derivable from anything more fundamental."

This is the critical reframing. The author now leads with "fine-structure constant is not derivable from anything more fundamental" — naming the *global closure failure* explicitly, not just a local model gap. The question mark on the whiteboard becomes the "visible edge of a *global* closure failure: three-dimensional physics cannot derive the constant the framework was handing my engineer, and three-dimensional physics cannot derive half a dozen others like it."

**Layered support (lines 16–21):** The revised §1 now builds a three-part closure argument:
1. **Gravity:** "does not derive cleanly from the symmetries of three spatial dimensions... the coupling... the theory does not derive. It is put in by hand."
2. **Electromagnetism:** "carries constants the textbook hands you and does not earn... nobody in the three-dimensional picture has been able to tell you *why it is that particular number.*"
3. **Dark sector:** "has no natural home inside three spatial dimensions."

Each is named as a closure problem, not a measurement problem. The dark sector explicitly: "it is a cosmological-constant term added to the equations by hand... it is a ghost particle we have been unable to catch."

**Explicit statement (lines 22–23):** "The framework does not add dimensions to be interesting. It adds them because the math refuses to close without them."

This is the hook the spec demands: structural necessity, not aesthetic choice.
