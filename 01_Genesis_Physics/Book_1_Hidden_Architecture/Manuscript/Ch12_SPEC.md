The chapter has to earn credibility against every crank-alarm in the reader's head: *faster than light* is the phrase people have been selling on late-night television for forty years, and *dark matter as this mystery waters field* is indistinguishable, to a tired skeptic, from the theology that sells those same TV slots. ## "Why" Chain

1. **Why this chapter after Ch 11?** — Because Ch 11 established, as its closing move, that *c* is the wave speed of the firmament and nothing made of the firmament's disturbances can exceed it. The honest next question is: is *c* really the *same* everywhere on the firmament? Or are there regions — zone boundaries — where the local wave speed differs? If so, where does the framework predict those regions are, and what would a departure look like? That is Claim A of this chapter. Claim B is the identification of dark matter and dark energy with the two reservoirs, which has been building in the background since Ch 3 and is now the direct consequence of Ch 5's open-system frame and Ch 6's extra dimensions. Ch 12 is where the framework's specific predictions on the two most consequential dark-sector questions become explicit.

2. **Why is *c* a local quantity?** — Because *c* in the framework is the wave speed of the firmament, and wave speed is always a property of the medium. The physics classroom derivation of *c* from Maxwell's equations assumes the permittivity and permeability of free space — constants. The framework agrees those are the nominal values of the membrane's tension and mass density. But the membrane has a geometry (Ch 6), and the geometry has variations (the extra dimensions are not flat in general — the warp factors depend on position). Wherever the geometry varies, so can the local values of the nominal constants, and so can the local wave speed. Relativity's global-*c* postulate is the statement that, to the precision any measurement has been made, the local value equals the nominal. The framework agrees — within the regimes measured. It predicts specific departures outside those regimes.

3. **Why identify dark matter as Waters Below?** — Because the framework needs a reservoir field that is (a) gravitating, (b) non-Standard-Model-coupling, (c) clustering, (d) capable of being distributed inhomogeneously at galactic scales. The ξ extra dimension already plays the role of the reservoir in Ch 5's open-system picture (dark energy). The η extra dimension, by the same 6D architecture, plays the role of the second reservoir. The scalar field on η is non-coupling to the membrane interface's standing-wave patterns (Ch 9), so it has no Standard Model portal; but it gravitates by producing the usual metric curvature (Ch 10). Ω_DM ≈ 0.266, w ≈ 0, clustering structure — three checks. The Bullet Cluster explanation is immediate: a non-coupling field passes through collisions without scattering, while gas does not. The null direct-detection result across forty years of experiments is a *prediction* of the framework, not a post-hoc fit. The framework says: there is no portal, so there will be no detection. Four checks; the identification is sharp.

4. **Why identify dark energy as Waters Above?** — Because the reservoir field on ξ, sitting at its ground state, produces exactly the equation of state w = −1. Ground states are stationary — zero pressure gradient, constant density — and for a scalar field that is what w = −1 means. The cosmological constant problem — the factor of 10¹²⁰ mismatch between the quantum-field-theory vacuum energy estimate and the observed dark-energy density — is read by the framework as a category error: the QFT vacuum (fluctuations of Standard Model fields on the membrane) and the ξ-dimensional ground state (the reservoir's rest state) are two different things, and there is no principled reason the former should equal the latter. The coincidence problem — why Ω_Λ and Ω_DM are comparable today rather than differing by many orders of magnitude — is resolved by the zone geometry: the two reservoirs' densities are not independent free parameters but are related by the architecture of the 6D manifold, and the ratio is of order unity.

5. **Why do the FTL mechanisms not violate causality?** — Because in the framework's 6D geometry, the causal structure is set by the 6D null cone, not the 4D null cone. A 6D null geodesic that uses the ξ or η dimensions as a bypass is still a causal trajectory — still timelike-or-null with respect to the 6D metric — but its 4D projection can exceed the 4D null cone. This is not a paradox; it is the consequence of projecting a higher-dimensional causal structure onto a lower-dimensional one. The Sabbath-boundary condition prevents the pathological configurations (closed timelike curves, grandfather paradoxes) that a naive reading might worry about. The framework's five mechanism classes all preserve causality with respect to the local wave speed, which is the right thing for them to preserve. Ch 11 made this point in its forward flag; Ch 12 makes it explicitly.

6. **Why does the chapter close into Ch 13?** — Because the local-wave-speed claim makes a concrete prediction about one specific, long-standing astrophysical puzzle: the starlight problem. Galaxies are billions of light-years away and their light is here; young-earth frameworks have struggled to reconcile observed starlight arrival times with their cosmological timelines, and mainstream frameworks have the distant-starlight problem in its own form (horizon problem, inflation postulated to solve it). The framework's local-wave-speed variations at zone boundaries give a specific, testable account of how starlight from distant galaxies could reach a young cosmos without violating anything. Ch 13 unpacks this. Ch 12 plants the seed.
