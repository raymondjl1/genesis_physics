# Chapter 12 — Faster Than Light, Darker Than Dark

The range was quiet in the way a flight-test range goes quiet when everything is working. The ScanEagle was out beyond the ridge somewhere, two hours into endurance, a dot on the map and a stream of telemetry on the screen in front of me. The dielectric slowing was not a bug. Out of the antenna, the radio wave stepped into the lower atmosphere and accelerated — up close to vacuum c now, free-space propagation across the range. On a strange day, a temperature inversion could form a duct along the ridge line, and a signal that should have terminated at fifty kilometers would keep going. An RF engineer thinks about this the way a sailor thinks about wind — a known behavior of a known medium, measured and planned around.
