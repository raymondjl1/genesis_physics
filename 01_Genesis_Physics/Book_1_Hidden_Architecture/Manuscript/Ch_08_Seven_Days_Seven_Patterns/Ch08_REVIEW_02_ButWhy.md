## "But Why?" Audit by Section

- **§1 (CDR scene).** Why this opening? To anchor the reader's intuition of *structural corroboration* before making the mapping claim. PASS.
- **§2 (three tests).** Why these three tests? Answered in-section: (1) 5,040 arbitrary orderings mean a count-only match is worthless; (2) word-sharing alone does not establish correspondence; (3) generic operations would let any seven-list fit. PASS.
- **§3 (Genesis 1 as specification).** Why is each stage named as it is? Answered: "the framework reads the seven stages as a systems engineer reads a requirements specification." Reading discipline stated; summaries follow. PASS.
- **§4 (what is not claimed).** Why these three disclaimers (24-hour / geological epoch / YEC-OEC)? Answered: these are the most common misreadings, and the chapter's actual argument is about *sequence*, not duration. PASS.
- **§5 (mapping).** Why each pair? Each paragraph answers: stage operation and operator are the same kind of thing. Alternatives considered and rejected (see Stage 6 → TRANSFORMATION, where POINT and REPETITION are explicitly ruled out). PASS.
- **§6 (ordering).** Why these six constraints? Answered with intuitive justification on the page ("nothing can happen without a time axis"; "matter has to exist before it can be localized"); derivation explicitly delegated to Foundations Vol 1 Ch 9. PASS.
- **§7 (what it does and does not prove).** Why four confidence levels? Strong/strong/weaker/open. Epistemic posture consistent with prior chapters. PASS.
- **§8 (closing).** Why recap three things (architecture, grammar, corroboration)? To set up Ch 9's payoff. PASS.

## Strongest "Why" Moments

1. **§2 Test 1.** "7! = 5,040; count is cheap; specific ordering constrains the problem." Directly addresses numerology and explains the rigor.
2. **§5 Stage 6 to TRANSFORMATION.** Three-operator comparison: "not POINT (nothing new is being localized), not REPETITION (humans aren't described as multiplying), but TRANSFORMATION." Model of "why not the alternatives?" reasoning.
3. **§6 Constraints paragraph.** Six constraints, each intuitively defended, each traced to the architecture of Ch 7 / Foundations Vol 1 Ch 9.
4. **§7 Confidence ladder.** Naming four confidence levels is the clearest form of epistemic honesty in the book so far.
