## Resolution (2026-06-13)
The control the reviewer demanded has been run. The same MBSE/operator/build-order reading
used on Genesis 1 (Ch 8 §5–§6) was applied tablet-by-tablet to the Babylonian **Enuma Elish**,
the standard rival creation text, and briefly to **Atrahasis** as a second control.

**Finding:** *Enuma Elish* FAILS the build-order constraint set. Of the five constraints Genesis 1
satisfies in order (substrate-first / matter-before-sources / sources-before-life /
life-before-higher-life / everything-before-completion), Enuma Elish satisfies at most one, and
that one only vacuously. It fails substrate-first decisively (sky/earth are Tiamat's bisected
corpse, produced in Tablet IV after war and a divine genealogy, not a substrate laid first), has
no matter-condensation step, no repetition-of-life step, and closes with the fifty names of Marduk
(doxology of the maker) rather than a recursive closure of the made order. The structural reason:
Enuma Elish is a theomachy — its sequence is driven by who-defeats-whom (plot), not by
what-depends-on-what (construction dependency), so it has no reason to line up with a substrate-first
build order, and it does not. Atrahasis fails harder (no cosmogony at all; humankind from a slain
god's blood to relieve divine labor). **Genesis 1's distinctiveness survived the adversarial control.**

This was the honest test: had Enuma Elish satisfied the constraints, Ch 8 would have had to retreat.
It did not. Result is intellectually honest and favorable. Confidence ladder in §7 unchanged (the match
remains *corroboration*, not proof); the previously-open "does the correspondence generalize?" item is
now reported as tested-and-survived for the two standard rivals.

**Residual (minor, not blocking):** the control covers the two standard Mesopotamian rivals, not
*every* ancient creation sequence (e.g., Egyptian Memphite/Heliopolitan, Hindu, etc.). A wider
comparative survey is noted in §7 as a remaining open question but is out of scope for a trade book —
it belongs to a future comparative paper, not Ch 8.
