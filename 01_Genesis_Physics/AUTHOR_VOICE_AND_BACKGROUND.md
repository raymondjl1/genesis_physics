## 1. Who the Author Actually Is

Jeff L. Raymond is not an academic. Not a theologian. Not a pop-science journalist. He is an operator — a systems engineer and senior leader who has spent 25+ years building, flying, certifying, and commanding real hardware in the most consequential domains in the United States: national-security space, unmanned aviation, wartime operations, and frontier agriculture.

He held TS/SCI SI/TK clearance with counterintelligence polygraph — a top-tier trust credential held by a very small number of Americans.

He now leads operations at a defense AI-sensing company that builds for the DoD, NASA, and SpaceX.

He also spent roughly a decade, quietly and in parallel, working out that the first chapter of Genesis is a physics document. He did this the way he does everything else: as an engineer reading a requirements specification.

This combination — cleared-community operator, aerospace engineer, startup COO/CTO/CEO, long-running YouTube creator, husband, community volunteer, believer who credits Christ with the insight — does not exist anywhere else on the popular-science or Christian-science shelf. It is the franchise's single biggest commercial asset. Every book, every cover, every chapter opening must leverage it.

## 3. The Voice

### The one-sentence pitch

A former Air Force intelligence officer who helped fly America's spy satellites, deployed to Iraq, ran a drone program now part of Boeing, and spent a decade quietly working out that the first chapter of Genesis is a physics document.

That sentence is the hook. It should appear (in variants) on the back cover of every book in the franchise, in every podcast intro, in every author bio.

### Voice pillars

**1. Operator, not professor.** The voice of someone who has delivered hardware under cost/schedule/performance constraints. Every chapter should feel like it was written by a person who has *watched* theory meet reality and knows which one wins. Engineering stories go in: Baghdad convoy routes, SCIF moments, flight-line scenes at Hood River, trout-tank moments at the homestead. These scenes land in ways a physics professor cannot replicate.

**2. Frontline-leader authority.** The author led joint teams in wartime. Ran IRTs on billion-dollar satellites. Stood up intelligence functions. Does not need to argue for credibility — lets the stories carry it. "When I was the Exec O for the Deputy Commanding General in Baghdad..." is a sentence Brian Greene cannot write.

**3. Cleared-community discretion.** TS/SCI SI/TK with CI poly teaches a habit of thought: you tell people exactly what you know, nothing more, and when you don't know you say so. On the page this becomes a refusal to overclaim. This is the single most credible posture possible in the faith-and-science genre, which is saturated with overclaimers on both sides.

**4. MBSE as a discipline, not a slogan.** The author pioneered Model-Based Systems Engineering at Insitu. MBSE is *architected around tracing every requirement to its origin.* "Always answer why" is not a tagline — it is literally how his brain works. Every claim in every book traces to a derivation. Every derivation traces to an axiom or a scripture. The reader can walk the trace.

**5. Builder's honesty.** The author has built things that failed. Prototypes that didn't work. Calculations that were wrong by 76 orders of magnitude. He will say so on the page. This earns more trust than a thousand footnotes.

**6. Quiet faith, not loud faith.** The author is a believer. He credits Christ with the insight. But he learned — from TheRealMartian, from leadership in mixed-religious military environments, from running a NASA-spinoff company — that faith on the page lands best when it is *structural* rather than *decorative*. It shapes the questions he asks, not the words he uses. Scripture appears when it illuminates. It does not appear to argue.

### Voice rules (concrete)

- **Open chapters with scenes, not ideas.** Put the reader in a SCIF, on a flight line, in a Baghdad convoy, at the kitchen table at 2am with a legal pad. *Then* unfold the physics.
- **First person, sparingly.** "I was sitting with my wife at the kitchen table when..." The reader is walking with Jeff, not an anonymous narrator.
- **Confidence levels on every claim.** Strong, weak, open. Borrowed from the satellite IRT posture. "I am confident that..." / "I suspect, but have not proven, that..." / "This is an open question."
- **Short sentences. Engineering rhythm.** Jeff's LinkedIn prose is already clipped, factual, verb-driven. That is the natural register. Editors should not polish this out.
- **The household is present but earned.** Wife, homestead, livestock (two dogs, three barn cats, chickens, two horses, one mule, three black angus cows), community — they appear because they are where his actual thinking happens, not because they are signaling. "I was out by the trout tank when I realized..." and "I was feeding the cows at first light when..." are sentences that cannot be imitated. The author and his wife do not have children; do not invent any. If a scene needs ambient household life, reach for the animals, the barn, or the shop — not kids.
- **No scripture as argument. Scripture as the text being read the way an engineer reads a requirements document.** The move is not "the Bible says X, therefore X." The move is "the text describes a system with these properties — let's see what that system would look like if we built it."
- **No jargon unearned.** Technical terms are allowed, but only after the reader has been walked to them. An aerospace engineer who says "delta-V" without explaining it loses the airport reader in one sentence.
- **The wife is a character.** She is not a dedication. She asked the question that broke this open. She is in the kitchen. She is on the homestead. She is part of the story.
- **The operator's posture on uncertainty.** "I don't know" is a complete sentence. It is more trustworthy than a hedge. Use it.

### The voice test

Before any chapter ships, read the opening aloud and ask:

> *Does this sound like the guy who held a TS/SCI SI/TK with counterintelligence polygraph, ran a wartime command staff, pioneered agile on Boeing drones, co-founded a NASA-spinoff ag-tech company, and films YouTube from his workshop — or does this sound like a physics professor?*

If it sounds like the professor, rewrite it.

## 5. What the Author Is Not

This section exists to keep the books honest and the voice disciplined.

- **Not a physicist by training.** Aerospace engineer by degree, operator by practice. The books do not pretend otherwise. The framework's rigor is earned by showing the work, not by claiming credentials the author does not have.
- **Not a theologian by training.** A believer who reads scripture carefully, and who collaborates with theological reviewers. The books do not argue theology — they describe what the text appears to describe, and acknowledge where exegetical questions remain open.
- **Not an academic institution.** The Foundations Series is the author's own multi-year work, peer-reviewed by the franchise's own review agents. Claims that require external peer review are flagged as such.
- **Not a prophet.** The author believes Christ gave him the insight. He does *not* write as if he is delivering revelation. He writes as if he is delivering an engineering report on an insight he is still testing. That posture is non-negotiable on the page.
